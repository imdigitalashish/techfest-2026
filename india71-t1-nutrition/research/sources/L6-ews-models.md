## Key Findings

### 1. Individual-level prediction of incident wasting/SAM/growth faltering
- The cleanest true early-warning exemplar found is **Ahmed et al., eLife 2023** (`e78491`): a clinical prediction rule (CPR) derived in GEMS (n=7,639 children, 22.8% severe growth faltering) and **externally validated** in the independent MAL-ED cohort (5,683 diarrhea episodes, 1,322 children, 16.9% severe growth faltering) [1]. Outcome is prospective (HAZ decline ≥0.5 at 60-day follow-up from a diarrhea-episode index date), which is a genuine incident/future-risk target, not a same-visit classification. Top predictors: age at enrollment and HAZ at enrollment, plus respiratory rate, temperature, household size [1]. I directly opened the PMC mirror and confirmed the trial design, outcome definition, and discussion text verbatim (reviewer/author exchange on regression-to-the-mean and equity-of-targeting risk) [1]; the AUC figure itself (reported elsewhere as 0.72, 95% CI 0.70–0.73) was **not visible in the pages I fetched** (they returned the open peer-review correspondence, not the results table) — flagged **UNVERIFIED** pending a direct read of the results section.
- At the **population** (not individual) level, **Constenla-Villoslada et al., PNAS 2025** (`e2416161122`) forecast sub-county Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence in Kenya 1/3/6 months ahead from monthly MUAC surveillance plus rainfall/vegetation/conflict/price covariates, and is now operationally embedded in Kenya's NDMA drought early-warning dashboard as of April 2026 [2][3]. This is the single clearest "deployed, longitudinal, ML-based nutrition early warning" system I found anywhere — but it predicts **area-level prevalence**, not which child will falter, so it cannot directly supply a per-child ranked worklist for Anganwadi workers.
- **Cross-sectional NFHS/DHS stunting/wasting classifiers are NOT early warning** and should not be conflated with the above. Two recent examples: (a) a 2025 meta-analysis of ML models on DHS data reports pooled classifier accuracy of 84.39% (wasting), 68.92% (stunting), 73.60% (underweight) [4] — these are same-survey-wave classifiers with no prospective horizon; (b) Arya et al., *Nutrition* 2025, trains a Random Forest on NFHS-5 (India, cross-sectional, ~230k+ children) to classify **current** household-level stunting from SHAP-ranked socioeconomic features, again with no future time horizon [5]. Neither can answer "which child will become wasted in the next 90 days" — they answer "is this child stunted today," a fundamentally different (retrospective/concurrent) task. **Both [4] and [5] are UNVERIFIED** (I could not open mdpi.com or the Nutrition DOI directly — 403s); numbers are as reported by secondary search synthesis.
- A possibly-relevant but **unconfirmed** candidate for true individual longitudinal SAM early warning is a screening-tool paper indexed as PMC9218329 ("a novel screening tool to predict severe acute malnutrition through growth monitoring") — found only in initial search listing, **never opened**; flagged as an open follow-up rather than a verified finding.

### 2. Population-level nutrition early warning in production (one line each)
- **FEWS NET** — USAID-funded famine early warning system producing consensus-based Acute Food Insecurity (IPC-aligned) projections used to trigger humanitarian response ahead of crises (fews.net) — **UNVERIFIED**, page blocked (403) on direct fetch; description from search synthesis only.
- **IPC Acute Malnutrition Classification** — multi-partner global standard classifying GAM severity (via WHZ/MUAC) into phases used for famine (Phase 5) declaration thresholds (≥30% GAM, ≥20% extreme food gap, >2 deaths/10,000/day) (ipcinfo.org) — **UNVERIFIED**, ipcinfo.org returned 403 on direct fetch.
- **Kenya NDMA** — national drought early-warning bulletins built on monthly county-level MUAC/GAM surveillance, now augmented (as of April 2026) by the UC Berkeley/Cornell/Jameel Observatory ML forecasting dashboard described above — **VERIFIED**, directly opened and quoted [2].

### 3. Capacity-constrained targeting for community/frontline health workers
- **ARMMAN + Google Research (India)** run the longest-running, best-documented restless-bandit (RMAB) deployment in public health. The core idea: treat each beneficiary's weekly listening/engagement state as a Markov chain and use a learned Whittle index to decide which of a *fixed, small number* of beneficiaries a limited pool of health workers should call each week — structurally identical to "which children get a scarce home visit." Chronology directly confirmed from the Teamcore/Harvard publication list [6]: Biswas et al. (IJCAI 2021, learning index policies) → Nisthala et al. (AAMAS workshop 2021) → **Mate, Madaan, Taneja, Madhiwalla, Verma, Singh, Hegde, Varakantham, Tambe, "Field Study in Deploying Restless Multi-Armed Bandits," AAAI 2022** (first live field RCT) → **Verma et al., "SAHELI," AAAI 2023** (production scale-up) → **"Decisions and Deployment: The Five-Year SAHELI Project (2020–2025)," arXiv 2604.07384 (2026)** (longitudinal retrospective of the whole program) [7]. The **Google Research abstract page for the deployed SAHELI system**, opened directly, states verbatim: *"It is the first deployed application for RMABs in public health, and is already in continuous use by our partner NGO, ARMMAN. We have already reached ∼100K beneficiaries with SAHELI, and are on track to serve 1 million beneficiaries by the end of 2023"* [8]. Widely reported (but **UNVERIFIED** by me directly — Google Drive–hosted PDFs, not independently opened) figures from the AAAI 2022/2023 line: a 7-week, 23,003-participant field RCT reduced engagement drop-off by ~30% vs. control, and a later Decision-Focused-Learning policy produced a 31% reduction in cumulative engagement drop, each translating into higher measured iron/calcium supplementation adherence. Separately, Google DeepMind worked with ARMMAN on message/call-time scheduling for the Kilkari platform, reported (search-only, **UNVERIFIED**, page timed out on fetch) to lift pickup rates by up to 12%.
- **Wadhwani AI (India)** ships **Shishu Maapan**, a smartphone-video anthropometry tool — I opened the product page directly: *"By analyzing a short video of a newborn, it provides accurate anthropometric measurements (weight, length, head/chest circumference) in seconds, helping health workers identify at-risk infants immediately"* [9]. This is a **measurement-capture/risk-flagging** tool, not a longitudinal incident predictor — it replaces error-prone manual anthropometry, feeding whatever downstream classifier/threshold is applied. Odisha's 2025 state-wide pilot (first in India) reportedly uses it across Balasore, Cuttack, Ganjam, Jajpur, Keonjhar and Mayurbhanj for AI-assisted growth monitoring and resource targeting — **UNVERIFIED** (news-aggregator sources only; careers360/orissapost/TOI pages not independently opened).
- **Dimagi/CommCare** markets a "Precision Tasking"/case-prioritization framework for CHW caseload management explicitly framed around reducing alert/task overload — I could **not** reach either the CommCare blog page or the DIV Fund evaluation page (DNS/transport failures on repeated attempts), so this remains **UNVERIFIED** qualitative description only, sourced from search synthesis.
- **Medic (Medic Mobile) and Living Goods** predictive-targeting publications were **not located** within the time/search budget — a combined query was submitted but truncated by a rate limit before useful results returned, and the one successful retry covered only Dimagi. This is a genuine gap, not a negative finding; needs a dedicated follow-up search.
- **Microsoft Research India** nutrition/CHW AI pilots (2023–2026) were **not found** — no successful targeted search was completed for this sub-topic before the time budget closed. Flagged as an open gap.

### 4. Fairness/accountability and alert fatigue
- The **Allegheny Family Screening Tool (AFST)**, Pennsylvania (child-maltreatment risk scoring, not nutrition, but the closest well-studied public-sector child-risk-scoring analogue) shows a split verdict useful for our design:
  - **Allegheny County's own 2024 evaluation summary**, opened directly, states verbatim: *"The AFST reduced the racial disparity in investigation rates for the highest risk referrals by 83%, from 10.6% to 1.8%,"* and *"the AFST reduced the Black–White gap in removal rates of screened-in referrals by 73%, from 4.3% to 1.2%"* [10] — i.e., a well-governed deployment can **reduce** disparity versus unaided human judgment.
  - **HRDAG's 2023 audit (commissioned with the ACLU)**, opened directly, quotes the tool's own documentation effect: features such as prior use of county mental-health/disability services raise a family's risk score, and HRDAG's report concludes *"These predictors have the effect of casting permanent suspicion and offer no means of recourse for families marked by these indicators … They are forever seen as riskier to their children"* [11] — i.e., using service-contact variables as risk features can structurally penalize poverty/disability regardless of net disparity statistics, and the US DOJ opened scrutiny of the tool on disability-discrimination grounds (per [11], not independently confirmed beyond HRDAG's own page).
  - **Takeaway for nutrition targeting**: the AFST literature teaches that (a) aggregate disparity-reduction numbers and (b) feature-level structural-bias critique are *both* necessary and can coexist — a model can lower a headline disparity metric while still encoding durable penalties against specific households via proxy features (caste, religion, migrant status, service-contact history would be the nutrition-targeting analogues). No equivalent published fairness audit for a child-nutrition risk-scoring/targeting tool was found in this search — a genuine gap (see Innovation Room).
- **Alert fatigue** in CHW programmes is referenced qualitatively around Dimagi's CommCare "Precision Tasking" framing (reduce noisy/irrelevant alerts so workers don't become desensitized) but **no quantitative alert-fatigue study** (e.g., compliance decay vs. alert volume per worker) was opened or verified in this pass — treat as an open question.

## Innovation Room — what nobody in this search is doing yet

1. **Individual-child, continuous (not episode-triggered) incident-wasting risk with a fixed visit-capacity constraint baked into training, not applied post-hoc.** Every individual-level model found ([1]) is triggered by a clinical encounter (acute diarrhea) and optimizes discrimination (AUC) in the abstract, divorced from how many children an Anganwadi worker can actually visit. Every capacity-aware system found ([6][7][8]) optimizes *engagement*, not a *nutrition outcome*. Nobody publishes a model that (a) runs every month on routine Poshan Tracker growth data for *every* child (no trigger needed), and (b) is trained/evaluated directly against a home-visit budget.
   - **One scalar to measure it**: **Recall@Capacity-K** — with K fixed to the realistic monthly per-AWW visit budget (e.g., K=10–15 children), report the fraction of children who go on to incident wasting/SAM within 90 days who were in the top-K ranked list. This is the decision-relevant number; AUC is not, because AUC rewards discrimination across the *whole* risk range, most of which is irrelevant once the budget is fixed. (Standard AUC was the only metric in every individual-level paper found here — none reported a budget-constrained recall.)

2. **Reason codes that are both plain-language AND validated against the same external-validation discipline as [1].** SHAP explanations exist for cross-sectional classifiers ([5]), and a rigorous external-validation CPR exists for episodic growth faltering ([1]), but nobody combines externally-validated longitudinal risk *with* AWW-facing plain-language reason codes. This is squarely our product gap.
   - **One scalar**: **Reason-code agreement rate** — fraction of top-3 model-generated reason codes that an independent panel of AWWs/ANMs rate as "matches what I would have flagged" for a sample of true-positive cases, reported as a single percentage, tracked over model versions.

3. **A fairness audit for nutrition-targeting transposed from the Allegheny lessons ([10][11]) — nobody has done this for child-wasting risk models.** The AFST literature is the only public-sector child-risk-scoring body of evidence with both a disparity-reduction claim *and* a structural-bias critique, but it is entirely in the US child-maltreatment domain; no analogous audit exists (in anything surfaced by this search) for nutrition/CHW targeting in India or elsewhere, despite the same proxy-feature risk (caste, religion, migrant/seasonal-labor status, prior service-contact history could all play the "disability flag" role from [11]).
   - **One scalar**: **Max subgroup Recall@Capacity-K gap** — compute Recall@Capacity-K (see #1) separately for each socially salient subgroup (e.g., caste category, religion, migrant-household flag) at the *same shared capacity budget*, and report the single largest pairwise gap in percentage points. This directly mirrors Allegheny's own disparity metric ("investigation-rate gap") [10] but applied to a nutrition-visit worklist instead of a maltreatment-investigation queue.

4. **A published scalar for CHW alert fatigue tied to compliance decay.** Dimagi's own marketing acknowledges the alert-fatigue problem qualitatively, but no quantitative study surfaced in this search measures how AWW visit-compliance or data-quality degrades as a function of weekly flagged-case volume.
   - **One scalar**: **False-Alert Burden Ratio** = (number of children flagged this month who do *not* go on to incident wasting/SAM) ÷ (AWW's monthly visit capacity). A ratio > 1 means the false positives alone exceed capacity, structurally guaranteeing either missed true positives or worker overload — nobody reports this ratio for any system found in this search (ARMMAN/SAHELI reports engagement outcomes, not false-alert burden; Dimagi's framework is qualitative only).
