## (b) Design implications

1. **Research exemption ≠ service-delivery decisioning.** s.17(2)(b) only exempts processing "if the personal data is not to be used to take any decision specific to a Data Principal" (verified, row 2). Routing a task to an AWW/ASHA for a *named* at-risk child is exactly such a decision. **The risk-scoring-for-service-delivery pipeline must be legally grounded in s.7(b)/7(c)+s.9 machinery (and, once in force, the Fourth Schedule), never in s.17(2)(b).** Only the downstream research/model-training use of pseudonymised cohort data may rely on 17(2)(b) — and even that needs Second Schedule compliance (row 12) and is not yet in force (row 15).
2. **Accuracy-for-decisions duty → `credibility_engine`.** s.8(3) (row 6) is currently un-cited anywhere in `policy.yaml` but directly supports the `credibility_engine` component: any growth/risk record that is "used to make a decision that affects the Data Principal" triggers a completeness/accuracy/consistency duty. Recommend wiring `credibility_engine`'s data-quality gate explicitly to s.8(3), and blocking action-routing when a record fails that gate.
3. **No-tracking vs. growth monitoring — does service-delivery scoring fall under an exemption?** Answer: **not automatically, and not yet.** s.9(3)'s blanket "no tracking... of children" is in real tension with longitudinal growth monitoring. The only clean exemption path is Rule 12 + Fourth Schedule Part B item 1 ("function... in the interests of a child, under any law") (row 13) — grounded in ICDS's statutory mandate (recommend citing National Food Security Act 2013 explicitly, not yet independently verified this session) — but that Schedule **does not commence until 13 May 2027** (row 15), and even then it never excuses s.9(2)'s detrimental-effect duty (row 9), which has no exemption at all.
4. **s.7(b) needs a visible notice/contact UI, not just a policy-code string.** Second Schedule cl.(g) (row 12) requires giving the Data Principal (parent/guardian) an intimation naming a contact person and a channel to exercise rights whenever 7(b) is relied on. This is a UX/consent-screen requirement for Anganwadi enrolment, not just a backend `legal_basis` field.
5. **Split "s.7(b)" into two distinct legal bases.** Use **s.7(b)** (+ consent-at-enrolment or a Central-Government "notified database" designation for ICDS-MIS/Poshan Tracker) strictly for the AWW→beneficiary service-delivery leg; use **s.7(c)** ("performance... of any function under any law," anchored to NFSA 2013) for LS/CDPO/DPO/DM/State supervisory and monitoring data flows that are not themselves "issuing a subsidy/benefit/service" to that specific child at that moment (row 1).
6. **Stop citing "State instrumentality" as a general DPDP carve-out.** s.17(2)(a) (row 11) is a narrow, Central-Government-notified, sovereignty/security/public-order exemption — it does not apply to ICDS and should not be invoked anywhere in the architecture docs.
7. **Retention design needs two numbers, not one.** Default "erase on consent withdrawal / purpose served" (s.8(7), row 8) coexists with a **mandatory minimum 1-year retention of processing logs** (Rule 8(3)) and a **mandatory minimum 1-year retention of security logs** (Rule 6(1)(e), row 7) — `audit_ledger` needs both floors encoded, and must *not* borrow the Third Schedule's 3-year figure, which applies only to large e-commerce/gaming/social-media intermediaries.
8. **Commencement is the headline legal risk, not the substance.** Rows 15–16 show that essentially every Rule and Schedule the design leans on (Rules 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 16 and the Second/Third/Fourth/Seventh Schedules) **does not take effect until 13 May 2027**, and there is unconfirmed-but-credible evidence that the underlying Act sections 7–17 themselves may also only commence on that date. The Techfest proposal should (i) date-stamp every DPDP citation with its actual/likely commencement date, (ii) explicitly build the compliance architecture "exemption-ready for 13 May 2027," and (iii) separately identify what law *does* govern Poshan Setu's data processing in the interim (e.g., IT Act 2000 + SPDI Rules 2011, ICDS scheme guidelines, NFSA 2013, State government data-sharing orders, ABDM policy) rather than presenting DPDP Act/Rules citations as currently-operative law without caveat.
9. **Consent Manager ≠ ABDM HIE-CM.** (row 5) `policy.yaml`'s `health-record-with-consent` rule conflates DPDP's own Consent Manager (s.6(7)-(9)/Rule 4, itself not in force until 13 Nov 2026) with ABDM's distinct Health Information Exchange & Consent Manager regime. These need separate legal citations and likely a separate verification pass against National Health Authority / ABDM primary documents.
10. **Public dashboards may sit outside the Act entirely, which is a *stronger*, simpler claim than any exemption.** (row 4) If small-cell suppression + DP noise genuinely prevent re-identification, the published output is arguably not "personal data" at all (no exemption needed). Recommend the policy rationale say so explicitly, with the irreversibility/re-identification-risk threshold documented as an engineering control, not a legal citation.

### Note on method

Per the reliability constraints for this pass, `web_fetch` was kept to ≤5000-character pages with `start_index` paging for discovery and HTML sources. For the two long, bot-protected government Gazette PDFs that are central to this brief, I downloaded them directly to local disk with `curl` (a spoofed desktop User-Agent was sufficient to avoid the 403s `web_fetch` hit) and extracted text locally with `pdftotext`, then read only the targeted line ranges with `grep`/`view` — this avoided ever loading a whole PDF into the conversation while still letting me quote the Gazette text verbatim from a file I opened myself.
