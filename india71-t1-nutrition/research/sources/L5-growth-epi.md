## 1. Key Findings

### 1.1 Mertens et al. 2023, *Nature* — "Child wasting and concurrent stunting in low- and middle-income countries" (ki consortium) — **VERIFIED**, opened PMC full text directly [1]

- Pooled analysis of **21 longitudinal cohorts, 10 LMICs** (South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America), **11,448 children**, **198,154 anthropometry measurements**, monthly, birth–24 months, WHO 2006 growth standards.
- **Wasting prevalence is highest AT BIRTH** (11.9% overall), contradicting the older assumption that wasting peaks 6–24 months. **In South Asian cohorts specifically, at-birth wasting prevalence was 18.9%.**
- **Incidence peaks birth–3 months**: 12.9% of all children experienced wasting onset by 3 months — **47.8% of all children who ever wasted by 24 months**.
- **Cumulative incidence by 24 months: 29.2%** of children had ≥1 wasting episode (vs only 5.6% point-prevalence at 24mo); **10.0% had ≥2 episodes**. **South Asia cumulative incidence was highest of all regions: 52.2%.**
- **Recovery**: 39.0% of episodes resolved within 30 days, 64.7% within 60 days, **71.1% within 90 days** — South Asia recovery was *lower* than other regions. 91.5% of moderate / 82.5% of severe episodes eventually met the "recovered" definition.
- Episode definition: a 60-day (2 consecutive monthly visits) run of WLZ ≥ –2 is required to call a child "recovered."
- **Recurrence/median onset ages**: 1st episode median onset 264 days (~8.7mo); 2nd episode 449 days; 3rd episode 551 days — later episodes occur later, consistent with episodic/recurring rather than single-event wasting.
- Children wasted at birth/0–6mo recover to a *higher* post-recovery WLZ than those wasted later (P<0.001), but do not fully catch up to never-wasted peers, and have elevated risk of later concurrent wasting+stunting.
- **Concurrent wasting+stunting**: 10.6% of children experienced ≥1 concurrent wasted+stunted measurement by 24 months.
- Seasonality section and exact z-score swing by rainy-season month were **NOT extracted verbatim** — a fetch into that part of the Results timed out repeatedly (see §4). AI-search synthesis (not independently opened/quoted) suggested population WLZ varied ">0.5 z in some cohorts" in high-seasonal-rainfall settings — treat as **UNVERIFIED** until the Results/Fig. 4 text is directly read.
- Context citation (not a ki-original finding, cited from ref. 6 within the paper): wasting mortality RR 2.3, rising to RR 12.3 if also stunted+underweight — flagged **UNVERIFIED-AS-KI-ORIGINAL**, needs separate primary sourcing if used for calibration since it is a pass-through citation.

### 1.2 Benjamin-Chung et al. 2023, *Nature* — "Early-childhood linear growth faltering in low- and middle-income countries" (ki consortium) — **VERIFIED**, opened via Europe PMC fullTextXML [2]

- Pooled analysis of **32 longitudinal cohorts, 14 LMICs, 52,640 children, 412,458 measurements**, birth–24 months (17 of the 32 cohorts, 21,223 children, were in South Asia).
- **"The highest incidence of stunting onset occurred from birth to the age of 3 months, with substantially higher stunting at birth in South Asia."** (abstract)
- Stunted at birth: **13% overall** (range 0.3–42% across cohorts); new stunting onset 0–3 months: **18% overall** (range 7–57%).
- **South Asia specifically: 20% of children were stunted at birth, and another 21% became stunted by 3 months** — i.e. ~41% of South Asian children already stunted by 3 months of age.
- Children stunted birth–3mo accounted for 23% of all children who ever experienced stunting by 24 months; **overall cumulative stunting incidence by 24 months = 69% of children ever stunted.**
- **By 15 months: 34.0% of children were stunted (point prevalence), 50.5% had ever been stunted (cumulative), 16.5% had reversed and were no longer stunted.**
- **Stunting relapse after reversal: 2.0–3.5% per month from ages 6–15 months** (similar across regions).
- **86% of children who ever became stunted had LAZ < 0 at birth** — i.e. sub-optimal birth length is a dominant precursor.
- Reversal (recovery) was "rare" from 0–15 months, and children who reversed often relapsed; children born stunted (LAZ<–2 at birth) were especially prone to transient reversal + relapse. After 6 months, reversal rates were low regardless of birth status (<7%/month if born LAZ<–2; <5%/month if born LAZ –2 to 0).
- Post-reversal trajectory: among children who reversed stunting before 6 months, mean LAZ 9 months later was **–0.69 (95% CI –0.84, –0.55)** lower than at the time of reversal — i.e. "recovery" is not sustained/biological, LAZ keeps drifting down with age.
- **Growth velocity**: length velocity 3–24 months averages **0.75–1.25 cm/month**; within-child LAZ change per month is largest 0–3 months and <0.3 z/month after 3 months (directly references the WHO growth velocity standard percentile bands as the comparator).
- Abstract conclusion: "Early onset and low reversal rates suggest that improving children's linear growth will require life course interventions for women of childbearing age and a greater emphasis on interventions for children under 6 months of age."

### 1.3 Mertens et al. 2023, *Nature* — "Causes and consequences of child growth faltering in low-resource settings" (ki consortium) — **VERIFIED**, opened PMC full text directly [3]

- Pooled analysis of **33 longitudinal cohorts, 15 LMICs, 83,671 children, 662,763 measurements** (growth) / 97,988 children for mortality sub-analysis; 30 harmonised exposure variables tested via TMLE (targeted maximum likelihood estimation), reporting Population Intervention Effects (PIEs).
- **"Improving maternal anthropometry and child condition at birth accounted for population increases in length-for-age z-scores of up to 0.40 and weight-for-length z-scores of up to 0.15 by 24 months of age."** (abstract) — this is the single strongest aggregate predictor block (maternal height/BMI + birth size/gestational age together).
- **"Children with multiple growth deficits exhibited higher mortality rates from birth to 2 years of age than children without growth deficits (hazard ratios 1.9 to 8.7)."**
- **Boys consistently had higher risk of all forms of growth faltering than girls** (sex effect, direction confirmed, magnitude reported only in Fig. 2/3 images — not extracted as text numbers).
- Model fit: pooled cross-validated R² for the top-10 determinants + child sex was **0.25 for LAZ** and **0.07 for WLZ** — i.e. even the best-known predictors explain only a modest fraction of WLZ variance, which is directly relevant to the ceiling on any early-warning model's achievable AUC from static/maternal risk factors alone.
- **Season had a large population-level effect on WLZ**, with higher WLZ in drier periods (consistent with the wasting paper's seasonality finding).
- Exclusive/predominant breastfeeding <6mo associated with higher WLZ (not LAZ) at 6 months, but not a major predictor of 24-month z-scores.
- Mediation analysis: parental-anthropometry effects on child z-scores at 24mo are only **partially mediated by birth size/order/gestational age/delivery mode — adjusted z-score differences attenuated by a median of 30%** when controlling for birth characteristics, meaning most of the maternal-height/BMI effect operates through pathways other than birth size alone (not purely "maternal height → birth weight → outcome").
- Exact numeric PIE/ATE values specific to "maternal height +1 SD," "preterm vs term," "low birth weight vs normal" individually are reported only in Figs. 2–3 as plotted points (images), not as in-line text numbers — **NOT extracted verbatim as single numbers**; the 0.40 LAZ / 0.15 WLZ aggregate ceiling above is the only exact text-quoted magnitude available without parsing the figure images (flag in §4).

- **CHRD/UNICEF India 2021 "Analysis of wasting and severe wasting and its associated risk factors among under-5 children in India"** — **VERIFIED**, opened directly via the ENN (Emergency Nutrition Network) Field Exchange summary of the report [5]. This is the single best India-specific, multi-dataset, age-pattern source found in this session and directly answers the "India cohort with monthly growth data" ask:
  - Used three India datasets: **CNNS** (Comprehensive National Nutrition Survey, nationally representative, 0–59mo), **KI** (ki-consortium India-specific cohort subset, 0–23mo, pooled from 15 national studies), and **WINGS** (Women and Infants Integrated Growth Study — a Delhi-based intervention-arm birth cohort, 0–11mo).
  - **Wasting prevalence: CNNS 17.3%, KI 16.4%, WINGS 5.8%.** **Severe wasting: CNNS 4.9%, KI 3.7%, WINGS 0.5%.**
  - **"Across all datasets, the burden of wasting and severe wasting was highest in the first six months of life compared to older age groups."** — directly confirms the India <6-month wasting peak pattern across 3 independent India datasets (though not NFHS-5 itself).
  - **Incidence of wasting (new episodes per 365 person-days of follow-up): 0.32 (KI) and 0.55 (WINGS).**
  - Risk factors confirmed in this India-specific analysis: **lower maternal education, maternal underweight, Caesarean section, low birthweight, male child** — all associated with higher wasting risk; small-for-gestational-age and low-birth-weight children had higher wasting/severe-wasting prevalence than appropriate-for-gestational-age/normal-birthweight children.
  - **"Wasting in the first six months of life was found to be an independent risk factor for wasting at 12 and 24 months of age (WINGS and KI datasets)"** — direct evidence that early wasting predicts later wasting, highly relevant to an early-warning model's target definition.
