# Mumbai context — plastic leakage through nullahs, creeks and outfalls

Owner: research worker "T3 research: mumbai + policy". Access date for every web source: **2026-10-04**. Status: WORK IN PROGRESS (raw evidence log below; synthesis sections are filled at the end).

## Raw evidence log (append-only while researching)

### E1. Origin of the brief's "~5 million kg (5,000 t) per year" figure
- **Primary source = The Ocean Cleanup "Smart Rivers Survey" (press release, Mumbai, 10 Feb 2026).** QUOTE: "The survey found that the metropolitan region releases an estimated 5 million kilograms of plastic waste into the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean every year, causing direct and severe impacts across 220 km of coastline, 152 km² of mangroves, 107 protected species, and nearly 1.9 million livelihoods dependent on coastal and marine ecosystems." Method named: "drones, AI-enhanced remote-sensing cameras, GPS drifters, and global data registries". [S1]
- Same release: QUOTE "Trombay and Malad waterways identified as initial priority sites; deployment is planned for 2026" and "Once operational, these initial systems are expected to recover 61 to 92 tonnes of plastic per year." → i.e. the first two sites target ~1.2–1.8 % of the 5,000 t/yr estimate (DERIVED: 61/5000 = 1.2 %, 92/5000 = 1.8 %). [S1]
- Same release: QUOTE "Mumbai has been identified as one of 30 critical urban centres worldwide that together account for nearly one-third of global river-based plastic emissions into the ocean." and "The deployments will be undertaken in close collaboration with the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai and Maharashtra Pollution Control Board". [S1]
- Note: the estimate is for the "metropolitan region" (not only MCGM/Greater Mumbai) and is a modelled/surveyed estimate in a press release; no methods paper or per-outlet breakdown was found in the release. [S1]
- **Follow-up press release (Mumbai, 30 April 2026):** QUOTE "The initiative targets the elimination of 5 million kg of plastic entering the Arabian Sea annually"; "Memoradum [sic] of Understanding signed with the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board"; "Permits secured from the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai for action at Trombay Creek and Malad Creek sites"; "Implementation of the deployments will be done in collaboration with Plastic Fischer and United Way Mumbai". Permits came from "Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM), Storm Water Drain Department – the authority of waterways within the municipal jurisdiction of Greater Mumbai". QUOTE "On-ground operations and waste management will be led by social enterprise Plastic Fischer, which, with more than 100 employees, currently operates across seven Indian cities." [S2]
- → The brief's "drainage outlets like Malad or Trombay" = **Malad Creek and Trombay Creek**, The Ocean Cleanup's two pilot Interceptor sites (MCGM SWD permits, 2026). [S1][S2]

- Free Press Journal repeats the release verbatim-in-substance (5 million kg; 220 km coastline; "152 sq.km. of mangroves"; 61–92 t/yr from first systems; collaboration with "the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation and the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board"). Independent corroboration of the claim's *origin*, not of the number. [S3]
- Lokmat Times adds detail not in the release: QUOTE "According to a year-long study by The Ocean Cleanup, nearly five million kilograms of plastic enter the Arabian Sea annually through around 50 drains and nullahs across the city. … The study identified key pollution hotspots such as Thane Creek, Malad Creek, and Vasai Creek". Also: QUOTE "two such systems are expected to be installed in Trombay and Malad before the monsoon season. These interceptors are projected to collect between 61 and 92 tonnes of plastic initially, with plans to expand coverage to all major nullahs over the next three years." and QUOTE "Strong tidal movements cause waste to flow not only into the sea but also back into the city during high tide". And: QUOTE "the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) has implemented measures such as installing trash barriers in select locations, which have helped collect thousands of tonnes of waste." and QUOTE "A significant portion of the waste reaching the sea consists of low-value plastics that are often ignored in recycling processes". (Article date not shown in fetched text; secondary source.) [S4]

### E2. Other (model) estimates — disagreeing numbers
- **Meijer et al. 2021 (Science Advances) global river-emission model, as presented by Plastic Bank's river pages:** QUOTE "the global inventory built by Meijer et al. in 2021 ranks it [Malad Creek] #30 of 31,819 modeled outfall points, with a modeled estimate of 2,724 metric tons per year of plastic reaching the sea." Page also: "These rankings are *estimates* rather than measurements". Page "Last reviewed: July 20, 2026". (Secondary presentation of the Meijer dataset; the dataset itself was not opened.) [S5]
- → DISAGREEMENT: the Meijer-model value for **Malad Creek alone (2,724 t/yr)** is ~54 % of The Ocean Cleanup's **whole-metropolitan-region** survey estimate (5,000 t/yr) (DERIVED: 2724/5000 = 0.545). The two are different methods (global probabilistic model vs city "Smart Rivers Survey" with drones/cameras/drifters); treat 5,000 t/yr as the current best city-scale figure and quote the range honestly. [S1][S5]

- **Meijer et al. 2021 Table S5 (50 named top outfalls), as listed on Plastic Bank's India page:** QUOTE "Ulhas River: rank #3, 13,433 metric tons per year"; "Malad Creek: rank #30, 2,724 metric tons per year"; "Panvel Creek: rank #36, 2,520 metric tons per year"; "Mithi River: rank #48, 2,182 metric tons per year" (of "31,819 modeled points"). Page caveat: QUOTE "The value is a modeled estimate, not a direct measurement." [S6]
- DERIVED: the four Mumbai-Metropolitan-Region outfalls in Meijer's top 50 sum to 13,433 + 2,724 + 2,520 + 2,182 = **20,859 t/yr**, ≈ 4.2× The Ocean Cleanup's 2026 survey figure of 5,000 t/yr (Ulhas and Panvel lie outside MCGM limits, in the wider MMR). → Estimates for Mumbai span **~2,000–5,000 t/yr (single creek → TOC metro survey) up to ~21,000 t/yr (Meijer model, four MMR outfalls)**; present 5,000 t/yr as the brief's and TOC's figure and flag model uncertainty. [S1][S6]
