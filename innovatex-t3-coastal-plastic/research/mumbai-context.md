Owner: research worker "T3 research: mumbai + policy" (child of the T3 lead). Access date for every web source: **2026-10-04**. Sources [S1]–[S7] were opened by the author; [S8]–[S23] were opened by worker sub-agents of this session, whose exact quotes are copied here (not re-opened by the author because of network timeouts). ## TL;DR
1. **The brief's "≈5 million kg (5,000 t) per year" comes from The Ocean Cleanup's 2026 "Smart Rivers Survey"** (press release, Mumbai, 10 Feb 2026): "the metropolitan region releases an estimated 5 million kilograms of plastic waste into the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean every year". It is a survey/model estimate for the whole metropolitan region, not a BMC measurement. [S1]
2. **The brief's "Malad or Trombay" = Malad Creek and Trombay Creek**, The Ocean Cleanup's two pilot Interceptor sites; permits came from the MCGM Storm Water Drain Department, an MoU was signed with MPCB, and Plastic Fischer runs on-ground operations (30 Apr 2026). The first systems are expected to recover **61–92 t of plastic per year**, i.e. only ~1.2–1.8 % of 5,000 t/yr (DERIVED). [S1][S2]
3. A secondary report says the leakage comes "through around 50 drains and nullahs", with **Thane, Malad and Vasai creeks** as hotspots, and that tides push waste **back into the city at high tide**. [S4]
4. **Model estimates disagree:** Meijer et al. 2021 (as tabulated by Plastic Bank) gives Malad Creek alone **2,724 t/yr** (rank #30 of 31,819 outfalls), Mithi 2,182 t/yr, Panvel Creek 2,520 t/yr and Ulhas 13,433 t/yr. That is ≈20,859 t/yr for four MMR outfalls (DERIVED), about 4× the survey figure. [S5][S6]
5. **Drain network:** ~2,000 km of open drains, 440 km of closed drains, **186 outfalls**, >30,000 water entrances; most drains handle about **25 mm/h of rain at low tide**, and the city gets >1,400 mm in June–July (Wikipedia summary of BRIMSTOWAD; the primary report PDF could not be fetched). [S7]
6. **Monsoon pulse is huge and visible:** after >1,000 mm of rain in early July 2026, BMC removed **2,498.66 t** of marine waste from seven beaches in 11 days (9–19 Jul 2026), with **1,825 t** from Juhu alone. [S17][S18]
7. **Accountability gap:** the EOW SIT's FIR in the Mithi desilting scam cites a **₹65.54 crore** wrongful loss (scam "claimed to be worth ₹1,000 crore"), and the SIT is examining **₹1,100 crore** spent by BMC and MMRDA on the Mithi since 2005. In June 2026 Mithi stretches near Kurla/BKC were still "choked with vegetation, floating waste and accumulated silt" at the desilting deadline. [S8][S9]
8. **Composition: plastic dominates.** Plastic was 75.5 % of 52,770 litter items at Juhu and Aksa (CIFE). Jayasiri et al. 2013 measured 7.49 g and 68.83 plastic items per m² on four Mumbai beaches. Mangrove sediments average 6,730 microplastic particles per kg. Plastic bags were the biggest beach-trash component in a 2015–16 ReefWatch survey. [S10][S11][S12][S13]
9. **Waste system:** Mumbai generated **6,330 t/day** of MSW in 2022 (BMC ESR 2022-23). An older BMC SWM estimate said **500 t/day is not collected and "ends up in the storm water drains"**. [S10][S16]
10. **The plastic ban is weakly enforced:** in 2024 BMC collected ₹41.7 lakh in penalties from 44,448 establishments and confiscated only 2,148 kg of plastic (another outlet reports 3,148 kg). [S19][S21]

## 1. Where the "5,000 t/yr" number comes from, and how far to trust it
- Origin: The Ocean Cleanup (TOC), 30 Cities Program. The Smart Rivers Survey used "drones, AI-enhanced remote-sensing cameras, GPS drifters, and global data registries". Claimed impacts: "220 km of coastline, 152 km² of mangroves, 107 protected species, and nearly 1.9 million livelihoods". [S1]
- No methods paper, uncertainty range or per-outlet breakdown is public in the releases. A secondary article calls it a "year-long study" covering "around 50 drains and nullahs". [S1][S4]
- Model alternatives: the Meijer et al. 2021 global model (Science Advances; values via Plastic Bank's tabulation of Table S5) gives 2,182–13,433 t/yr *per outfall* for MMR outfalls. Cottom et al. 2024: **NOT FOUND** (not reached in this session). [S5][S6]
- How to use it: quote **5,000 t/yr (TOC 2026 survey, metro region)** as the headline, and show the range "≈2,000 t/yr for one creek (Meijer) up to ≈21,000 t/yr for four MMR outfalls (Meijer)" as model uncertainty. Our monitoring layer can turn this into a measured, per-nullah number.

## 2. Stormwater network, Mithi and the "Malad / Trombay" outlets
- Network (BRIMSTOWAD, via Wikipedia): "2,000km of open drains, 440km of closed drains, 186 outfalls and more than 30,000 water entrances"; "The capacity of most of the drains is around 25mm of rain per hour during low tide"; "Most of the storm water drains are also choked due to the dumping of garbage by citizens." The plan was conceived after the 1985 floods; a proposal was submitted in 1993. Cost: "Rs 12billion" by 2005. [S7]
- Malad Creek: a "tidal creek that drains Mumbai's western suburbs into the Arabian Sea … with mangroves lining parts of its course"; debris "move[s] back and forth with each tide". [S5]
- Trombay Creek and Malad Creek: TOC pilot sites under MCGM SWD permits, before the 2026 monsoon per one report. [S2][S4]
- Mithi: still choked with floating waste and plastics at the 10 June 2026 desilting deadline near Kurla/BKC. [S9]
- **BMC ₹13,000 crore integrated flood-control plan (Mumbai Live, 2026; author-verified):** QUOTE "Mumbai's existing stormwater drainage system is designed to drain approximately 50 mm of rainfall per hour. Under the new plan, the Municipal Corporation aims to increase this capacity to 100–120 mm per hour." / "flood gates and pumping systems will be installed at 33 additional locations" / "To prevent garbage from entering stormwater drains, protective mesh screens measuring 8 to 10 feet in height will be installed at selected locations." / "the sensor-based monitoring system is operational on a pilot basis at seven locations" (water-level sensors in major SWDs and the Mithi, Dahisar and Poisar rivers) / "pumping stations at Mahul and Mogra" and "18 flood gates under the third phase of the Mithi River project" / "Work on the project is scheduled to begin in October 2026, with completion targeted for October 2029." / "special flood mitigation measures at 350 flood-prone locations". Also: "On July 26, 2005, Mumbai recorded an unprecedented 944 mm of rainfall in a single day". → BMC is already buying **garbage screens + water-level sensors**, a direct integration point and a procurement channel for our system. [S24]
- **UNVERIFIED (could not open primary):** nullah lengths (major 200 km / minor 87 km), outfall split (136 to the sea, 26 to Mahim Creek, 10 to Mahul Creek, 14 to Thane Creek), the six commissioned pumping stations (Haji Ali, Irla, Love Grove, Cleveland, Britannia, Gajdharbandh; Mahul and Mogra are confirmed only as first-phase works in [S24]), 2026 high-tide days (a search lead says 24 days above 4.5 m, highest 4.89 m on 16 Jul), and Mithi length and catchment.

## 3. Trash booms, interception and desilting accountability
- TOC Interceptors (2026): 2 pilots (Trombay, Malad), 61–92 t/yr, "plans to expand coverage to all major nullahs over the next three years". [S1][S4]
- BMC "trash barriers in select locations … have helped collect thousands of tonnes of waste" (no dates, counts or costs given). [S4]
- Mithi desilting scam (FIR, May 2025): ₹65.54 crore loss; ₹1,100 crore spent since 2005 under examination. [S8]
- **NOT FOUND:** BMC/MRDPA trash-boom counts, dates, costs, tonnage and failures, and *how* desilting quantities were (not) verified (weighbridge slips, video, GPS). These need a follow-up from primary EOW/BMC documents.

## 4. Waste generation, litter composition, mangroves, clean-ups
- MSW: 6,330 t/day (2022, ESR 2022-23), 88 % to Kanjurmarg. Older estimate: ~10,000 t/day to dumping grounds and 500 t/day uncollected flowing via SWDs to the sea. [S10][S16]
- Segregation: "There is no penalty for the residents if they mix it", and "sub-contractors get paid by the volume of waste". [S16]
- Beaches: Jayasiri et al. 2013 found 7.49 g/m² and 68.83 items/m², with 41.85 % microplastics (1–5 mm). CIFE (reported 2023) found 75.5 % plastic among 52,770 items, Juhu ~1,698 items/50 m and Aksa ~407 items/50 m. Off Mumbai, the NE Arabian Sea holds 379 t of debris, 40.6 % plastic by weight (CIFE 2020). [S11][S12]
- Mangroves: surface debris 62 % plastic by number and 43 % by weight (CIFE, via HT; quote truncated in notes). Sediment: 6,730 microplastic particles/kg dry (Versova >7,800, Sewri >5,700; 30 samples, Oct–Dec 2021), more than half of them fibres. [S11][S13]
- Versova clean-up (from Oct 2015): "over 4,000 tons" (UNEP, 2016) and "5.3 million kilograms" in 85 weeks (secondary, 2017). This is a *different* "5 million kg" from the brief's figure; do not confuse them. [S14][S15]
- Monsoon: 2,498.66 t from seven beaches in 11 days (July 2026), described as "plastic, thermocol, wooden debris … from drains and natural waterways". [S17]
- **NOT FOUND:** a measured *nullah* composition (film/sachet/MLP share by count or weight) for Mumbai, and the slum population living along nullahs.

## Pain points → design implications
| Pain point (Mumbai-specific) | Evidence | What our solution must do |
|---|---|---|
| Tidal creeks push waste back and forth; waste "flow[s] … back into the city during high tide" | [S4][S5] | Tide-aware design: two-way capture or upstream siting above the tidal reach; schedule clearing by tide tables; log tide stage with every count |
| Monsoon pulse: thousands of tonnes in days (2,498.66 t in 11 days) | [S17] | Size for surge, not the average; sensor-triggered dispatch; a breakaway/overflow mode so barriers never worsen flooding |
| Drains carry only ~25 mm/h at low tide (older design; ~50 mm/h today per BMC), so flooding is a political red line | [S7][S24] | Prove zero added head loss (fail-open booms); place barriers at outfalls or wide sections, not at choke points |
| Low-value plastics dominate, so scrap revenue is weak | [S4][S10] | Revenue from municipal fees, EPR/OBP credits and MRV data rather than scrap (see policy-economics.md) |
| 500 t/day uncollected waste; dumping into nullahs; segregation not enforced | [S4][S10][S16] | Per-nullah counts and source attribution to target wards and markets upstream; community collection points |
| Desilting quantities are disputed (₹65.54 cr scam) | [S8][S9] | Tamper-evident MRV: weighed, geotagged, time-stamped, photo-verified catch logs that BMC and auditors can check |
| Mangroves trap plastic (152 km² affected; 62 % of debris items plastic) | [S1][S11] | Intercept upstream of mangrove fringes; low-impact anchoring; mangrove-cell permits (UNVERIFIED process) |
| Ban enforcement is thin (2,148 kg seized in 2024) | [S19] | Item- and brand-level composition data from intercepted catch to guide enforcement and EPR claims |
| BMC's ₹13,000 cr plan already buys 8–10 ft garbage mesh screens + water-level sensors (7 pilots) | [S24] | Plug into BMC procurement: sensor/camera add-on for the screens, fill-level alerts to the central control room, and clearing dispatch |
| TOC already covers Malad and Trombay with 2 large Interceptors (61–92 t/yr) | [S1][S2] | Complement, don't duplicate: low-cost modular units for the other ~48 nullahs plus an open MRV layer usable by TOC, Plastic Fischer and BMC |

### E1. Origin of the brief's "~5 million kg (5,000 t) per year" figure
- **Primary source = The Ocean Cleanup "Smart Rivers Survey" (press release, Mumbai, 10 Feb 2026).** QUOTE: "The survey found that the metropolitan region releases an estimated 5 million kilograms of plastic waste into the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean every year, causing direct and severe impacts across 220 km of coastline, 152 km² of mangroves, 107 protected species, and nearly 1.9 million livelihoods dependent on coastal and marine ecosystems." Method named: "drones, AI-enhanced remote-sensing cameras, GPS drifters, and global data registries". [S1]
- Same release: QUOTE "Trombay and Malad waterways identified as initial priority sites; deployment is planned for 2026" and "Once operational, these initial systems are expected to recover 61 to 92 tonnes of plastic per year." → i.e. the first two sites target ~1.2–1.8 % of the 5,000 t/yr estimate (DERIVED: 61/5000 = 1.2 %, 92/5000 = 1.8 %). [S1]
- Same release: QUOTE "Mumbai has been identified as one of 30 critical urban centres worldwide that together account for nearly one-third of global river-based plastic emissions into the ocean." and "The deployments will be undertaken in close collaboration with the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai and Maharashtra Pollution Control Board". [S1]
- Note: the estimate is for the "metropolitan region" (not only MCGM/Greater Mumbai) and is a modelled/surveyed estimate in a press release; no methods paper or per-outlet breakdown was found in the release. [S1]
- **Follow-up press release (Mumbai, 30 April 2026):** QUOTE "The initiative targets the elimination of 5 million kg of plastic entering the Arabian Sea annually"; "Memoradum [sic] of Understanding signed with the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board"; "Permits secured from the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai for action at Trombay Creek and Malad Creek sites"; "Implementation of the deployments will be done in collaboration with Plastic Fischer and United Way Mumbai". Permits came from "Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM), Storm Water Drain Department – the authority of waterways within the municipal jurisdiction of Greater Mumbai". QUOTE "On-ground operations and waste management will be led by social enterprise Plastic Fischer, which, with more than 100 employees, currently operates across seven Indian cities." [S2]
- → The brief's "drainage outlets like Malad or Trombay" = **Malad Creek and Trombay Creek**, The Ocean Cleanup's two pilot Interceptor sites (MCGM SWD permits, 2026). [S1][S2]

- Free Press Journal repeats the release verbatim-in-substance (5 million kg; 220 km coastline; "152 sq.km. of mangroves"; 61–92 t/yr from first systems; collaboration with "the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation and the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board"). Independent corroboration of the claim's *origin*, not of the number. [S3]
- Lokmat Times adds detail not in the release: QUOTE "According to a year-long study by The Ocean Cleanup, nearly five million kilograms of plastic enter the Arabian Sea annually through around 50 drains and nullahs across the city. … The study identified key pollution hotspots such as Thane Creek, Malad Creek, and Vasai Creek". Also: QUOTE "two such systems are expected to be installed in Trombay and Malad before the monsoon season. ### E2. Other (model) estimates — disagreeing numbers
- **Meijer et al. 2021 (Science Advances) global river-emission model, as presented by Plastic Bank's river pages:** QUOTE "the global inventory built by Meijer et al. in 2021 ranks it [Malad Creek] #30 of 31,819 modeled outfall points, with a modeled estimate of 2,724 metric tons per year of plastic reaching the sea." Page also: "These rankings are *estimates* rather than measurements". Page "Last reviewed: July 20, 2026". (Secondary presentation of the Meijer dataset; the dataset itself was not opened.) [S5]
- → DISAGREEMENT: the Meijer-model value for **Malad Creek alone (2,724 t/yr)** is ~54 % of The Ocean Cleanup's **whole-metropolitan-region** survey estimate (5,000 t/yr) (DERIVED: 2724/5000 = 0.545). The two are different methods (global probabilistic model vs city "Smart Rivers Survey" with drones/cameras/drifters); treat 5,000 t/yr as the current best city-scale figure and quote the range honestly. [S1][S5]

- **Meijer et al. 2021 Table S5 (50 named top outfalls), as listed on Plastic Bank's India page:** QUOTE "Ulhas River: rank #3, 13,433 metric tons per year"; "Malad Creek: rank #30, 2,724 metric tons per year"; "Panvel Creek: rank #36, 2,520 metric tons per year"; "Mithi River: rank #48, 2,182 metric tons per year" (of "31,819 modeled points"). Page caveat: QUOTE "The value is a modeled estimate, not a direct measurement." [S6]
- DERIVED: the four Mumbai-Metropolitan-Region outfalls in Meijer's top 50 sum to 13,433 + 2,724 + 2,520 + 2,182 = **20,859 t/yr**, ≈ 4.2× The Ocean Cleanup's 2026 survey figure of 5,000 t/yr (Ulhas and Panvel lie outside MCGM limits, in the wider MMR). → Estimates for Mumbai span **~2,000–5,000 t/yr (single creek → TOC metro survey) up to ~21,000 t/yr (Meijer model, four MMR outfalls)**; present 5,000 t/yr as the brief's and TOC's figure and flag model uncertainty. [S1][S6]
