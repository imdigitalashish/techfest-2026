## Notes per company (evidence log, filled as sources are opened)

### The Ocean Cleanup — Interceptor family + 30 Cities Program (non-profit, NL)
- **Mumbai is a 2026 target city.** Press release "The Ocean Cleanup Enters India; To Tackle Mumbai's 5 Million kg Plastic Flow", Mumbai, 10 Feb 2026: Mumbai is "among 30 cities worldwide to cut one-third of global river-based plastic emissions"; "Trombay and Malad waterways identified as initial priority sites; deployment is planned for 2026"; their 'Smart Rivers Survey' used "drones, AI-enhanced remote-sensing cameras, GPS drifters, and global data registries"; "the metropolitan region releases an estimated 5 million kilograms of plastic waste into the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean every year"; "these initial systems are expected to recover 61 to 92 tonnes of plastic per year" *(claim/forecast)*. → **This is the source of the "5 million kg" figure in our brief.** https://theoceancleanup.com/press/press-releases/the-ocean-cleanup-enters-india/
- **Permits + MoU.** Press release "The Ocean Cleanup moving towards deployment of Mumbai Interceptors", Mumbai, 30 Apr 2026: MoU signed with the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board (MPCB); "secured permits from Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM), Storm Water Drain Department"; pilots "in Trombay Creek and Malad Creek"; "On-ground operations and waste management will be led by social enterprise Plastic Fischer, which, with more than 100 employees, currently operates across seven Indian cities"; implementation also with United Way Mumbai. https://theoceancleanup.com/press/press-releases/the-ocean-cleanup-deploys-mumbai-interceptors/
- **Programme scale.** Update "Funding unlocked to help stop one-third of river pollution" (published 9 Mar 2026): The Audacious Project committed "$121 million of the $350 million needed"; 2026 planned Interceptor deployments listed include Barranquilla, Santo Domingo, **Mumbai**, Jakarta, Denpasar, Panama City; organisation states it has "prevent[ed] nearly 50 million kilograms of trash from reaching the ocean" and intercepts "an estimated 2-5 percent of global river-borne plastic emissions" *(claim)*. https://theoceancleanup.com/updates/funding-unlocked-for-the-30-cities-program/
- **Rivers page:** "just 1000 of the three million rivers ... are responsible for 80% of riverine pollution"; Kingston (Jamaica) "was the first to feature multiple Interceptors in one city"; Interceptor 006 (Las Vacas, Guatemala) "our highest performing to date". https://theoceancleanup.com/rivers/
- **Implication for us:** the two outfalls named in the brief (Malad, Trombay) are already being taken by the best-funded player in the field. Our pitch should not be "another barrier at Malad"; it should be the *measurement + dispatch + MRV layer* that (a) works at the hundreds of smaller nullah outfalls a 61–92 t/yr Interceptor programme does not reach, and (b) could plug into Interceptor/Plastic Fischer sites as an independent verifier.

## Practitioner critiques and social signal (evidence log)
- **The Revelator (Aug 2026), "Why the Ocean Cleanup hasn't solved the plastic pollution crisis"** — states experts "have long been skeptical of Slat's model"; after failed ocean prototypes "the team pivoted in 2019 toward a new model that was first suggested by critics and skeptics as an alternative in 2015. Now they mostly intercept plastic when it first leaves rivers and enters the ocean". https://therevelator.org/why-ocean-cleanup-has-not-solved-plastic-pollution/
- **Hacker News thread on that article (20 Aug 2026, 97 points)** https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49374036 — critique: high-profile interventions "distract from real unsexy solutions like grab the plastic in rivers BEFORE they make it to the ocean" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49375433); reply: "They pivoted to mostly river cleanup (interceptors) many years ago ... Ocean cleanup is last step, river grab is the step before that and having proper garbage collection would be best" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49375766); counter-critique that collection/recycling is itself a deflection and source reduction matters more (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49377389).
- **Take-away for the pitch:** judges who know the field will ask "why intercept rather than fix collection?" → our answer must include the *ward hotspot feedback loop* (source reduction) and position interception as measured last-line-of-defence, not the solution.

### The Great Bubble Barrier (NL) — compressed-air bubble curtain
- DutchNews (17 Sep 2024), citing an official City of Amsterdam report: the Westerdok Bubble Barrier "was launched in 2019 and since then has caught some 190,000 pieces of plastic waste a year, weighing around one tonne"; city waterways chief: "We expect the Bubble Barrier to catch 86% of floating plastic before it heads for the IJ waterway" *(expectation, not a measured independent figure in this article)*; "a 60m punctured pipe that runs along the bottom of the channel ... thanks to its diagonal positioning and the river current, the bubbles push pieces of plastic ... to one side, where it can be collected"; expansion to five more sites "at a cost of €600,000, plus €50,000 a year in operating costs"; "the impact on migrating fish still needs to be assessed". https://www.dutchnews.nl/2024/09/amsterdams-bubble-barrier-to-collect-canal-plastic-is-a-success/
- Company site https://thegreatbubblebarrier.com/en/ timed out (curl + web_fetch) on 2026-10-04 → India/Asia presence **NOT FOUND**.
- **Borrow:** no obstruction to boats/flow (a flood-safe idea for drains); diagonal deflection to a bank-side catch point. **Beat:** needs continuous compressed air (mains power) and a current to deflect; at tidal Mumbai outfalls the flow reverses twice a day, so a fixed diagonal curtain works only on the ebb (inference, to verify) — our tide-aware design handles both directions.
