### Evidence notes — stormwater network and BRIMSTOWAD

- A BRIMSTOWAD/MCGM storm-water drainage report says Greater Mumbai's SWD system includes about 2,000 km of roadside surface drains, about 440 km of underground drains/laterals, major and minor nullahs of 200 km and 87 km respectively, and 186 outfalls discharging surface runoff to rivers and the Arabian Sea. Its summary table gives total SWD length as 2,991 km, 30,208 water entrances, 136 outfalls to Arabian Sea, 26 to Mahim Creek, 10 to Mahul Creek, and 14 to Thane Creek. Source: BRIMSTOWAD report hosted by MoEF forest clearance portal, URL in Sources, accessed 2026-10-04.
- The same report states the old SWD system could handle 25 mm/hour at low tide, and that rainfall above 25 mm/hour combined with high tide creates waterlogging risk because tidal variation strongly affects stormwater discharge. Source: BRIMSTOWAD report, accessed 2026-10-04.
- Mumbai Live (2026 flood-control plan report) says Mumbai's existing system is designed for about 50 mm/hour and BMC's new plan aims to raise capacity to 100–120 mm/hour, with flood gates/pumping at 33 additional locations, 18 Mithi flood gates, mesh screens 8–10 ft high at selected spots, sensors on major drains/rivers, and pilots at seven sites. Source: Mumbai Live, accessed 2026-10-04.
- Hindustan Times reported BMC's BRIMSTOWAD plan for eight pumping stations after the 2005 deluge; by 2015 Lovegrove and Cleveland at Worli were set to operate, with four under construction and two yet to start, while project cost had escalated from ₹1,200 crore to ₹4,000 crore. Source: Hindustan Times, accessed 2026-10-04.
- The Hindu reported the Britannia storm-water pumping station at Reti Bunder was inaugurated in June 2016; it could flush 36,000 litres/second into the sea, cost ₹103 crore, had six pump sets and 20,000 litres diesel storage, and followed Haji Ali and Irla (completed 2011), Love Grove and Cleveland. Source: The Hindu, accessed 2026-10-04.
- Free Press Journal (2024) says eight pumping stations were recommended after the 26 July 2005 deluge: commissioned/operational sequence Irla (Juhu) in 2010, followed by Haji Ali, Cleveland, Love Grove, Britannia, and Gajdharbandh; Mogra and Mahul remained delayed by land issues. Source: Free Press Journal, accessed 2026-10-04.
