# C13 Recovery & upcycling — where floating flexible plastic from Mumbai drains can go
Access date for all sources: 2026-10-04.
## TL;DR (5–10 bullets, each with [Sx] citations)

- **Raw drain/creek flexible plastic is not a direct cement-kiln feed.** CPCB says co-processing needs pre-processing into a uniform stream, with lab checks for “Moisture, Calorific value, Ash, Chorine [chlorine]…” and size/chemical/heat-content control; waste with **>1.5% chlorine** should go to the main burner [S1].
- **RDF Grade I/II specs are dry and low-chlorine:** CPHEEO/MoHUA’s 2018 RDF table sets Grade I at **moisture <10%, chlorine <0.5%, NCV >4500 Kcal/kg**, and Grade II at **moisture <15%, chlorine <0.7%, NCV >3750 Kcal/kg** [S2].
- **Indicative RDF value is positive only after meeting specs:** CPHEEO/MoHUA suggests **₹1,800–3,600/t for RDF Grade I**, **₹1,500–3,000/t for Grade II**, **₹1,200–2,400/t for Grade III**, and **₹600–1,200/t for SCF**, derived from ₹/1000 Kcal assumptions [S2].
- **Mumbai’s planned Deonar WtE route is for municipal waste, not specifically drain film:** the EC is for “**600 TPD Waste to Energy Project to generate about 4 MW**” at Deonar; PIB later reported BMC’s timeline as **October 2025**, **about 7 MW**, and **₹504 Crores** capital cost [S7][S8].
- **Mechanical recycling is the highest-value route only for sorted/dry film.** A current India LDPE scrap benchmark page quoted **₹24.50/kg** and a 30-day range of **₹20.00–₹57.00/kg**; no verified source found that Mumbai recyclers accept wet, silt-laden drain film at these prices [S9].
- **Road construction has strict cleanliness and resin limits:** IRC SP:98-2019 allows only specified plastics and requires shredded plastic passing **2.36 mm** and retained on **600 micron**, with dust/impurities **≤1%**, at **6–8% by weight of bitumen** [S3].
- **EPR can monetise processing only through registered Plastic Waste Processors (PWPs).** CPCB’s SOP says recyclers, WtE plants, co-processing cement plants, plastic-to-oil units and composting facilities must register, and that PWPs issue certificates after audit/validation [S6].
- **For river-plastic operations, moisture/organics dominate gross mass.** Plastic Fischer states: “For every **1kg of plastic**, we have to move **2 kg of water and 1kg of organic materials**,” with **50% moisture**, **25% organics**, **25% non-organic** in what its systems handle [S5].
- **Local 2026 opportunity:** The Ocean Cleanup says Mumbai pilots target Trombay Creek and Malad Creek, with Plastic Fischer leading “responsible waste processing,” and estimates Mumbai leaks nearly **5 million kg** of plastic annually [S4].

## 1. Co-processing in cement kilns

**Regulatory route.** CPCB’s January 2017 draft guideline covers “Pre-processing and Co-processing of Hazardous and Other Wastes in Cement Plants” [S1]. It states that SPCBs may grant consent for cement plants to co-process “**SCF, RDF, plastic & other packaging wastes, tyre chips**” and that co-processing must comply with MoEF&CC emission standards notified by **GSR No. 497(E) dated 10.05.2016** [S1].

**Acceptance logic for our feedstock.** The relevant constraint is not simply “plastic yes/no”; it is whether recovered drain film can be converted into a homogeneous, dry, low-chlorine, high-CV feed. CPCB says a facility laboratory must test “**Moisture, Calorific value, Ash, Chorine, Fluorine, Carbon, Hydrogen, Sulphur, Nitrogen, Phosphorous, alkali and heavy metals**” [S1]. CPCB also says pre-processing is required because of waste heterogeneity, and the uniform waste-mix characteristics are “**particle size, chemical composition and heat content**” [S1]. Pre-processing equipment explicitly includes shredding/cutting, separation of foreign materials, “**Drying, Screening, Crushing, Palletisation, Granulation**,” metal detection and trommel/rotary/disc screening [S1].

**Chlorine/feed-point rule.** CPCB states: “**Wastes containing stable toxic components and also wastes containing more than 1.5% chlorine should be fed to the main burner**” [S1]. This matters because flexible PVC contamination and food/salt/silt contamination can push chlorine risk; our system must remove PVC-like items and test chlorine before sending material to kilns.

**Maharashtra/near-Mumbai cement plants.** VERIFIED from opened sources: CPCB/MoHUA sources prove the regulatory pathway but, in the fetched excerpts, did not provide a complete current Maharashtra plant list. The background research lead identified ACC Chanda Cement Works, Chandrapur, but I did not capture that exact list line in an opened web_fetch excerpt; therefore it is kept under UNVERIFIED / NOT FOUND rather than used as a cited fact.

**Gate fee / price.** The best verified price evidence is CPHEEO/MoHUA’s RDF guideline, not a plant-specific Mumbai gate fee. It says commercial acceptability was agreed at “**Rs. 0.4 per 1000 Kcal/kg**,” that prices should be dynamic and coal-linked, and gives indicative ranges: SCF **₹600–1,200/t**, RDF Grade III **₹1,200–2,400/t**, Grade II **₹1,500–3,000/t**, Grade I **₹1,800–3,600/t** [S2]. These are for specification-compliant SCF/RDF, not wet drain film.

**Transport.** CPHEEO/MoHUA recommends a cement-plant influence zone up to **400 km** and says “**transport cost for SCF/RDF up to 100 km from the cement plant shall be borne by cement plant**”; later it says beyond **100 km up to 400 km** the cost is borne by the ULB [S2].

## 2. RDF and waste-to-energy in MMR

**RDF grades.** CPHEEO/MoHUA’s 2018 guideline table defines SCF and RDF Grades III, II and I [S2]:

| Parameter | SCF | RDF Grade III | RDF Grade II | RDF Grade I |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Intended use | WtE/RDF pre-processing | Co-processing directly/after processing | Direct cement-kiln co-processing | Direct cement-kiln co-processing |
| Size | >400 mm only by mutual agreement | <50 mm or <20 mm | <50 mm or <20 mm | <50 mm or <20 mm |
| Ash max | <20% | <15% | <10% | <10% |
| Moisture max | <35% | <20% | <15% | <10% |
| Chlorine max | <1.0% | <1.0% | <0.7% | <0.5% |
| Net calorific value | >1500 Kcal/kg | >3000 Kcal/kg | >3750 Kcal/kg | >4500 Kcal/kg |

**MMR facilities.** The verified Deonar source is the SEIAA/MoEFCC EC letter for “**Development of 600 TPD Waste to Energy Project to generate about 4 MW of power at Deonar, Mumbai**,” dated **07/12/2021**, EC ID **EC21B037MH160158** [S7]. PIB later reported that BMC generates **6400 TPD** MSW, processes/disposes **5800 TPD** at Kanjurmarg through “Bio-reactor and composting technology,” and disposes **about 600 TPD** at Deonar; it also says a **600 TPD** WtE project was undertaken, commissioning **October 2025**, expected **about 7 MW**, cost **about Rs. 504 Crores** [S8].

**Does RDF/WtE accept dirty wet film?** NOT FOUND in opened primary sources. The Grade I/II/III moisture limits imply wet drain film must be dried; no opened BMC/MCGM source states that Kanjurmarg or Deonar will accept silt-laden flexible film directly.

## 3. Mechanical recycling of film (incl. wet/dirty acceptance, prices)

**Likely role.** Mechanical recycling should be the first value route for clean, sorted LDPE/HDPE film and carry bags, but it is fragile for drain-recovered flexible packaging because water, organics, grit/silt and MLP laminates add washing/drying cost and damage recyclate quality.

**Verified price lead.** A current market benchmark page for India LDPE scrap stated: “**currently trading at ₹24.50 / KG**” and “**Over the last 30 days, spot benchmarks ranged between ₹20.00 and ₹57.00 / KG**” [S9]. This is not Mumbai-drain-specific and should be treated as a clean/sorted market benchmark, not the value of wet gross recovery.

**Wet/dirty acceptance.** NOT FOUND: no opened source verified that Dharavi/Mumbai LDPE film recyclers accept wet, silt-laden drain film, nor a verified price discount for dirty vs clean film. Our design should assume dirty drain film needs: draining, organics removal, grit removal, washing, dewatering/drying, sorting by polymer, and rejection of MLP/foamed/cloth/biomedical-like items before sale.

**MLP value.** NOT FOUND: no opened source gave a public ₹/kg for post-consumer MLP sachets from drains. Treat MLP as end-of-life/co-processing unless a named registered recycler/board maker gives a gate price.

## 4. Pyrolysis / waste-to-oil

**Regulatory status.** CPCB’s PWP SOP explicitly includes “**plastic waste to oil units**” as PWPs that must register on the centralized EPR portal [S6]. The process-code table includes **E4: End of life PW processing in Waste to Oil Units** [S6].

**Feedstock/criticism.** NOT FOUND in opened sources: no CPCB pyrolysis technical SOP, Maharashtra registered waste-to-oil unit list, feedstock moisture/chlorine limits, oil-quality requirements or emissions criticism was successfully opened. Therefore, pyrolysis should not be a primary design outlet until a registered Maharashtra PWP, consent conditions, and feedstock specs are verified.

## 5. Roads and other routes

**Roads.** IRC SP:98-2019 is viable only for a clean, dried, shredded subset. It says end-of-life packaging films are a problem fraction, but the specification requires only acceptable thermoplastics and says “**PVC shall not be used since they release lethal levels of dioxines**” [S3]. It also requires plastic passing a **2.36 mm** sieve and retained on **600 micron** sieve, dust/impurities **not more than 1 percent**, and melt-flow testing for LDPE/HDPE [S3]. The dosage is **6 to 8** percent by bitumen weight for dense mixes and **6% to 8%** for open-graded/premix surfacing [S3].

**Process temperatures.** IRC states aggregate is heated to **150–175°C**, plastic-coated aggregate is mixed with bitumen at **150–170°C** for about **15 secs**, and road laying temperature is **130–160°C** [S3]. It warns TGA shows gas evolution/thermal degradation may occur beyond **180°C** [S3].

**Boards/lumber/upcycling.** NOT FOUND in opened sources for Mumbai-specific MLP boards/plastic lumber examples. Plastic Fischer says it operates small recycling labs that upcycle river plastic into “high-visibility vests… floaters… candles, pouches” as circular-economy examples, but its main non-recyclable outlet is national co-processing [S5].

## 6. EPR certificates via registered processors

**Who can generate certificates.** CPCB’s SOP says PWPs are “**recyclers, waste to energy plants including co-processing cement plants, plastic waste to oil units, and industrial composting facilities**” that must register with SPCBs/PCCs on the centralized EPR portal [S6]. It states PWPs issue certificates against plastic waste processed, and “**The PWP shall be able to issue certificates to PIBOs post conduction of Audit and validation of its facilities by the SPCB/PCC**” [S6].

**Relevant process codes.** CPCB’s process-code list includes recycling CAT-I/II/III to pellets/chips/products, **C1** composting CAT-IV, **E1** co-processing in cement plants, **E2** co-processing in steel plants, **E3** WtE, and **E4** waste-to-oil [S6]. For our flexible source, the relevant end-of-life certificate route is usually E1/E3/E4 after registration; mechanical recycling would be R2/R5 for Category II if accepted by a registered recycler [S6].

**Plastic categories.** The opened secondary mirror of the 2022 PWM amendment says Category 1 is rigid packaging; Category 2 is “**Flexible plastic packaging of single layer or multilayer… plastic sheets… carry bags, plastic sachet or pouches**”; Category 3 is multilayered plastic packaging with at least one plastic and one non-plastic layer; Category 4 is compostable packaging/carry bags [S10]. Because the official egazette URL could not be fetched, treat this as high-confidence but not primary in this file.

**Drain/river-recovered eligibility.** NOT FOUND explicitly. The rules/SOP are based on category and processing by registered PWPs; no opened source says drain- or river-recovered plastic is ineligible. Practical eligibility likely depends on documentation, weighing, category segregation, registered PWP processing, and audit trail.

**Public certificate price.** NOT FOUND. No opened CPCB/EPR source gave ₹/kg certificate prices by category/year.

**The Ocean Cleanup / Plastic Fischer disposal.** The Ocean Cleanup says Plastic Fischer will lead “efficient collection of intercepted trash and responsible waste processing” in Mumbai [S4]. Plastic Fischer says “Recyclables enter local markets, while non-recyclables are sent to leading national co-processing plants, with our expenses covering both transport and treatment” [S5]. Pinwheel’s project page similarly says collected material is dried/sorted/co-processed; recyclables go to local recycling companies and non-recyclables are used as alternative energy sources in cement plants [S11].

## 7. Moisture and contamination of drain-recovered plastic

**Best verified river-plastic datum.** Plastic Fischer provides the most directly relevant number for river/drain interception operations: “**For every 1kg of plastic, we have to move 2 kg of water and 1kg of organic materials**” [S5]. It further shows handled categories as **Organics 25%**, **Non-Organic 25%**, and **Moisture 50%** [S5].

**DERIVED implication.** If gross intercepted material is 4 kg (1 kg plastic + 2 kg water + 1 kg organics), then net plastic is **25% of gross wet handled mass**; water drying/draining loss alone is **50% of gross handled mass**. This matches Plastic Fischer’s own category percentages [S5].

**Other studies.** NOT FOUND in opened sources: no Indian municipal or peer-reviewed drain/river study giving Mumbai nullah plastic moisture/silt mass loss was opened.

## Design implications for our system (table: Finding | Implication)

| Finding | Implication |
|---|---|
| Gross river/drain recovery may be only ~25% net plastic after water and organics [S5]. | Size interception baskets, carts, drying floors, labour and economics on gross wet mass, not reported dry plastic mass. |
| Cement/RDF specs require low moisture, low chlorine and high NCV [S1][S2]. | Add a low-cost pre-processing line: draining, drying, grit removal, sorting, shredding, chlorine/PVC exclusion, and batch testing. |
| Co-processing has indicative positive RDF value only after specification compliance [S2]. | Do not model revenue for raw drain film; model avoided disposal + EPR/end-of-life certificates + possible RDF value after pre-processing. |
| Mechanical recycling price benchmarks are much higher than RDF values but not verified for wet drain film [S9]. | Split “clean recyclable film” early; protect it from further contamination; send dirty MLP/residue to end-of-life routes. |
| Roads require ≤1% impurities and 2.36 mm–600 micron shredded plastic [S3]. | Road route is an outlet for a small clean, dry, controlled-polymer fraction, not the main mixed drain-plastic sink. |
| CPCB PWP SOP has separate process codes for recycling and end-of-life routes [S6]. | Partner only with registered PWPs and keep batch-wise category/weight documentation so EPR certificates can be generated. |
| Deonar/Kanjurmarg data describes bulk MSW systems, not source-separated drain film acceptance [S7][S8]. | Do not assume BMC facilities are buyers/offtakers; secure MoUs with specific PWP/kiln/recycler. |
| The Ocean Cleanup/Plastic Fischer is piloting in Trombay and Malad in 2026 [S4]. | Benchmark our Mumbai field design against their flow: intercept → dry → sort → local recyclers/co-processing, and seek complementarity rather than duplicating proof-of-concept claims. |
