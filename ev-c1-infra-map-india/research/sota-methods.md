## TL;DR

1. **India's own aggregate charger-count data disagrees across authoritative sources within the same ~12-month window** — WRI India says 34,629 stations (Nov 2024) for 5.4M EVs (1:156 ratio) [verified]; PIB/CEA-sourced press figures say 25,202 (Oct 2024); Down To Earth citing an ICCT Oct 2024 working paper says ~12,000–ish with a "1 charger per 191 EVs" ratio; IEEFA says 5,151 (2022)→52,718 (Jul 2026). None of these reconcile cleanly — this is itself the strongest evidence for why a transparent, methodologically-defensible integration platform (exactly what the judging criteria reward) is needed, rather than any single "best" dataset.
2. **No peer-reviewed or preprint work anywhere applies capture-recapture / multiple-systems estimation (MSE) to EV charging station completeness** — confirmed via extensive, differently-phrased searching by a dedicated sub-agent. This is a genuine, verified white space your team could be first to fill (§g).
3. **No published entity-resolution methodology targets EV charging station records specifically** either (Splink/dedupe/Zingg have zero POI/charging examples in their own docs) — but **Open Charge Map's actual production code** (`openchargemap/ocm-system`) is a real, readable, deterministic dedup cascade (exact-key → exact-string → title+250m → pure 50m radius) that explicitly flags its own PlugShare-matching as "not production ready" — the best concrete baseline-to-beat found anywhere (§f).
4. **2024–2026 siting literature has real validated-on-real-data papers for bi-level, queueing-aware, grid-constrained, and GNN/LLM-assisted siting** (Montreal, Changchun, Hefei, Dallas, Hanoi, Chengdu, Glasgow/Edinburgh, Scotland) — but **zero papers apply classic FRLM by name to an Indian highway corridor**, despite PM E-DRIVE's own "charger every 25 km" highway spacing rule being a policy analogue crying out for a formal flow-refueling treatment (§b).
5. **2SFCA-family accessibility metrics have been applied to EV charging in China (3 verified 2023–2025 papers, Gaussian-2SFCA) and generically reviewed (91-study review)**, but **no paper applies 2SFCA to EV charging in India**, and **no India-specific drive-time-isochrone EV-accessibility study exists** — both confirmed gaps (§c).
6. **The EU AFIR regulation gives exact, legally binding kW-per-BEV and per-corridor targets** (1.3 kW/BEV, 0.8 kW/PHEV cumulative; 400→600 kW pools every 60 km on TEN-T core by 2025/2027) that are directly adaptable as an India benchmark — but **no equivalent India target exists today**, and **no formal numeric "charging desert" threshold exists anywhere in the literature** (confirmed gap — DOE/NREL/academic sources explicitly avoid a hard cutoff) (§d).
7. **Charger reliability numbers are large, real, and alarming, and nobody is folding them into "available infrastructure" counts**: Delhi field survey 83.7% non-functional (IEEFA, Jun 2024 sample of 37); Bay Area peer-reviewed 73.3% functional of 655 EVSEs (Rempel et al. 2024, *Human Factors*); US national J.D. Power 2025: 14% of all charging visits failed; India secondary sources repeatedly cite "~50–60% functional" / "nearly half non-functional" (§h). A platform that reports installed counts without an uptime-derating layer is reporting a number the literature says is substantially wrong.
8. **EV adoption forecasting SOTA (2026) is a Gaussian-process neighbourhood-disaggregation model that explicitly beats logistic-growth and scaled-scenario baselines** (Flower, Li & Padget, *Nature Communications*, validated on 1,294 neighbourhoods in England/Wales) — directly transplantable to India using VAHAN district/RTO history + NITI/CEEW state ceilings, and nobody has done this for India yet (§e).
9. **Production-grade, actively-maintained open-source building blocks already exist for two of your hardest sub-problems**: `pysal/spopt` (381★, BSD-3, classic MCLP/p-median/LSCP, pushed 2026-10-02) for siting, and `moj-analytical-services/splink` (2,452★, MIT, Fellegi-Sunter, pushed 2026-09-30) for entity resolution — neither has ever been applied to EV chargers, so doing so would itself be a novel application, not just an engineering exercise (§b, §f).
10. **Both GitHub repos found for "India EV charging demand prediction" have 0 stars and are small individual projects** — confirming there is no existing serious open platform for India-specific EV charging demand/siting analytics, which is exactly the space this competition is asking you to fill.

## (a) EV charging demand estimation from registrations / mobility / POI / traffic data — India focus + spatial disaggregation

### Global/general methodological anchors

- **NREL EVI-Pro / EVI-Pro Lite** — bottom-up simulation tool: ingests household/vehicle travel-diary data (trip distances, timing), EV range/efficiency assumptions, and charger-access scenarios (home/work/public/DCFC) to simulate per-vehicle charging events and aggregate them into a required-charger count and grid load profile, by charger level and location type. [NREL EVI-Pro Overview (search-summary)](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy21osti/77651.pdf) — **UNVERIFIED by direct read**: the PDF domain resolution failed in this session (`nrel.gov` DNS lookup error via `web_fetch`); description above is from `web_search` synthesis only, not independently opened. Treat the mechanism description as plausible but not source-verified; do not quote specific EVI-Pro output numbers without re-fetching. A public-facing version, **EVI-Pro Lite**, is hosted at `afdc.energy.gov/evi-pro-lite` per the same search synthesis — also not independently opened. Maturity: **in production** (US DOE-hosted public tool) per secondary description, **but UNVERIFIED by us**.
- **ICCT global/EV-CHARGE model** — ICCT's standard methodology for translating projected EV stock into public-charger and kW needs, varying inputs by vehicle class, home-charging access, and urban density; outputs a "vehicles-per-charger" (VPC) ratio. [ICCT "Global electric vehicle charging infrastructure market monitor" PDF](https://theicct.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ID-462-%E2%80%93-EV-Charging-Monitor_research-brief_final-1-1.pdf) — **fetch attempt timed out twice in this session — UNVERIFIED by direct read.** VPC figures circulating (Norway ~3:1, Europe ~8–15:1) are from `web_search` synthesis only and are **not** independently confirmed; do not cite the specific ratios without re-verification. This is the same ICCT methodology family used for the India truck-charging working paper below.

### Spatial disaggregation (dasymetric) of registration counts

- **Classic method family (not independently re-verified in depth this session, standard GIS technique)**: dasymetric mapping redistributes coarse administrative-unit counts (e.g., district-level VAHAN registrations) into finer spatial units using ancillary correlates — land use/land cover, building footprints, population/socio-economic rasters, road network density — under the assumption that (e.g.) residential density correlates with vehicle ownership. Tooling mentioned in `web_search` synthesis (not independently opened): **EPA's Dasymetric Toolbox**, a GRASS GIS machine-learning dasymetric plugin ([ResearchGate PDF, "A Tool for Machine Learning Based Dasymetric Mapping Approaches in GRASS GIS"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354026696_A_TOOL_FOR_MACHINE_LEARNING_BASED_DASYMETRIC_MAPPING_APPROACHES_IN_GRASS_GIS/fulltext/611fade41e95fe241ae5409b/A-TOOL-FOR-MACHINE-LEARNING-BASED-DASYMETRIC-MAPPING-APPROACHES-IN-GRASS-GIS.pdf), not fetched directly — **UNVERIFIED**), and a "Disaggregator" tool for ArcGIS Pro ([Springer, *Int. J. Digital Earth*, DOI via link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12145-021-00737-9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12145-021-00737-9) — not fetched directly, **UNVERIFIED**). An "HPSDRM" (Hybrid Population Spatial Data Redistribution Model) is cited by `web_search` synthesis as outperforming traditional dasymetric mapping "by at least 10%" — **UNVERIFIED, not independently opened, do not quote the 10% figure as confirmed.**
- **Best verified SOTA anchor for this exact sub-problem — not India, but directly transplantable**: **Flower, I., Li, F., & Padget, J., "A probabilistic forecasting framework for neighbourhood-level disaggregation of electric vehicle adoption scenarios,"** *Nature Communications*, published online 2026-02-12 per institutional record / 2026-06-13 per a secondary citation. **[Verified by direct fetch of the full abstract](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/a-probabilistic-forecasting-framework-for-neighbourhood-level-dis/)** (University of Bath institutional repository record), accessed 2026-10-04 — maturity: **peer-reviewed journal article, paper-only (no code link found in this session)**.
  - Exact quoted abstract: *"This paper introduces a Gaussian process (GP)-based forecasting framework that combines granular historical EV registration data with top-down regional scenarios to generate probabilistic neighbourhood-level forecasts. The GP captures how local adoption deviates from regional trends, encoded in the GP's mean function, ensuring consistency with broader scenarios while accounting for local variation and uncertainty. We validate the framework using ten representative local authority districts in England and Wales, covering a total of 1,294 neighbourhoods. The framework outperforms baseline methods (scaled scenario, logistic growth, linear extrapolation) in normalised mean absolute error, with statistically significant improvements at horizons of three years and beyond."*
  - **DOI discrepancy flagged**: the institutional record lists `10.21203/rs.3.rs-7074730/v1` (a Research-Square-pattern preprint DOI) while tagging the venue as *Nature Communications*; a `web_search` separately reported the article as published with DOI `10.1038/s41467-026-74155-0` and authorship Flower/Li/Padget, 2026-06-13. Direct fetch of `nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74155-0` failed with HTTP 406 in this session. **Treat the exact final-journal DOI as UNVERIFIED-by-us**; the abstract content itself is verified via the Bath institutional mirror.
  - **How to use/beat it**: this is precisely "spatial disaggregation of registration counts," done with a principled uncertainty-quantified method (GP) that **beats logistic growth as a baseline** — exactly the naive method most India EV-forecast commentary implicitly uses. Nobody has applied this GP-neighbourhood-disaggregation approach to VAHAN data. Doing so — using VAHAN RTO/district history as the "granular historical registration data" and NITI Aayog/CEEW state-level scenarios as the "top-down regional scenario" — would be a direct, novel, and methodologically defensible transplant of 2026 SOTA to India, with a built-in backtesting protocol (3+-year-horizon NMAE comparison against logistic-growth/scaled-scenario baselines) already demonstrated by the source paper.

## (c) Spatial accessibility metrics — 2SFCA family & drive-time isochrones

> ⚠️ Caveat flagged by the researching sub-agent and preserved here: the six classic-variant descriptions below rest mainly on `web_search` AI-synthesis of secondary sources, cross-checked against one paper (PMC7959250) that **was** read directly. Treat the variant *descriptions* as good-confidence secondary summaries, not primary-source-verified quotes, except where marked otherwise.

### Classics (originally healthcare accessibility, pre-2024)

1. **Original 2SFCA** — Radke & Mu (2000); formalized by **Luo & Wang (2003)** for Chicago-region primary-care access. Uses a **binary/fixed-radius catchment** (step-function distance decay: 1 inside threshold, 0 outside). [Partially corroborated directly via PMC7959250, which states: *"Since its inception (Luo and Wang, 2003)... The classic 2SFCA uses a fixed catchment area size."*]
2. **E2SFCA** (Luo & Qi, 2009) — replaces the binary catchment with **tiered/continuous distance-decay weighting across multiple travel-time zones**, so nearer facilities count more.
3. **Gaussian 2SFCA** (commonly attributed to **Dai, 2010**, *Health & Place*, metro-Detroit breast-cancer study) — replaces the step function with a **continuous Gaussian distance-decay curve**.
4. **3SFCA** — Wan, Zou & Sternberg (2012), *Int. J. Geographical Information Science* — adds a **third step modeling demand-side competition** among population locations for the same provider (corrects gravity-based 2SFCA's demand overestimation). [Title/authors/venue/year verified via Semantic Scholar API.]
5. **i2SFCA** (inverted 2SFCA) — Wang (2018) — **swaps supply and demand roles** to measure facility-side "potential crowdedness" (e.g., patients-per-bed) rather than resident-side accessibility, using the same generic distance-decay function. **[Directly verified, read in PMC7959250, Table 1.]**
6. **Hybrid variant closest to "MH3SFCA"**: a **Modified-Huff-based Variable 3-Step FCA (*Health & Place*, 2023)** combines **Huff-model competition weighting with variable (non-fixed) catchment sizes** — a healthcare-only hybrid; **no EV-charging application of this specific variant was found.**

### EU AFIR — exact legally-binding kW-per-BEV and corridor targets

**[Verified — exact primary legal text, fetched directly from EUR-Lex]**: **Regulation (EU) 2023/1804** of the European Parliament and Council, 13 Sept 2023, on the deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure, Article 3. > *"Member States shall ensure that, at the end of each year, starting from 2024,... (a) for each light-duty battery electric vehicle registered in their territory, **a total power output of at least 1,3 kW** is provided through publicly accessible recharging stations; and (b) for each light-duty plug-in hybrid vehicle..., **a total power output of at least 0,80 kW**..."* (Art. 3(1)) — with a derogation once BEV share reaches **≥15%** of the light-duty fleet (Art. 3(2)).

## (e) EV adoption forecasting at regional level

### Classic anchor

**Bass, F.M. (1969), "A New Product Growth for Model Consumer Durables,"** *Management Science*, 15(5), 215–227. DOI: [10.1287/mnsc.15.5.215](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.15.5.215). **[Verified directly via Crossref API]**, accessed 2026-10-04 — maturity: **classic, paper-only**. ### 2025–2026 SOTA: hierarchical/pooled regional forecasting beats naive logistic growth

- **Flower, Li & Padget (2026), *Nature Communications*** — see full citation and method in §a. **This is the most important single forecasting-methods finding in this whole document**: its GP-based framework **explicitly benchmarks against, and beats, logistic-growth and scaled-scenario baselines** ("statistically significant improvements at horizons of three years and beyond"), on a real validated dataset (1,294 neighbourhoods, 10 local authority districts, England & Wales). It directly operationalizes "hierarchical pooling across regions" (top-down regional scenario + bottom-up local registration history, reconciled via a GP mean function) — i.e., partial pooling in spirit, via a different (GP, not explicit Bayesian-hierarchical) mechanism.
- **Hierarchical Bayesian regional-pooling for US BEV sales** — "Forecasting U.S. Electric Vehicle Adoption and Electricity..." SSRN working paper. **Fetch attempt returned HTTP 403 in this session — UNVERIFIED by direct read.** Per `web_search` synthesis only (not independently confirmed): uses a **national Bayesian logistic-diffusion model** combined with **state-level regression-based ceilings** (predictors: charging density, income, urbanization, gasoline prices, ZEV mandates), with **partial pooling** so data-sparse states borrow strength from the pooled national trend while data-rich states (e.g., California with ZEV mandates) deviate further — projecting persistent divergence between ZEV-mandate and non-mandate states through 2040. **Do not cite specific numeric forecasts from this source without re-verification**; the modeling *approach* (Bayesian logistic diffusion + state ceiling regression + partial pooling) is a reasonable, standard hierarchical-Bayes pattern consistent with the broader literature, but we could not confirm this specific paper's exact claims ourselves.

### Backtesting methodology — what the literature actually does

The only backtesting protocol **directly verified in this research** is Flower/Li/Padget's: fit on historical data, forecast forward at multiple horizons, compare **normalised mean absolute error (NMAE)** against baselines (scaled-scenario, logistic-growth, linear-extrapolation), and report **statistical significance of the improvement, specifically broken out by forecast horizon** (their result: significant from 3-year horizons onward — i.e., their method's advantage *grows* with horizon length, which is exactly the regime infrastructure planners care about). Generic "backtesting best practices" for logistic-curve EV forecasts (rolling-window refit, hold-out-year cross-validation, RMSE/MAE/MAPE residual analysis) surfaced repeatedly in `web_search` synthesis but **were not independently traced to a single primary methodological source** in this session — treat as standard-practice description, not a verified citation.

**How to use/beat this for India**: no source found runs a rigorous, published backtest of a Bass/logistic/Bayesian/GP forecast specifically against India VAHAN data. The GRAAM FY21-22→FY25-26 series is an ideal, already-cleaned ground-truth series for exactly this: fit any candidate model (Bass, logistic, hierarchical-Bayes-with-state-pooling, or a GP-disaggregation model à la Flower et al.) on an early subset of years, forecast forward, and report the same NMAE-by-horizon comparison — directly reproducing 2026 SOTA methodology on India's own data, which nobody has published yet.

### The one genuinely relevant real-world artifact: Open Charge Map's own production deduplication code

[`openchargemap/ocm-system`](https://github.com/openchargemap/ocm-system) — **verified via GitHub API: 151 stars, pushed 2026-09-18 → actively maintained** (org also includes `ocm-app` 47★ pushed 2026-09-18, `ocm-export` 47★ pushed 2026-04-23, `ocm-docs` 9★ pushed 2026-04-13, and a **deprecated** `ocm-data` 29★ last pushed 2021-11-05).

**Directly read in full** (via GitHub Contents API + base64 decode): `Import/OCM.Import.Common/ImportManager.cs` and `Import/OCM.Import.Common/Providers/ImportProvider_PS.cs`.

- **Real multi-network provider list confirmed** by directory listing (`Import/OCM.Import.Common/Providers/`): includes **AFDC** (US DOE), **PlugShare** (`DataProviderID = 27`), and ~9 others.
- **Candid admission of difficulty, quoted directly from source code**:
  ```csharp
  ProviderName = "PlugShare";
  DataProviderID = 27;
  IsProductionReady = false;   // flagged NOT production-ready
  AllowDuplicatePOIWithDifferentOperator = true;
  MergeDuplicatePOIEquipment = false;
  // TODO: operator not well matched, usage type not known, multiple connectors
  // at same site not imported due to duplicate POI. Requires merge process.
  ```
  This is **direct, primary-source proof that even OCM's own maintainers consider cross-network PlugShare-matching unsolved** — "as close to ground truth on real-world EV-charging ER difficulty as exists" anywhere in this research.
- **The actual matching algorithm** (`DeDuplicateList()` / `IsDuplicateLocation()`, read in full) is a **deterministic rule cascade, no ML/probabilistic weights** (architecturally simpler than Fellegi-Sunter/Splink):
  ```csharp
  public const int DUPLICATE_DISTANCE_METERS = 50;
  public const int DUPLICATE_TITLE_DISTANCE_METERS = 250; // wider radius when title also matches
  ```
  Cascade (first match wins, cheapest checks first): (1) exact lat/lon equality → (2) same `DataProviderID` + same source-native key (definite match) → (3) exact identical address string → (4) matching title AND within 250 m → (5) coarse bounding-box pre-filter then precise Haversine-style distance **< 50 m**.
- Matches are **not hard-deleted**: marked "delisted duplicate" with a `ParentChargePointID` pointing to the canonical record and an auto-comment `"[Duplicate of OCM-{id}]"` — a cluster-ID/golden-record pattern, with a safety guard (`if (cpList.Count < 50) report.Delisted = new List<ChargePoint>();`) against mass-mis-delisting on incomplete batches.

**How to use/beat it**: OCM proves at production scale that a **simple deterministic cascade is viable and cheap** for merging multi-network charging POI data, but its own code admits this breaks down for operator mismatches and multi-connector sites — exactly where **Fellegi-Sunter probabilistic weighting (Splink) or LLM-based semantic matching (GER-LLM-style)** would add value OCM currently lacks. **No academic benchmark dataset for EV-charging-station entity resolution (equivalent to `febrl` for persons) appears to exist publicly** — building one from India's CPO landscape (Statiq, Tata Power, ChargeZone, IOCL/BPCL/HPCL, state DISCOM lists, OCM-India subset, PlugShare-India subset) and open-sourcing it would itself be a novel, citable contribution.
