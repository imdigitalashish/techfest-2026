## 1. Problem understanding

**Who decides what.** MHI, ICAT, state nodal agencies, DISCOMs and urban local bodies decide *where*, *how many* and *what type* of public chargers to fund or permit, and they must defend those choices. That needs three things that are not published together today:
- a located, deduplicated inventory of public charging;
- demand by region and vehicle segment;
- a forward estimate of need.

**Fragmentation, measured.** Six publications report India's public charging very differently:

| Count | As on | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 12,146 stations | Feb 2024 | government data via IEEFA |
| 34,629 stations | Nov 2024 | WRI India |
| 25,202 | Dec 2024 | MoP, via PIB 2086533 |
| 26,367 | 1 Apr 2025 | BEE, via PIB 2118894 |
| 29,277 | 1 Aug 2025 | MoP, via PIB 2151390 |
| 52,718 stations | Jul 2026 | IEEFA |

Units and definitions differ (stations vs chargers vs connectors), and no official series gives locations. Operators count in their own units: "10000+ EV Chargers Installed" (Statiq) and "2,000+ mobility stations" (Jio-bp, fuel and EV combined). The Ministry of Power's 2024 guidelines say BEE "has created National online database of all public charging stations across India", and that operators "will share annual data on energy sold per EV charger" with it. We found no public, machine-readable feed of that database, and BEE's EV Yatra portal did not respond from our network on 4 Oct 2026 (UNVERIFIED whether it offers bulk access).

**What open data can see.** We linked three open sources:
- OpenStreetMap: <!--n:osm_n_in_india-->599<!--/n--> features in India
- Open Charge Map: <!--n:ocm_n_in_india-->1,869<!--/n-->
- Overture Maps places: <!--n:ovt_n_in_india-->1,039<!--/n-->

They link into <!--n:linked_sites-->3,336<!--/n--> distinct sites, which is <!--n:open_coverage_pct-->11.4%<!--/n--> of the official 29,277. Only <!--n:sites_in_all3-->7<!--/n--> sites appear in all three. *An honest national map therefore needs a measured coverage, not just more dots.*

**Demand is observable.** The VAHAN 4 dashboard covers all 36 states/UTs and 1,468 of 1,469 registering offices. We count battery-electric registrations as the fuels `ELECTRIC(BOV)` and `PURE EV`:
- <!--n:nat_bev_cum-->10,550,090<!--/n--> to date (4 Oct 2026);
- <!--n:nat_bev_2025-->2,356,971<!--/n--> in calendar 2025, which is <!--n:nat_bev_share_2025-->8.05%<!--/n--> of all registrations;
- a nowcast of <!--n:nowcast_2026_m-->3.60 million<!--/n--> for 2026 (+<!--n:nowcast_growth-->53%<!--/n-->; §2.4).

**First gap signal, with dates aligned.** BEV registered by July 2025 (<!--n:nat_bev_jul2025-->6,919,965<!--/n-->) against the official stations of 1 August 2025 gives <!--n:nat_bev_jul2025_per_pcs-->236<!--/n--> BEV per public station nationally. With today's registrations it is <!--n:nat_bev_per_pcs-->360<!--/n-->. Among large states (date-aligned) the range runs from <!--n:state_bev_jul2025_per_pcs_karnataka-->99<!--/n--> (Karnataka) through <!--n:state_bev_jul2025_per_pcs_uttar_pradesh-->565<!--/n--> (Uttar Pradesh) and <!--n:state_bev_jul2025_per_pcs_bihar-->736<!--/n--> (Bihar) to <!--n:state_bev_jul2025_per_pcs_assam-->759<!--/n--> (Assam).

Registrations are a fair proxy for the on-road BEV fleet: <!--n:bev_since_2020_pct-->95.3%<!--/n--> were registered in 2020 or later and <!--n:bev_since_2022_pct-->90.8%<!--/n--> in 2022 or later. Survival curves matter mainly for the <!--n:bev_before_2020-->500,459<!--/n--> older registrations. We still treat the ratio as a *signal, not an answer*, because most e-2W/3W charge at home or depots, or swap batteries; segment-aware demand is §2.6.

**Policy anchors (verified at the source).**
- **MoP Guidelines 2024** (No. 12/2/2018-EV, 17 Sep 2024), clause 11(1):
  - urban areas: one charging station per 1 km × 1 km grid by FY2030;
  - highways, expressways and major roads: every 20 km on both sides;
  - long-range and heavy-duty: fast charging every 100 km.
- **PM E-DRIVE:** ₹2,000 crore for 22,100 fast chargers for e-4W, 1,800 for e-buses and 48,400 for e-2W/3W (PIB 2060774).
- **NITI Aayog:** "there is no global consensus on number of charging points required for a certain number of EVs".

Requirements must therefore be derived bottom-up and transparently, not from a single ratio.

**Study boundary.** India; public and semi-public charging, all segments. Levels: national → state → district → urban grid → highway corridors. Horizon: today to 2030. Demonstration geography: **Maharashtra** (<!--n:mh_bev_cum-->1,185,108<!--/n--> BEV registrations, second only to Uttar Pradesh), with district results on current boundaries (incl. Palghar).

## 2. Analytical approach — built and tested on real data

We built 14 components with typed contracts and an isolated metric each ([architecture](../architecture/components.md)). Below, what each stage does and how well it works today.

### 2.1 Integration: one provenance-tracked census of charging sites
- **VAHAN ingest** replays the public dashboard's own requests: state, RTO, vehicle class, fuel and month, with no browser and no third-party mirror. The sum over RTOs equals the state total exactly: error <!--n:mh_reconciliation_err-->0.0<!--/n--> across Maharashtra's <!--n:mh_rtos-->61<!--/n--> offices, and likewise for Chhattisgarh.
- **Charger sources** are each normalised to one model: a *site* with connectors (type, count, kW), operator, status and as-on date. Every record keeps its source and licence (OSM: ODbL; Overture: CDLA-Permissive-2.0 / Apache-2.0 / CC0; OCM: per provider).
- **Linkage:** records are matched across sources by mutual nearest neighbour within `150 m`, then grouped into connected components (sites). Next: a Fellegi–Sunter matcher (Splink) on distance, name, operator and connectors, scored by pairwise F1 on hand-labelled pairs.
- **Overture category is incomplete.** Many Indian chargers are filed under other categories, so we add a strict EV-name rule. In a random sample of 30 name-matched places, all were charging sites; one lay outside India and is removed by the polygon clip ([sample](../research/data/overture_name_rule_sample.csv)).
- **Free-text fields are unreliable.** 156 Open Charge Map entries carry a state name that contradicts their own coordinates, so every record is placed by point-in-polygon, never by text.

### 2.2 How complete is the census? (spike 1)
We treat each source as an independent "capture" of the true set of sites. A log-linear multiple-systems model (Poisson regression on the 7 observed capture histories, chosen by AIC, with bootstrap 90% CIs) estimates the sites that no source lists. We found no prior application of this method to EV-charger inventories ([search log](../research/fact-check.md)).

**Results, stated as found:**
- **National:** <!--n:mse_nat-->80,833<!--/n--> sites (90% CI <!--n:mse_nat_lo-->35,915<!--/n-->–<!--n:mse_nat_hi-->171,036<!--/n-->).
- **State validation** against the official counts, which the estimator never sees (<!--n:mse_states-->17<!--/n--> states with ≥ 100 stations):
  - the median estimate is <!--n:mse_median_ratio-->0.58<!--/n-->× the official count, against <!--n:union_median_ratio-->0.094<!--/n-->× for the raw union;
  - the 90% CI contains the official count in only <!--n:mse_ci_coverage-->47%<!--/n--> of states.
- The estimator therefore **<!--n:mse_passes-->fails<!--/n-->** our pre-registered pass rule (median error within 25% *and* CI coverage ≥ 80%).
- **Robustness:** across 4 model-selection rules and 3 linkage radii (<!--n:mse_sens_runs-->6<!--/n--> variants), the national estimate ranges from <!--n:mse_nat_min_model-->21,339<!--/n--> to <!--n:mse_nat_max_model-->80,833<!--/n-->. <!--n:mse_sens_any_pass-->none<!--/n--> of the variants passes (best CI coverage <!--n:mse_sens_cov_max-->47%<!--/n-->). The causes are thin overlaps and heterogeneous capture: every list finds the same easy, branded, urban sites.
- **What we use instead:** the per-state *open-data coverage* (open sites ÷ official count) is shown on the map, so a planner sees where the data is too thin to trust. The estimator is re-run whenever a denser list arrives (operator feeds, UEI on Beckn, the BEE database).

### 2.3 District supply: small-area allocation, validated
Where no official district count exists, we allocate the official state total across districts:
- weight for each district = BEV<sup>b</sup> · (open sites + 1)<sup>c</sup>;
- b and c are fitted across the other states: ln(official) on ln(BEV) and ln(open sites + 1);
- 90% predictive intervals come from a coefficient bootstrap plus multinomial allocation.

**Validation on official district counts.** Chhattisgarh is the only state with official district counts (PIB 2086533 annex). Over its <!--n:cg_districts-->28<!--/n--> districts:

| Allocation method | WAPE vs official district counts | Spearman |
|---|---|---|
| Share of open-data sites | <!--n:cg_wape_open_share-->0.99<!--/n--> | <!--n:cg_spearman_open_share-->0.46<!--/n--> |
| 50:50 blend | <!--n:cg_wape_blend_50_50-->0.70<!--/n--> | <!--n:cg_spearman_blend_50_50-->0.77<!--/n--> |
| Share of BEV registrations | <!--n:cg_wape_bev_share-->0.54<!--/n--> | <!--n:cg_spearman_bev_share-->0.85<!--/n--> |
| **Small-area model** | **<!--n:cg_wape_small_area_model-->0.41<!--/n-->** | <!--n:cg_spearman_small_area_model-->0.84<!--/n--> |

The model's 90% predictive intervals contain the official count in <!--n:cg_ci_coverage-->82%<!--/n--> of districts. Two limits: this is one state, and the state-level leave-one-out error is still a median |ln| of <!--n:sa_loo-->0.52<!--/n-->. More official district annexes would strengthen the test.

### 2.6 Requirement estimation and recommendations (Round 2; designed, not yet built)
- **Demand by segment and charging mode:** stock (registrations with survival curves) × daily km × Wh/km × the public share of energy (vs home, depot or swap). Every parameter is sourced, with a range, and shown in a sensitivity chart.
- **Two requirement views per district and corridor:**
  - energy-based: chargers by class = public kWh per day ÷ (kW × utilisation), with a queueing check at peak;
  - norm-based: MoP 2024 clause 11(1), counted on urban 1 km grid cells and on highway segments. The 20 km and 100 km gaps come from OSM national highways.
- **Siting:** maximal-covering location (PySAL `spopt`) over candidate sites (fuel stations, parking, highway amenities, public land), with a grid-readiness flag. It must beat random, population-greedy and norm-grid placements on demand covered per added charger.
- **Scenarios to 2030:** business as usual, policy target and high, from the §2.4 forecaster.

### 2.7 Validation and transparency
- Frozen evaluators with development and held-out splits, and a results ledger for every experiment, kept or not (`results*.tsv`).
- Official counts are held out from the estimators.
- District validation on official annexes.
- A doc-drift check.
- Every dashboard figure shows its source and as-on date.

## 3. Innovation (with evidence)
1. **A coverage-aware national census:** the first capture–recapture audit of an Indian charger inventory that we know of, validated against official counts. Its failure today is itself the finding: open data shows <!--n:open_coverage_pct-->11.4%<!--/n--> of stations, and we map where.
2. **Validated small-area supply estimates:** district station counts with honest intervals, tested against official district data (WAPE <!--n:cg_wape_small_area_model-->0.41<!--/n--> vs <!--n:cg_wape_open_share-->0.99<!--/n--> for naive open-data shares).
3. **A live, browser-free VAHAN pipeline** down to RTO, month and fuel, reconciled exactly to state totals.
4. **Evidence-first planning outputs:** ranges, ranked reasons and source lineage on every district, rendered from code so the documents cannot drift.

## 5. Limitations (what a jury should challenge)
- Registrations ≠ vehicles on the road.
- Official counts are as of 2024–25 (we align dates where we can), and the sources differ in definitions.
- Open data is sparse and urban-biased.
- Capture–recapture fails validation today.
- District supply rests on a model tested in one state.
- The RTO → district crosswalk is ours, by name and district headquarters; it needs an official check.
- Boundaries are LGD-aligned but not Survey of India–certified.
