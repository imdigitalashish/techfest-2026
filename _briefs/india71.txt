                           Techfest 2026-27

                The India @ 71/100 Challenge


Objective:
India has made remarkable progress across education, healthcare, financial
inclusion, and social welfare over the past decade. According to the NITI Aayog
SDG India Index 2023–24, the country now scores 71 out of 100, reflecting
real, measurable gains.

But national averages often hide what's happening on the ground. Good policies
exist; the gap lies in how well they reach the people who need them most. The
India @ 71/100 Challenge invites students to close exactly this gap: identify
where implementation is falling short, and design practical, scalable solutions that
strengthen what already exists rather than reinventing it. Bring your ideas to life at
Techfest, IIT Bombay, Asia's largest science and technology festivals.​
​
The India @ 71/100 Challenge is a national innovation competition where
participants identify a real implementation challenge within one of the three
competition themes and propose a practical, evidence-based solution. Using
research, credible data, and ground-level understanding, teams will develop
solutions that are innovative, feasible, and scalable. ​
​
Challenge question​
Identify one measurable gap between India's development scorecard and the lived
reality of an underserved group (including socially marginalised groups) within one
of the three competition themes, and design a solution that could be realistically
implemented and scaled in the real world.

The challenge spans three themes; choose one and build a solution within it. ​


Theme 1: Maternal & Early Childhood Nutrition

Overview

India runs one of the world's largest maternal and child nutrition data ecosystems -
Anganwadis, ASHA workers, and Poshan Abhiyaan/Mission Poshan 2.0 - yet
undernutrition remains one of the country's most persistent public health
challenges. The ICDS-CAS/Poshan Tracker application already digitally tracks
growth-monitoring and service-delivery indicators for close to 9 crore children. The
implementation gap is therefore rarely an absence of data; it is that this data is not
consistently governed, integrated, or acted upon in real time - it sits in siloed
dashboards, is used mostly for retrospective reporting rather than early warning,
and is not interoperable with the health system's own digital records. This theme
asks participants to treat nutrition data as critical public infrastructure and design
the data-governance and decision-support layer that policy intent alone cannot
deliver.

Problem Statement
From a data governance and policy-implementation perspective, design a
technologically robust solution that closes the gap between what Poshan
Abhiyaan/Mission Poshan 2.0 already collects and what frontline systems actually
act on. ​
Your solution must engage with at least one of the following:

1) Interoperability & data flow - how should Anganwadi (ICDS-CAS/Poshan
Tracker), ASHA/health-system, and community-level records be linked or made
interoperable so at-risk children are flagged before, not after, severe malnutrition
develops?

2) Decision rights & accountability - who should see which data, at what frequency,
and through what escalation protocol, so that a block or district administrator can
act on early-warning signals within existing service-delivery timelines?

3) Data quality & governance safeguards - how should data validation, consent, and
child-data privacy be built into the system design from the outset, consistent with
applicable data-protection and data-sharing norms?

Participants are expected to submit a concrete technical architecture - a data
pipeline, alerting or decision-support model, or platform design - and not a policy
recommendation alone.

   ●​ Poshan Tracker / ICDS-CAS dashboards, Ministry of Women & Child
      Development;
   ●​ National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), 2019-21, Ministry of Health &
      Family Welfare;
   ●​ Mission Poshan 2.0 guidelines, Ministry of Women & Child Development;

   ●​ Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 - for consent and data-governance

Theme 2: Foundational Learning Outcomes
Overview
India has achieved near-universal school enrolment, yet ASER 2024 and the
government's own PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan (2024) confirm that a majority of
children are not acquiring foundational reading and numeracy skills at their
expected grade level. The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 and the NIPUN
Bharat Mission for Foundational Literacy and Numeracy already set clear policy
targets, and India runs one of the world's largest school data systems through
UDISE+. The implementation gap is less about missing data than about how
policy-mandated assessment and attendance data is governed, aggregated, and
converted into classroom-level and system-level decisions. This theme asks
participants to close the gap between what schools already record and what
actually changes for a struggling learner.

Problem Statement
From a data governance and policy-implementation perspective, design a
technologically robust intervention that converts India's existing school data
ecosystem (UDISE+, ASER, PARAKH, state assessment systems) into a
decision-support system aligned with the NIPUN Bharat/FLN policy mandate.

Your solution must engage with at least one of the following:

1) Data governance & standardisation - how should attendance, assessment, and
learning-level data be standardised and made interoperable across UDISE+, state
assessment platforms, and classroom-level tools so a teacher, block official, and
state administrator see a consistent picture?

2) Decision-support at point of use - how can existing data be surfaced as an
actionable, low-bandwidth tool for teachers to identify and remediate specific
learning gaps, rather than remaining a compliance report?

3) Policy-monitoring feedback loops - how should PARAKH/ASER-style diagnostic
data feed back into resource allocation, teacher training, or School Management
Committee decisions at the block or district level?

Participants are expected to submit a concrete technical architecture - a data
pipeline, diagnostic or analytics engine, or decision-support platform - grounded in
these existing datasets, and not a generic ed-tech pitch.

Suggested Data Sources & References:

   ●​ ASER 2024, Annual Status of Education Report, ASER Centre/Pratham
   ●​ PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024, Ministry of Education / PARAKH
      (Performance Assessment, Review and Analysis of Knowledge for Holistic
      Development)
   ●​ UDISE+ (Unified District Information System for Education Plus), Ministry of
      Education
   ●​ National Education Policy (NEP) 2020
   ●​ NIPUN Bharat Mission for Foundational Literacy and Numeracy, Ministry of
      Education, 2021
   ●​ DIKSHA - National Digital Infrastructure for Teachers

Theme 3: Financial Inclusion & Meaningful Banking Access
Overview
India has dramatically expanded formal banking access through the Pradhan Mantri
Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY), bringing over 56 crore people into the financial system,
and the RBI's Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index) confirms steady national
progress. Yet roughly a quarter of these accounts remain inactive, and usage of
digital payments, credit, and DBT-linked benefits remains uneven across gender,
geography, and literacy levels. The policy and data infrastructure for inclusion
already exists - PMJDY, the DBT Mission, Aadhaar-enabled payments, and UPI - but
converting formal access into meaningful, sustained usage requires better
governance of the data these systems already generate. This theme is not about
building new fintech products; it is about using the transaction, DBT, and
account-status data already flowing through India's Digital Public Infrastructure to
close the access-to-usage gap.

Problem Statement
From a data governance and policy-implementation perspective, design a
technologically robust solution that uses existing financial-inclusion data - PMJDY
account and transaction status, DBT Mission records, RBI FI-Index indicators - to
move beneficiaries from merely holding a formal account to actively and
meaningfully using financial services.

Your solution must engage with at least one of the following:

1)Data-driven targeting & governance - how should dormancy, DBT-linkage, and
usage data be governed and shared, within applicable RBI/NPCI/DBT Mission
data-protection norms, to identify and prioritise specific inactive-account segments
for intervention?

2) Interoperable last-mile infrastructure - how can Aadhaar-enabled payment
systems, UPI, and Business Correspondent networks be better integrated with
existing account data to reduce friction for low-literacy or rural users?

3) Policy feedback & accountability - how should account-activity data feed back
into the National Strategy for Financial Inclusion targets so implementers can be
held accountable for usage, not just enrollment?

Participants are expected to submit a concrete technical architecture - a data-driven
targeting model, interoperable payment/access layer, or governance dashboard -
and not a new financial product concept alone.

Suggested Data Sources & References:

   ●​ Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) dashboards, Department of
      Financial Services, Ministry of Finance
   ●​ RBI Financial Inclusion Index (FI-Index), Reserve Bank of India
   ●​ National Strategy for Financial Inclusion, Reserve Bank of India
   ●​ Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Mission dashboards, DBT Bharat, Cabinet
      Secretariat
   ●​ National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) - UPI/AePS usage data
   ●​ Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 - for consent and data-sharing
      safeguards in financial data use


Objectives & Deliverables

Participants are expected to:

   ●​ Identify a Measurable Implementation Gap: Select a specific problem
      within one of the three competition themes where India's development
      indicators differ from the lived experiences of an underserved community.
   ●​ Analyze the Existing Ecosystem: Study current government schemes,
      public infrastructure, available datasets, or community initiatives addressing
      the problem, and identify why the implementation gap continues to persist.
   ●​ Develop an Innovative Solution: Design a practical intervention that
      strengthens existing systems through technology, process improvements,
      behavioural interventions, community participation, policy innovation, or
      other suitable approaches.
   ●​ Demonstrate Feasibility: Explain how the proposed solution can be
      realistically implemented, considering stakeholders, resources, operational
      constraints, and long-term sustainability.
   ●​ Estimate Social Impact: Clearly define the intended beneficiaries, expected
      outcomes, measurable impact, and the potential for scaling the solution
      beyond its initial implementation.

Competition Format

Round 1: Concept Proposal Submission

This is the preliminary screening round where teams present their understanding of
the problem and their proposed solution. The objective is to evaluate the
significance of the identified implementation gap, the novelty of the proposed
approach, and its overall feasibility.

The proposal should be concise and include:

   ●​ Team Information: Team Name, Team ID, Institution, and Contact Details.
   ●​ Problem Identification: The implementation gap selected within the
      chosen theme and the underserved community it affects.
   ●​ Evidence & Context: Supporting data, reports, or research demonstrating
      the significance of the problem.
   ●​ Existing Ecosystem: Brief analysis of current schemes, initiatives, or
      solutions and the remaining implementation gap.
   ●​ Proposed Solution: A high-level description of the solution, explaining how
      it addresses the identified gap.
   ●​ Expected Impact: Intended beneficiaries and anticipated social impact.

The complete proposal should not exceed 6 pages (A4, 1-inch margins, Arial font,
minimum 11pt, single PDF file)

Submission: All Round 1 proposals must be submitted via the official Round 1
Google Form (link to be shared with registered teams). The form will require you to
upload your documentation PDF. Ensure your PDF is named India71_100_[Team
ID].pdf. Submissions via email will not be accepted.

Shortlist: Top 10 teams per theme (30 total) advance to Round 2.


Round 2: Final Presentation (Techfest, IIT Bombay)

Shortlisted teams will present a refined version of their proposal before an expert
jury followed by a Q & A during Techfest, IIT Bombay.

The presentation should include:

   ●​   Problem validation and stakeholder understanding
   ●​   Existing ecosystem analysis
   ●​   Detailed solution
   ●​   Implementation roadmap
   ●​   Feasibility analysis
   ●​   Scalability
   ●​   Impact assessment

Each team will receive 10 minutes for the presentation followed by a 5-minute
jury interaction. Winners are determined by jury scores. Decisions of organizers and
judges are final and binding.

Evaluation, Prizes, and Recognition

Evaluation Criteria
Proposals and presentations are evaluated on the following, drawn directly from what
teams are asked to submit across both rounds:

   ●​ Problem Identification: How clearly does the team define the
      implementation gap and the specific underserved community it affects?
   ●​ Evidence & Context: How well is the problem supported by credible data,
      reports, or research demonstrating its significance and scale?
   ●​ Existing Ecosystem Analysis: Has the team studied current government
      schemes, infrastructure, or community initiatives already addressing this
      problem, and clearly explained why the gap persists despite them?
   ●​ Proposed Solution: How well does the solution address the identified gap?
      Is the approach, whether technology, policy, process, or community-based,
      clearly explained?
   ●​ Feasibility & Implementation Roadmap: How realistic is the plan for
      implementation, considering stakeholders, resources, and operational
      constraints?
   ●​ Impact & Scalability: How clearly are the intended beneficiaries and
      expected outcomes defined, and how credible is the potential to scale the
      solution beyond its initial pilot?
