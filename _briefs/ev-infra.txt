          EV Infra & Green Mobility AI Challenge

Objective
India’s automotive sector is undergoing a major transformation, driven by the
adoption of electric mobility, the expansion of charging infrastructure, advances in
vehicle technologies, and an increasing focus on environmentally sustainable
manufacturing. National initiatives such as FAME and PM E-DRIVE have supported
this transition, creating a need for robust, India-specific digital tools and datasets
that can strengthen infrastructure planning and sustainability assessment.

Two critical information gaps currently constrain evidence-based decision-making.
First, EV charging infrastructure information remains fragmented across multiple
platforms and networks, making it difficult to assess national coverage, identify
underserved regions, and align future deployment with actual vehicle populations.
Second, automotive Life Cycle Assessment studies largely rely on international
datasets that may not adequately reflect India’s electricity mix, materials,
manufacturing processes, supply chains, logistics systems, vehicle-use patterns,
and recycling ecosystem.​
​
Government Institutions, including the Ministry of Heavy Industries and its CPSEs /
ABs/ Test agencies under it such as International Centre for Automotive Technology
(ICAT), require scalable and transparent analytical solutions to support EV
infrastructure planning, automotive sustainability assessment, policy formulation,
technology evaluation, and industry engagement.

Through this innovation challenge, student teams are invited to develop practical,
data-driven solutions under either of the following two challenge tracks.
Challenge Question
Participants may choose to participate in any one of the following two challenges.

Challenge 1: EV Infra Map India
Mapping and Integration of Nationwide EV Charger Installations Based on Vehicle
Population Using VAHAN Data
India's charging infrastructure is expanding through the efforts of government
agencies, public-sector entities, charge point operators, utilities, and private
companies. However, information on installed chargers is distributed across multiple
applications and databases. This fragmentation limits the ability to develop a unified
view of charging availability and assess whether infrastructure deployment is
aligned with EV adoption across regions.

Objective
Develop a data-driven national platform that:

   ●​ Integrates and maps existing public EV charging infrastructure across India.
   ●​ Uses VAHAN vehicle registration data to assess the relationship between EV
      population and charging availability.
   ●​ Identifies underserved areas and charging infrastructure gaps.
   ●​ Estimates future charging infrastructure requirements across different
      regions.
   ●​ Supports evidence-based planning by MHI, ICAT, state governments, and
      other relevant stakeholders.

Expected Deliverables
Teams selecting this challenge should submit:

   ●​ Functional prototype or web-based platform presenting a unified view of
      EV charging infrastructure.
   ●​ Interactive national GIS map showing existing chargers, infrastructure
      coverage, and identified gaps.
   ●​ Analytical methodology document explaining the approach used for
      integration, gap assessment, and estimation.

   ●​ Presentation or demonstration video showcasing the solution for at least
      one representative geography.
   ●​ Scalability roadmap outlining how the solution could be expanded into a
      nationwide planning platform.




Challenge 2: Green Auto India
Creation of an India-Specific Life Cycle Assessment Database for the Automotive
Sector
The environmental footprint of a vehicle extends beyond its operation and includes
raw material extraction, component production, vehicle manufacturing, energy
consumption, transportation, maintenance, recycling, and end-of-life treatment.
However, many Life Cycle Assessment studies currently depend on international or
global-average datasets that may not accurately represent Indian conditions.

An indigenous automotive LCA database can support more credible environmental
assessments, sustainable product development, green manufacturing, policy
evaluation, ESG reporting, and technology comparison across conventional, electric,
hybrid, and other emerging vehicle technologies.

Objective
Develop an India-specific automotive      Life   Cycle Assessment database and
assessment framework that:

   ●​ Represents key materials, components, processes, energy sources, logistics
      activities, and end-of-life pathways relevant to India.
   ●​ Enables estimation of the environmental footprint of automotive products and
      technologies.
   ●​ Supports comparisons across vehicle technologies, materials, components, or
      manufacturing choices.
   ●​ Provides a transparent and scalable foundation for sustainability assessment.
   ●​ Supports MHI, ICAT, automotive manufacturers, suppliers, researchers, and
      policymakers in evidence-based decision-making.

Teams are not expected to create an exhaustive national database during the
competition. They may demonstrate the proposed framework using a defined set of
materials, components, processes, or vehicle systems. All sources, boundaries,
assumptions, limitations, and data-quality considerations must be clearly stated.

Expected Deliverables
Teams selecting this challenge should submit:

   ●​ Prototype India-specific automotive LCA database covering a clearly
      defined scope.
   ●​ Database architecture and data-collection framework demonstrating
      how additional datasets can be incorporated.
   ●​ Environmental impact calculation engine for selected automotive
      materials, components, or vehicle technologies.
   ●​ Web-based dashboard or visualisation platform presenting lifecycle
      results and comparisons.
   ●​ Methodology document explaining system boundaries, assumptions, data
      sources, calculations, and limitations.
   ●​ Demonstration case study applying the proposed database to at least one
      automotive product, component, material, or technology.
   ●​ Scalability roadmap outlining how the solution could support wider
      adoption by ICAT, industry, researchers, and policymakers.

Suggested Solution Approach (Indicative)
Teams are free to develop their own methodology. A broad solution-development
approach may include:

   ●​ Problem Definition: Define the intended users, decision-making
      requirement, study boundary, and expected outputs.
   ●​ Data Collection: Identify, compile, and document suitable public,
      open-source, sample, or synthetic datasets.
   ●​ Data Integration: Standardise and combine datasets into a consistent
      analytical structure.
   ●​ Analysis     and     Estimation: Apply suitable statistical, geospatial,
      optimisation, LCA, or AI-based techniques.
   ●​ Validation: Test the model against available benchmarks, sample cases, or
      alternative scenarios.
   ●​ Visualisation: Present outputs through a clear, interactive, and
      non-technical user interface.
   ●​ Recommendations: Translate analytical results into actionable planning or
      sustainability insights.
   ●​ Scalability Assessment: Explain how the prototype can be expanded across
      geographies, datasets, vehicle categories, or automotive applications.
Solutions will be judged on the intelligence, transparency, and defensibility of the
approach rather than access to premium or proprietary datasets.
