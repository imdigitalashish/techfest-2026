## Likely judge questions — and answers
1. **"Low-cost sensors are inaccurate, especially for dust. Why trust them?"** We agree they're qualitative in absolute terms (CSTEP 2025). We use *differences* between identical sensors placed metres apart, which cancels the shared background and most humidity effects. We calibrate the scale against a reference co-location (US EPA targets: R² > 0.70, RMSE < 7 µg/m³ for PM2.5) and re-estimate between-node drift every quiet night. For penalties, DPCC still verifies with reference instruments; we tell them where to look.
2. **"What if the wind is calm, which is exactly when Delhi is worst?"** Then we say "inconclusive" and log it. We report the abstention rate as a metric rather than hide it. In calm conditions the site's dust accumulates near the boundary, so the ring still sees a localised excess; we're testing whether that can be attributed safely (replay harness).
3. **"A neighbouring site or a road upwind?"** It raises the upwind nodes too, so it lands in the background, not in this site's increment. If two sites face each other, both rings see it, and the dashboard shows the overlap.
4. **"Contractors will spray water on your sensors."** One node reading lower than the ring while its humidity jumps is a spray signature, and we flag it. Data is signed on the device and hash-chained; enclosures are sealed; misting is flow-metered; the site's CCTV (already mandatory) gives a cross-check.
5. **"Isn't dust less harmful than combustion smoke?"** Per microgram, the evidence points that way: the Lancet 10-city study's lead author warns against fixating on dust. We don't claim to fix Delhi's health burden alone. We fix an enforcement failure that costs workers weeks of wages, and we cut PM10 where workers breathe. The same ring flags diesel-generator and waste-burning spikes on site next.
6. **"How much will it reduce AQI?"** We won't promise a city AQI number. We'll measure the fence-line PM10 increment with and without closed-loop misting at a real site (Nov), and report water per µg avoided. Until then, any reduction figure would be a guess.
7. **"Cost?"** A 4-node ring + wind + gateway + misting controller; the BOM with vendor prices is in our prototype plan. The yardstick is CAQM's ₹5 lakh flat fine, and ₹7,500 per missing anti-smog gun.
8. **"Why would a builder install this?"** Lower fines and dispute evidence today; the right to keep working through restriction periods tomorrow (a pilot we'll propose to CAQM); and less water spent on timer-run cannons.
9. **"How is this different from Airveda or Prana Air compliance monitors?"** They stream absolute PM to the DPCC cloud. We measure the site's *own* contribution, with uncertainty, tamper flags and closed-loop suppression.
10. **"Maintenance?"** Optical sensor fans and inlets need monthly cleaning in dust. The nightly self-calibration flags a node that drifts, and the dashboard tells the supervisor which node to service.
11. **"Weather — fog, 45 °C summers, monsoon?"** Fog: humidity correction plus a fog flag (RH > 95 %); heat: radiation shield and a ventilated enclosure; monsoon: IP65 boxes and rain-aware control (no misting when it's raining).
12. **"Power and connectivity?"** Solar + battery per node, LTE Cat-1 gateway with store-and-forward; budget in the prototype plan.
13. **"Data privacy / who owns the data?"** The site owns the raw data; the regulator gets signed summaries and exceedance events. The public view shows site rankings, not personal data.
14. **"Why Delhi?"** Delhi uses blanket construction bans every winter, already mandates sensors and CCTV, and runs an AI command centre (live 1 Oct 2026). The policy hook exists; the measurement doesn't.
15. **"What's original here?"** Fence-line attribution exists for mines and refineries. We bring it to Indian construction sites at low cost, with self-calibration from idle periods, abstention, tamper signatures and a closed misting loop. We found no Indian product that does this (see landscape, "What companies ship today").
