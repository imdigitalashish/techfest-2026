**Numbers box (slide 7)** — real data: `eval/results/forecast_walkforward.json` (CPCB/DPCC data, 4 Delhi monitors, monthly expanding window, test Nov 2025–Sep 2026). Yesterday's value predicts tomorrow's daily-mean PM2.5 within **23.8 µg/m³** on average (r 0.91). Gradient boosting with ERA5 weather improves this by at most 4 % overall and does worse in winter; the global CAMS model at one city point is off by 53.6 µg/m³. *Message: with the history we have, city-level next-day forecasting gives us little; the state (IITM) already runs it. The missing piece is site-level attribution.*

**Attribution replay (slide 7, second panel; on screen: "real Delhi background + wind, simulated site and sensors").** Lead with the *wind-resolved ring and honest abstention*, not the self-calibration percentage. On held-out 2026 hours across 5 simulated sites, a fixed upwind/downwind pair is off by 97.8 ± 15.6 µg/m³; a wind-resolved ring, by 58.0 ± 16.0; adding idle-hour self-calibration, by 33.8 ± 2.6. **The self-calibration gain is a hypothesis**: an independent review found our simulator favours it (quiet hours guaranteed clean, one gain for ambient and dust, no persistent spatial gradients). Stress tests (evaluator v2) and a controlled dust test come before we claim it.

## Likely judge questions — and answers
0. **"AQI is the most crowded theme here. Why you?"** (30 s) "Most teams will measure city AQI or predict tomorrow. Delhi already has 47 reference stations, a state forecasting system, and vendors streaming PM to the DPCC cloud. We target the missing decision: which construction site actually added the dust? DustLedger combines a wind-resolved fence-line ring, honest 'inconclusive' calls and attributed suppression, so regulators can inspect causes instead of stopping everyone. Our attribution evidence is simulated today, but it's a Delhi-specific, testable route from sensing to fairer enforcement — and we'll show it working on a real dust source by December."
1. **"What have you actually built?"** Today the real pieces are the Delhi data pipeline (4 CPCB/DPCC monitors + ERA5) and a forecast benchmark on a time-ordered split. Site attribution runs in a replay where the background and wind are real but the site, sensors and plume are simulated. Misting control and tamper detection are designs. This is feasibility evidence, not field validation; the hardware ring and a controlled dust trial come in November.
2. **"Low-cost sensors are inaccurate, especially for dust. Why trust them?"** In absolute terms they're qualitative for construction PM10 (CSTEP 2025), and we agree. Differencing identical sensors removes only *common-mode* error; it doesn't guarantee they respond equally to coarse dust. So a controlled dust test and a reference co-location are mandatory, and until then the output is a triage signal for inspectors, not a legal measurement. For penalties, DPCC verifies with reference instruments.
3. **"What happens in Delhi's calmest, dirtiest hours?"** The current method can't safely attribute in calm winds, so it says "inconclusive", and inconclusive never means clean. We'll disclose how often that happens by season. Improving calm-wind handling (e.g. against a pre-work background) is on our research backlog, not a claim.
4. **"A neighbouring site or a road?"** A neighbour's plume can hit just one side of the ring, so it doesn't always land in the "background". When the wind signature and the node pattern disagree, the system calls it inconclusive. Rings on neighbouring sites, or an inspector, resolve it.
5. **"Isn't this just Aeroqual or Envirosuite, cheaper?"** Fence-line attribution is established for mines and refineries. Our claim is narrower: a low-cost workflow for Indian construction sites combining wind-resolved attribution, abstention, tamper evidence and control, which we didn't find in the Indian product pages we reviewed. That doesn't mean no one, anywhere, has built it.
6. **"Contractors will spray your sensors."** One node reading lower than the ring while its humidity jumps is a spray signature we can flag. Records are signed on the device and hash-chained, so edits become detectable (with keys in secure storage and the chain anchored off-site). Sealed enclosures, flow-metered misting and the site's mandatory CCTV are cross-checks. None of this is tested yet.
7. **"Isn't dust less harmful than combustion smoke?"** Per microgram, the evidence points that way: the Lancet 10-city study's lead author warns against fixating on dust. We aren't claiming to fix Delhi's health burden. We target an enforcement failure that costs workers weeks of wages, plus PM10 at the fence line where workers and neighbours breathe. A PM-only node can't identify diesel or waste-burning smoke; that would need added gas sensors.
8. **"How much will it reduce AQI or save in health?"** We don't know yet, and we won't extrapolate to city AQI or deaths. Suppression effectiveness is unverified in our research so far. In November we'll run controlled misting on/off and report fence-line PM10 and litres used.
9. **"Cost, and why would a builder pay without an exemption?"** The BOM is being priced from vendor listings; servicing and calibration costs aren't known yet, so we won't quote a total today. The near-term buyer case is evidence in disputes and fewer blind inspections. Permission to keep working during restrictions is a pilot we'll propose to CAQM, not an assumption.
10. **"Will it survive dust, fog, heat and outages?"** Not tested yet. A dust/fog bench test, a datasheet power budget, fouling detection (one node drifting from the ring) and fail-safe behaviour (no data → misting off, inconclusive flag) come first. Cleaning intervals will come from those tests.
11. **"Calibration standard?"** The only verified sensor target we have is US EPA's for PM2.5 (R² > 0.70, RMSE < 7 µg/m³). It isn't a PM10 construction-dust criterion; we're sourcing PM10 targets and CPCB guidance.
12. **"Power and connectivity?"** Solar + battery per node, LTE Cat-1 gateway with store-and-forward. Sizing comes from datasheet currents and Delhi winter irradiance, both still being sourced.
13. **"Data privacy / ownership?"** The site owns the raw data; the regulator receives signed summaries and exceedance events; the public sees site rankings, not personal data.
14. **"Why Delhi?"** Delhi uses blanket construction restrictions every winter (Nov 1–Jan 31 restrictions, Dec 10–Jan 20 total ban in the 2026-27 plan), already mandates CCTV, and runs an AI-enabled command centre with 334 enforcement teams. The policy hooks exist; the site-level measurement doesn't.
15. **"Will it work in other cities?"** The differential method needs no city-specific forecast model, but siting, local aerosol, climate and calibration have to be checked city by city. The hardware and software stay the same.
