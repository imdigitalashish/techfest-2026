## Insights for our design and pitch

- **The government is already moving toward our exact framing.** Delhi's Environment Minister (via IIT Kanpur MoU, Dec 2025) explicitly wants to replace "reactive and seasonal approaches" with source-apportionment-driven "targeted and timely interventions" (lead #8). This both validates the hypothesis and raises a sharp judge objection: *"Isn't the government already building this?"* Our answer: the IIT Kanpur system is city-scale/top-down source apportionment (what fraction of city PM is C&D vs vehicles vs biomass); it is not described anywhere we found as doing **per-site, fence-line, real-time attribution** that can name an individual non-compliant site and trigger automated, targeted suppression there. That gap — city-average attribution vs. site-level accountability — is our wedge.
- **The sprinkler/anti-smog-gun "gaming" scandal (leads #1–#3) is a live, credible, multi-sourced controversy** (Newslaundry, Indian Express, cross-examined by CPCB denial, under Supreme Court scrutiny per secondary reporting). It is also **politically charged** (AAP vs BJP blame-trading) — our pitch should present the underlying measurement-integrity problem (point sensors near a diffuse pollutant are trivially gameable by anyone, not just the government) as a *technical* argument for distributed, harder-to-game upwind/downwind fence-line networks, while explicitly avoiding partisan framing.
- **A real confound we must design against:** Indian Express's own reporting notes the observed PM decline during "gaming" episodes is confounded with the normal midday PM2.5 diurnal drop from thermal mixing (lead #2). This is a direct engineering requirement for our product: distinguishing genuine suppression/dilution of a monitored plume from ordinary meteorological variation requires simultaneous upwind/downwind differential measurement (exactly what the working hypothesis proposes) rather than a single absolute reading — we can use this news item as a concrete "why naive single-point monitoring fails" case study in the pitch.
- **Blanket GRAP construction bans have severe, poorly-mitigated human costs** (leads #4, #5): hundreds of thousands of daily-wage workers go unpaid for 2+ weeks at a stretch every winter; compensation schemes exist on paper (Rs 8,000 in 2024 → Rs 10,000 in 2025) but are gated on "registration" that in practice covers under ~7% of the workforce even after a Supreme Court order. This is strong, judge-facing, quantified evidence for the "targeted enforcement protects livelihoods" pitch angle — attributing pollution to specific non-compliant sites would let compliant sites keep operating (and keep paying workers) during GRAP stages.
- **CSE/Anumita Roychowdhury's institutional position (lead #6) functionally endorses our anti-"blanket emergency measure" framing**, even though her direct quote is about monsoon washout, not construction bans — cite carefully as analogous reasoning, not a literal GRAP quote (I was careful not to over-claim this).
- **Smog towers and (by extension) anti-smog guns face a credibility problem among officials themselves** (lead #9: DPCC's own NGT affidavit calls them ineffective). Pitch should avoid proposing "more misting/spraying" as the core suppression mechanism without strong caveats, and should foreground our proposal's "mist only when evidence attributes the exceedance to this site" trigger logic as explicitly different from indiscriminate anti-smog-gun/sprinkler deployment, which has become symbolically tainted (gaming scandal) and technically discredited (smog towers).
- **Forecasting is already fairly good at "poor/very poor" (80%+) but weak at the most severe days** (36% hit rate on AQI>400 in the best year so far, lead #7) and DSS is winter-only — there is a legitimate, evidenced gap for a complementary, more localized/ more-continuous AI forecasting layer, but we should not claim to "solve" forecasting that IITM/IMD already does reasonably — position our AI as hyperlocal dispersion modeling for a specific site's plume, not a city-wide AQI predictor competing with AQEWS.
- **Google Project Green Light (lead #11)** is a useful cost-model contrast (software-only, uses existing signal infrastructure, cheap/fast) but has no confirmed Delhi presence and is 2023-vintage — cite it only as an analogy for "AI leveraging existing infra without new hardware," not as evidence of adoption in NCR.
- **Official narrative risk:** the Minister's Jan 2026 "cleanest air in 8 years" statement (lead #10) means judges may already believe Delhi's AQI is "being solved" — our pitch needs to explain why city-average PM decline doesn't tell you where blame-worthy non-compliant point sources still are, i.e., averages can mask persistent local-site non-compliance even as city-wide measures (more sweeping, more sprinkling, fewer trucks) work in aggregate.
