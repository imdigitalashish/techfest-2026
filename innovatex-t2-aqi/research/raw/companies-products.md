## 2) What companies ship today, by category

### Sensor networks
Clarity/Breathe London is the strongest reference design: low-cost nodes are not trusted raw; they become useful through network-level calibration. Breathe London explicitly uses one-minute data, relative calibration to “gold” sensors, absolute calibration to references, weekly updates, and later ratification. Google Air Quality API ships gridded real-time/history/forecast data globally (“over 100 countries”, “500 x 500 meters”). Kunak ships standards-oriented stations. Airveda and Prana Air ship India-facing monitors and explicitly market DPCC construction compliance integrations.

### Construction / fence-line dust
Aeroqual and Envirosuite are the clearest global fence-line/industrial dust patterns: robust PM instruments, alerts, source pinpointing, reports, and operational schedule changes. In India, Airveda and Prana Air are selling to the DPCC/CAQM compliance need; opened vendor text confirms DPCC cloud integration and PM2.5/PM10 monitoring, but not regulator-approved vendor lists or technical QA.

### Forecasting and decision support
Google Air Quality API ships 96-hour forecasts. Microsoft Aurora is newer and more technically ambitious: a 2025 foundation model with an air-pollution prediction specialization, but not a turnkey municipal product. IBM Green Horizons is an important precedent for source-aware policy simulation, but the opened primary source is from 2015, so current shipping status is stale.

### Traffic
Google Project Green Light is the strongest current shipped traffic intervention. It avoids roadside sensors, using Maps driving trends and AI recommendations for city signal engineers. It names Kolkata and claims “up to 30% reduction in stops” and “10% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions”. This is a direct competitor/borrowable pattern if the Techfest idea pivots toward traffic.

### Drones / mobile mapping
Aclima/Google Air View and Sniffer4D represent mobile sensing. Air View is well documented: Street View cars carry Aclima scientific analyzers and low-cost sensor arrays. Sniffer4D remains an unverified lead because official pages were not opened. Drone monitoring appears more useful for inspections/hotspot discovery than continuous enforcement.

## 3) Where we can borrow or beat them

- **Borrow Breathe London’s calibration architecture**: upwind/downwind fence-line nodes should not be presented as raw truth; design “gold” co-location, weekly model updates, and delayed ratified recalibration.
- **Borrow Envirosuite/Aeroqual operations language**: the buyer wants alerts, source pinpointing, compliance reports, and schedule recommendations, not only AQI charts.
- **Borrow Google Green Light’s deployment posture**: recommend low-friction changes to existing infrastructure rather than asking cities to install heavy new hardware everywhere.
- **Beat India compliance vendors** by attributing *incremental site contribution* using wind + upwind/downwind differencing, not just streaming PM2.5/PM10 to DPCC cloud.
- **Beat mist cannons** by making suppression conditional: mist only when a site-attributed plume is detected/forecast, saving water and avoiding performative spraying.
- **Beat API-only products** by tying forecasts to a physical actuator and a regulator-facing evidence trail.

## 4) Gaps nobody fills (with evidence)

1. **Regulatory C&D attribution gap** — opened India vendor pages show compliance monitoring (“PM2.5/PM10 monitoring”, “DPCC cloud”), but none show wind-resolved upwind/downwind attribution to isolate a site’s own contribution.
2. **Closed-loop water efficiency gap** — no opened source showed an Indian product that triggers misting only when site-attributed dust exceeds a threshold.
3. **Calibration transparency gap** — Breathe London documents calibration in detail; Indian compliance vendors opened here do not expose comparable QA/calibration methodology.
4. **Outcome-evidence gap** — Google Green Light and IBM publish quantified outcomes; most sensor vendors opened here publish product claims/testimonials, not independently evaluated reductions in PM exposure.
5. **Construction-specific forecasting gap** — Google/Microsoft forecast ambient AQ, Envirosuite predicts industrial risk, but no opened Indian source combined background AQ forecast + microscale fence-line plume attribution for construction sites.
