## 5. Health Impacts

### 5.1 Lancet Planetary Health 10-city mortality study (2024)
- **URL:** https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/air-pollution-exposure-leading-to-deaths-even-when-meeting-indian-normsstudy-101720061627986.html
- **Publisher/date:** Hindustan Times, 4 July 2024, reporting the Lancet Planetary Health study (lead authors Bhargav Krishna, Jeroen de Bont)
- **Maturity:** High-trust secondary (direct verbatim quotes from the published paper and lead authors)
- **Quote (verbatim from paper, per HT):** "7·2% of all deaths were attributable to PM2·5 concentrations higher than the WHO recommended 15 μg/m³, corresponding to 33627 annual deaths across our ten cities... Delhi had the largest attributable fraction"
- **Quote:** "Delhi recorded the highest deaths attributable to air pollution across all cities studied – 11.5% of all deaths – amounting to 12,000 each year"
- **Quote:** "Each 10 micrograms per cubic metre increase in short-term PM2.5 exposure was associated with a 1.42% increase in daily deaths" (nationally; isolating local combustion sources nearly doubled this to 3.57%)
- **Quote (lead author Bhargav Krishna):** "we cannot remain too fixated on curbing dust. Local combustion sources such as vehicles, dg sets, waste burning need to be curbed to see improvements in health impacts"
- **Quote:** "Nearly 170,000 children under the age of 5 years are estimated to have died in India due to air pollution in 2021, according to the State of Global Air report released in June [2024]"
