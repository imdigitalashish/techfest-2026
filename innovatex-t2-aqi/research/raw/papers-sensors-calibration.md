2. **Di Antonio, A., Popoola, O.A.M., Ouyang, B., Saffell, J., Jones, R.L. (2018). "Developing a Relative Humidity Correction for Low-Cost Sensors Measuring Ambient Particulate Matter." Sensors, 18(9), 2790.**
   - URL opened: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6164928/ — publisher: MDPI (via PubMed Central) — date: 2018 — accessed 2026-10-04 — maturity: peer-reviewed paper only.
   - Quote: "The application of the correction algorithm, which assumed physically reasonable κ values, resulted in a significant improvement, with the overestimation of PM measurements reduced from a factor of ~5 before correction to 1.05 after correction."
   - Quote (describing the prior/critiqued approach, Crilley et al. style scalar κ-Köhler correction): "Their approach ... is in fact unphysical in that the application of a correction factor to the derived PM values is implicitly ... equivalent to a uniform reduction in particle number concentration throughout the whole particle size spectrum." — i.e., this paper proposes a size-distribution-resolved κ-Köhler correction as an improvement over simpler scalar RH corrections, tested on an Alphasense OPC-N2 vs a Palas Fidas 200S reference over 7 days in May 2017.
   - **Implication for us:** naive RH-scalar correction can be badly wrong; if we build an RH correction we should correct at the size-bin/count level (if the sensor exposes bin counts, e.g. Alphasense OPC-N3 or Sensirion SPS30 which report size bins) rather than on PM mass alone.

### Implication for our calibration component (section b synthesis):
- The published consensus approach (Barkjohn-style) is: correct PM2.5 as a **linear function of raw PM mass + RH term**, fit against FRM/FEM reference via collocation, nationwide/region-wide generalizable slope+intercept. For India we have NO equivalent published nationwide correction — this is a gap we could plausibly fill with our own project if we can get even limited reference co-location (e.g., against a CPCB CAAQMS station).
- For humidity correction specifically, a **physically-grounded, size-distribution-based κ-Köhler correction** (Di Antonio et al. 2018) outperforms a naive scalar RH correction, per their reported ~5x→1.05x overestimation-factor improvement (quoted above). If our chosen sensor (e.g., Sensirion SPS30/SEN5x or Plantower with raw bin counts) exposes particle-count bins, we should implement correction at that level, not on PM mass output alone.

## d. Upwind/downwind, fence-line, and wind-conditioned attribution methods

1. **US EPA fenceline monitoring regulation — 40 CFR § 63.658 (NESHAP Subpart CC, petroleum refineries), implementing EPA Methods 325A/325B.**
   - URL opened: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/63.658 — publisher: Cornell Law School LII, mirroring the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations — accessed 2026-10-04 — maturity: **regulation in force** (US federal rule).
   - Quote: "The owner or operator shall conduct sampling along the facility property boundary and analyze the samples in accordance with Methods 325A and 325B of appendix A of this part."
   - Quote: "The target analyte is benzene."
   - This confirms EPA's real fenceline practice is a **perimeter/property-boundary network of passive samplers** (not strictly "one upwind + one downwind" point pair), analyzed biweekly, with site-specific sampler placement rules tied to known internal VOC sources — i.e., fenceline ≠ simple 2-point upwind/downwind, but a perimeter ring enabling after-the-fact wind-sector attribution. This is a PM10/C&D-dust analogy point: a single upwind/single downwind low-cost PM pair is a simplification relative to EPA's own practice; we should consider whether 3-4 fenceline nodes around a construction site perimeter (not just 2) would better match regulatory-grade practice and be more defensible.
   - **Not independently confirmed from EPA's own PDF**: Method 325A's full technical deployment-density/spacing rules — the PDF (https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2019-08/documents/method_325a.pdf) was fetched but returned raw unparsed PDF binary stream content (tool could not render this specific PDF as text/markdown); flagged for follow-up with a different PDF reader.

## Implications for our calibration & attribution components (overall synthesis)

1. **Benchmark to beat/cite:** US EPA's Feb-2021 NSIM PM2.5 target values (R²>0.70, slope 1.0±0.35, intercept ±5 µg/m³, RMSE<7 µg/m³, SD<5 µg/m³) are real, numbers-verified, and directly quotable (nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1012RCE.txt) — use these as our prototype's explicit calibration QA/QC target, which also demonstrates "technical feasibility" rigor to judges.
2. **Humidity correction:** do NOT use a naive scalar RH-correction on PM mass; the verified Di Antonio et al. 2018 result (PMC6164928) shows a size-distribution-resolved κ-Köhler correction reduced an overestimation factor from ~5x to 1.05x, vs. a cruder scalar approach. Pick a sensor exposing raw bin counts (Sensirion SPS30/SEN5x/SEN6x do; Plantower partially does) to enable this.
3. **India-specific LCS/dust evidence is thin but real:** CSTEP's 2025 Indi-SET construction-dust guidance (Bengaluru) is the single most directly on-point India source found and verified this session — cite it as evidence our concept matches emerging domestic practice, and prioritize retrieving its full PDF (not accessible this session) for sensor-model-specific numbers.
4. **Fence-line design:** EPA's actual regulatory fenceline practice (40 CFR 63.658, verified) is a perimeter ring, not a single upwind/downwind pair — consider a minimum 3-4 node ring per construction site for defensibility, paired with openair-style CPF/polar-plot wind-conditioned attribution (tool identified but not yet directly verified/read this session).
5. **Public dataset:** AirDelhi (IIT Delhi, bus-mounted, NeurIPS 2023) is a real, citable, Delhi-NCR-relevant open dataset we can use for background-PM modeling/forecasting work, though its licence/size/landing page could not be confirmed this session (host domain unreachable) — retry needed.
