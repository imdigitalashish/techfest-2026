## Claim 4 — Latest CGWB Dynamic Ground Water Resources assessment for NCT Delhi

**Status:** CONFIRMED-PRIMARY.

**Opened primary source:** Central Ground Water Board, Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India, publication page `https://cgwb.gov.in/cgwbpnm/publication-detail/1741`, access date 2026-10-04. **Exact quote (publication page):** “National Compilation on DYNAMIC GROUND WATER RESOURCES OF INDIA, 2025” and “2025”. Download opened from `https://cgwb.gov.in/cgwbpnm/download/1741`.

**Exact quote (PDF page 154 of file; printed page 128; section 7.32 DELHI):** “The groundwater resources assessment of the UT has been carried out tehsil-wise. The total annual groundwater recharge of the UT has been assessed at 0.38 bcm, and the annual extractable groundwater resource at 0.35 bcm. The total current annual groundwater extraction has been assessed at 0.32 bcm, and the stage of groundwater extraction of the UT is estimated at 92.10%. Out of 34 assessment units (tehsils), 10 units (29.41 %) have been categorized as ‘Over-exploited’, 11 units (32.35 %) as ‘Critical’, 6 units (17.65 %) as ‘Semi-critical’, and 7 units (20.59 %) as ‘Safe’ categories of assessment units. Similarly, out of 1487.62 sq km recharge-worthy area of the UT, 337.44sq km (22.68 %) area are under ‘Over-Exploited’, 466.53 sq km (31.36 %) under ‘Critical’, 295.04 sq km (19.83 %) under ‘Semi-critical’, 388.60 sq km (26.12 %) under ‘Safe’ categories. ... As compared to 2024 assessment, the total Annual Ground Water Recharge and Annual Extractable Ground Water Resources has increased marginally from 379.89 mcm to 383.04 mcm and from 341.99 mcm to 345.57 mcm respectively. There is a minute decrease in the Annual Ground Water Extraction of the UT from 344.54 mcm to 318.28 mcm and the Stage of Ground Water Extraction has decreased from 100.77 % to 92.10 %.”

**Recommended slide wording:** “CGWB’s 2025 national compilation estimates Delhi’s stage of groundwater extraction at 92.10%; 10 of 34 tehsils are over-exploited, covering 337.44 sq km of Delhi’s 1,487.62 sq km recharge-worthy area.”
