## B. Findings

For generated deliverables, apply fixes to `deliverables/templates/`, then render; the generated files explicitly say "edit the template, not this file." Protocol corrections should be appended, not silently substituted: `eval/llm_gap_protocol.md` says "registered text is never edited." Severity reflects consequence for this submission, not an allegation of misconduct.

1. **major - The cost-halving headline is numerically wrong.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/templates/round1-abstract.md`, "it halves GPT-5.5 calls and the cost"; `eval/run_prob_pack.py`, `cost_call * frac_llm`. That formula assumes routed and skipped calls have the same mean cost. The committed GPT-5.5 slip calls use 17,672 output tokens, versus 6,274 for the skipped bug calls; each branch uses 15,964 input tokens. At the registered prices, retaining the slip branch costs **$5.0832/1,000**, a **30.5%** saving, not $3.6583 or 50%. For GPT-5.4-mini the corresponding saving is only **13.4%**, not 50%. `eval/run_cascade.py` makes the same full-sample-average assumption for real data. **Fix:** sum token costs over the actual retained item IDs, divide by all diagnoses, and label these as record-based estimates. Recompute both summaries and render the corrected claims. Do not assume the real-data dollar estimate is exact merely because its call-count saving is exact.

2. **major - The latency headline is neither an end-to-end measurement nor the right task comparison.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/templates/round1-abstract.md`, "answering in milliseconds rather than {{cons.gpt-5.5.latency}}"; `eval/run_prob_pack.py`, `"latency_s_per_diagnosis_cascade": r4(frac_llm * lat_med)`. The 3.1 seconds comes from repeated **real** items; the probability run's recorded LLM median is **3.5245 seconds** (`eval/results/llm_gaps_prob_diag.json`, `latency_s_median_llm_call`). Multiplying a median by the routing fraction is not the mixture median or mean. Replaying just the retained probability-call durations over all 120 diagnoses gives a **2.678-second mean**, even before engine overhead, not millisecond responses for the whole cascade. The 0.11 ms statistic is the engine's median over the 40-item consistency subset, not a universal successful-diagnosis or tutor-turn bound (`eval/results/llm_consistency.json`, `latency_ms_median`). **Fix:** report hit-path latency separately; calculate route-conditioned mean/p50/p95 for one workload, and distinguish offline replay from a live end-to-end cascade measurement.

3. **major - H8 is a construction check, not confirmatory evidence of comparative value.** **Pointer:** `eval/llm_gap_protocol.md` Addendum B, "engine coverage 0.5 by design" and "(a) holds by construction"; `eval/run_prob_pack.py`, `eng.append(x["code"] if x["answer"] == x["bug"] else None)`. Replacing known bug answers with their generating code cannot reduce accuracy; the prescribed 60 bug answers plus 60 slips force 500 calls per 1,000. Thus H8 passes regardless of the LLM's competence. The results expose this: `eval/results/llm_gaps_prob_diag.json` gives `ENGINE(abstain->NONE)` an `acc_all` of 1.0, while the GPT-5.5 cascade is 0.9833 and the GPT-4.1-mini cascade is 0.575. Neither removes any GPT-5.5/GPT-4.1-mini bug-item errors (`llm_errors_on_bug_items_removed`: 0.0). **Fix:** call this an engine-native routing/accounting demonstration, not confirmation of superiority. Add a frozen, non-constructed workload containing genuine engine misses that the LLM can recover; compare engine-only, LLM-only, cascade, and LLM-plus-verifier, with coverage treated as an outcome. Known metadata is legitimate for tutor-authored items, but does not test free-form recognition.

4. **major - Zero observed errors is being promoted to safety, aided by degenerate bootstrap intervals.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/architecture.md`, "so it is a safe second stage"; `eval/results/llm_gaps_real_negatives.json`, `fcr_N_ci` reported as `[0.0, 0.0]` for the reasoning models and engine. Resampling an all-zero vector cannot represent uncertainty about unseen errors. Even assuming independent Bernoulli cases, 0/152 permits an approximately **2.40% upper endpoint in a two-sided 95% exact binomial interval**; that calculation does not solve the question-clustering or sampling problems below. Similarly, 60/60 has a two-sided exact lower endpoint of about **94.04%**, not a population guarantee of 100%. There is also a concrete counterexample to unqualified safety in `eval/results/llm_gaps_prob_diag.json`: GPT-5.5 `false_attribution_slip` is 0.0333. **Fix:** say "0 observed false claims on these 152 selected negatives," add appropriate boundary uncertainty, and retain an abstain/clarification path. Do not describe the deployed fallback as safe on this evidence.

5. **major - Failure to find a gap is not equivalence, and "only ... fails" overstates the decision rules.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/round1-abstract.md`, "reasoning models already match an exact engine on accuracy and safety"; `research/landscape.md` section 8b, "matched ... on every test"; `architecture/adr/001-spike.md` Revision 1, "refuted for reasoning models." The registered rules test specified advantages or error thresholds, not equivalence/non-inferiority. `eval/results/llm_gaps_tracing.json` gives the GPT-5.5 paired tracer-minus-LLM difference as 0.06 with `H5_ci` `[-0.08, 0.2]`: meaningful differences in either direction remain compatible with the interval. GPT-5.4-mini actually scores 0.95 on L3 inversion (`eval/results/llm_gaps_inversion.json`, `exact_set_acc`) and 0.9667 on probability keys (`eval/results/llm_gaps_prob_pack.json`, `key_correct`), below the constructed engine's 1.0; those observations simply do not meet the registered gap thresholds. **Fix:** use the protocol's narrower conclusion: no H1-H7 gap was detected against the reasoning models on the tested samples. Describe the accuracy-advantage pitch as unsupported here, not universally refuted; predeclare an equivalence margin and suitable design before claiming equivalence.

6. **major - An executable explanation is not proof of the learner's actual misconception.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/round1-abstract.md`, "They are running a wrong rule ... and will repeat it until someone names it"; `deliverables/architecture.md`, "the system can say so with proof"; `architecture/components.md`, "One response then identifies the misconception." Uniqueness among five programmed single bugs does not exclude an unmodeled rule, an interacting set, or a coincidentally matching slip. Indeed the abstract itself says "One wrong answer can come from different misconceptions." **Fix:** distinguish a mathematical certificate, "this rule reproduces this answer," from a psychological diagnosis, "this is the rule you hold." Use provisional wording and a discriminating follow-up before confident learner labeling; keep the certificate explicitly conditional on the interpreter and candidate library.

7. **major - The evaluated classifier is not the complete fallback architecture being pitched.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/architecture.md`, "5 codes + NONE, catalogue retrieval" feeding "Diagnosis + posterior"; `eval/llm_gap_protocol.md` P_REAL, `{"code": "<LTR|SQ_DOUBLE|SUB_NEG|FRAC_ADD|NEG_TIMES_NEG|NONE>"}`. The evaluated prompt has fixed rule descriptions and a single code, not catalogue retrieval or calibrated soft evidence. `components/tutor/cli.py` constructs `SetBayesTracer(lib), BugBayesDiagnoser()`; `components/learner_model/core.py` accepts `diagnosis` but updates from `self.likelihood(item, v)` without consuming it. The CLI's persistence is real, but it is not evidence that the proposed frontier fallback updates learner beliefs end to end. **Fix:** distinguish built arithmetic CLI, standalone benchmark routing, and planned cascade service in the diagram. Specify and test how a fallback diagnosis becomes evidence for the five-code tracer, how NONE behaves, and how out-of-pack catalogue labels are handled without pretending they are executable pack states.

8. **major - "Guaranteed-correct, discriminative practice" exceeds the verified contract and probability implementation.** **Pointer:** `deliverables/round1-abstract.md`, "SymPy verifies every key"; `architecture/contracts/models.py`, `return self.well_formed and self.key_verified and self.target_fires`. Acceptance does not require `discriminative`. `components/verifier/core.py` checks an internal expression and a nonempty stem (`well_formed = bool(item.stem.strip()) and toks is not None`), not semantic agreement between arbitrary prose and that expression. Moreover, `components/bugs/probability.py` defines one `family`, `code`, `key`, and `bug` per `ProbItem`; `eval/run_prob_pack.py` declares the engine correct "by construction" rather than routing those items through the arithmetic SymPy verifier. The current probability path is not five composable switches over a common interpreter. **Fix:** scope the guarantee to the checks actually enforced on accepted arithmetic representations; enforce discrimination when promised, specify single-bug versus composite-set coverage, and add independent probability-key/stem validation before extending the guarantee to that pack. Label probability composition and tutor integration as future work, or remove them from MVP scope.

## C. Spike and ADR recommendation

**Keep the narrow engineering core; do not keep the original research-value ranking unchanged.** `architecture/adr/001-spike.md` awarded A **24/25**, including novelty **4/5**, and B **21/25** in its "Candidates" table. Its Revision 1 already says A is "re-scoped as stage 1 of an exact-first cascade and as the generator's verifier" and that B stays "for cost ... and for information-gain item choice, with no accuracy claim." That direction is defensible. The constructed engine-native test, weak real routing coverage, and uncertain tracing difference do not justify presenting those components as experimentally demonstrated superiority over frontier tutors.

I would append a Revision 2 with these changes:

- **A becomes dependable infrastructure:** owned arithmetic items, exact conditional evidence, and independently checked practice. Its claim is not recovery of the student's true mental process. Source boundary: `components/bugs/school_math.py` is the implemented arithmetic library, while `components/bugs/probability.py` describes "five problem families" rather than a shared composable interpreter.
- **B remains a bounded, falsifiable adaptive policy:** keep the five-code tracer for a useful persistent demo, but compare EIG against simpler fixed/uncertainty/mastery policies and against an LLM tutor with the same available history and practice pool. The basis is `eval/results/llm_gaps_tracing.json`, GPT-5.5 `H5_ci` `[-0.08, 0.2]`, and the ADR's own "no accuracy claim"; neither requires discarding B, but neither establishes its special learning value.
- **The new spike is learner benefit, not H8:** predeclare a feasible transfer/error-reduction study and a meaningful strong comparator. This directly addresses `_briefs/perceptron.txt`, "Show some evidence the targeted problems actually reduce that error type over time." A credible plan earns Round-1 credit; a completed pilot would strengthen the finals case, not retroactively become a Round-1 requirement.

The cascade argument is therefore **meaningful as a conditional resource optimization, but trivial as the current H8 accuracy/call-count experiment**.
