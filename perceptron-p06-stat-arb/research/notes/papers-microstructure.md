## 2) What is known about decay timescales (numbers, by market/tier)

- **On-chain cross-rollup / CEX-DEX arbitrage (2023–2024 Ethereum L2 data):** opportunities persist on average **10–20 blocks** (Arbitrum/Base/Optimism/zkSync Era block times are on the order of ~0.25–2s depending on chain, so this is roughly single-digit-to-tens-of-seconds in wall-clock terms, though the paper's unit of record is blocks, not seconds). Source: Gogol et al., arXiv:2406.02172, abstract.
- **Cross-chain arbitrage (L1↔L2, bridges) measured Sep 2023–Aug 2024:** trades using **pre-positioned inventory** settle in **~9 seconds** on average; trades requiring an actual **bridge transfer** settle in **~242 seconds**, i.e. roughly a 25x latency gap between inventory-based and bridge-based execution — this latency difference is the primary determinant of which execution mode is profitable. Source: Öz et al., arXiv:2501.17335, abstract.
- **CEX-DEX arbitrage value capture (zkSync Era SyncSwap vs Binance, Jul–Sep 2023):** cumulative extractable value (MAV) over 3 months was $104.96k, or **0.24% of trading volume** on that single pool — a magnitude benchmark for how much of cross-venue price divergence is actually captured once AMM liquidity depth and costs are accounted for (not simple mid-price divergence). Source: Gogol et al., arXiv:2403.16083, abstract.
- **Bitcoin cross-exchange arbitrage (classic, blockchain-settlement-driven):** price gaps coincide with periods of **high settlement latency** on the Bitcoin network itself, i.e. decay/persistence is gated by block-confirmation time, not by trading-venue speed; gaps are smaller between exchange pairs with **lower counterparty/default risk** (implying some arbitrage is structurally capped by trust, not by speed). Source: Hautsch, Scheuch, Voigt, arXiv:1812.00595, abstract.
- **Equity latency-arbitrage "race" duration (regulated, mature market, LSE/FTSE 100):** races are extremely short — **modal duration 5–10 microseconds** — and occur **about once per minute per symbol**; this is the fastest, most mechanical end of the decay spectrum and a useful microsecond-scale anchor. Source: Aquilina, Budish, O'Neill, BIS WP 955 / QJE 2022.
- **Makarov & Schoar (classic, cross-country CEX-CEX):** does not report a single half-life number in the materials opened here, but establishes that cross-country arbitrage spreads are **large and recurrent** (not instantly arbitraged away) and **co-move with BTC price appreciation**, implying persistence is driven by capital-control frictions rather than pure speed — i.e., a slow (days-to-weeks-scale, not microsecond-scale) decay regime fundamentally different from the equity latency-race regime above. Source: Makarov & Schoar, JFE 2020 / LSE repository abstract.

**Key cross-cutting point for P6:** the literature shows *at least three distinct decay regimes* that must not be conflated: (a) microsecond-scale mechanical latency races in regulated equities (Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill), (b) block-scale (seconds-to-tens-of-seconds) on-chain/CEX-DEX arbitrage gated by block time and gas (Gogol et al.), and (c) day-to-week-scale cross-country CEX-CEX arbitrage gated by capital controls and counterparty trust (Makarov-Schoar; Hautsch et al.). None of the papers opened here provide a clean OU/AR(1) half-life estimate specifically for CEX-CEX crypto spreads at sub-second/second resolution — this is a measurement gap (see Section 6).

## 3) Lead–lag / price discovery in crypto

- **Methodology stack used in the most recent crypto-specific paper found (Pascual, Rubio, Cebada, Veciana 2025, arXiv:2506.08718):** Hasbrouck Information Share (common-efficient-price VECM decomposition), Gonzalo-Granger Permanent-Transitory decomposition, and the Hayashi-Yoshida covariation estimator (handles non-synchronous tick data). This is the direct, modern application of the classic equity-microstructure toolkit (Hasbrouck 1995; Gonzalo-Granger 1995; Hayashi-Yoshida) to crypto venue pairs.
- **Findings (same paper):** for ETH, **Binance (CEX) typically leads Uniswap v2 (DEX)** in price discovery over a one-year sample with five focal 2024 events. For BTC, **CME futures tend to lead spot overall**, but the lead-lag relationship becomes **mixed during high-volatility periods** — i.e., venue leadership is not static and can invert or weaken under stress. This is an important nuance: a model trained to exploit "futures always lead spot" would be exposed to regime risk exactly when volatility (and arbitrage opportunity) is largest.
- **Foundational estimator (Hoffmann, Rosenbaum, Yoshida 2013, Bernoulli/arXiv:1303.4871):** formalizes lead-lag as a time shift θ such that (X_t, Y_{t+θ}) is a semimartingale, and gives a consistent contrast-optimization estimator built on a modified Hayashi-Yoshida covariation estimator, with convergence rate governed by sampling sparsity — directly reusable for any P6 venue-pair lead-lag estimation under non-synchronous ticks.
- **Empirical lead-lag facts, equities (Huth & Abergel 2014, arXiv:1111.7103):** more liquid instruments (shorter intertrade duration, tighter spread, lower volatility, higher turnover) tend to lead less liquid ones; cross-correlation functions are strongly asymmetric, especially futures-vs-stock; leader data predicts lagger's next mid-quote direction correctly **~60% of the time** (above chance), but **a naive market-order strategy cannot profit net of the bid/ask spread**. This equities result is the clearest documented *measurement pitfall vs. tradability gap* in the literature and should directly inform how P6 reports "lead-lag signal strength" vs. "net profitability."

## 4) Latency arbitrage evidence

- **Aquilina, Budish, O'Neill (QJE 2022 / BIS WP 955)** is the definitive empirical latency-arbitrage paper, enabled by access to full exchange **message data** (including failed/cancelled order attempts — the "losers" of a race, invisible in standard limit-order-book data). Key numbers (all independently confirmed from the BIS working-paper landing page, identical in substance to the QJE abstract):
  - Races occur **~once per minute per symbol** (FTSE 100 constituents).
  - Modal race duration: **5–10 microseconds**.
  - Races account for **~20% of total trading volume**.
  - Top 6 firms account for **>80%** of race wins and losses (high concentration).
  - Average race value: **~half a price tick**, but aggregate stakes are large due to volume.
  - Races constitute **~33% of the effective spread** and **~31% of price impact** (the standard microstructure cost-of-liquidity measures).
  - Latency-arbitrage "tax": **0.42 basis points** of trading volume ≈ **GBP 60 million/year in the UK**; extrapolated globally, **~$5 billion/year** in equity markets.
  - Eliminating latency arbitrage via market-design change could cut the **cost of liquidity by ~17%**.
- **Budish, Cramton, Shim (QJE 2015)** is the theoretical precursor: continuous limit order books mechanically generate frequent, obvious, speed-based arbitrage opportunities; **frequent batch auctions** (discrete-time uniform-price double auctions, e.g. every 100ms) are proposed as a design fix that converts speed competition into price competition.
- **Khapko & Zoican (2019, arXiv:1910.03068), laboratory evidence:** speed bumps (intentional trade delays) only partially curb investment in low-latency technology — asymmetric speed bumps cut investment by just ~20% on average, and symmetric speed bumps produce **no reduction at all** relative to no speed bump. This tempers any assumption that "just add a delay" fully solves latency arbitrage; the effect is design-sensitive and, per this evidence, is a lab (not field/production) result.
- **Crypto-specific latency arbitrage:** no primary academic paper specifically quantifying microsecond/millisecond-scale latency-arbitrage races *between crypto venues* (analogous to Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill's equity message-data study) was found and opened in this session. The closest verified crypto evidence is settlement-latency-driven (block-time-scale, not microsecond-scale): Hautsch et al. (arXiv:1812.00595) on Bitcoin network confirmation latency, and Gogol et al. (arXiv:2406.02172) on block-count persistence of L2 arbitrage. This is a genuine gap — see Section 6.

## 5) Measurement pitfalls we must handle

1. **Non-synchronous trading / asynchronous ticks across venues.** Standard correlation/covariance estimators are biased under asynchronous sampling (the Epps effect). The literature's standard fix is the **Hayashi-Yoshida estimator** (and its lead-lag extension by Hoffmann-Rosenbaum-Yoshida) — P6 should not compute naive lagged correlations on resampled/interpolated grids without accounting for this.
2. **Stale quotes / latency in data collection itself.** The entire Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill contribution rests on the fact that standard limit-order-book data **cannot see failed/cancelled race attempts** — i.e., using only "winning" trade prints will systematically understate race frequency and misattribute who is fast. Any P6 latency-arbitrage measurement using only trade/quote (not full message) data should explicitly caveat this blind spot.
3. **Statistical lead-lag ≠ tradable arbitrage.** Huth & Abergel (2014) show ~60% directional predictability from a leader asset does **not** survive the bid/ask spread in a naive strategy. P6 must report net-of-cost profitability, not raw predictive accuracy or raw price-divergence magnitude, consistent with the loss-versus-rebalancing distinction between gross price divergence and risk/cost-adjusted extractable value (Milionis et al.).
4. **Timestamp/clock errors and cross-venue clock skew.** Not independently verified with a dedicated primary source in this session (see Section 7), but is widely flagged as a standard crypto-data-engineering pitfall (exchange API timestamps vs. local receipt timestamps) that will bias any lead-lag or latency estimate if not corrected; P6 should treat this as a required engineering control even though we could not cite a primary academic source specifically for crypto clock-skew bias in this pass.
5. **Conflating decay regimes / units.** As noted in Section 2, the literature reports decay in at least three incompatible units — microseconds (equity races), blocks (on-chain arbitrage), and days/weeks (cross-country capital-control arbitrage). Any P6 "decay half-life" claim must specify which regime and data-generating process it refers to; a single blended number across these regimes would be a measurement error.
6. **Regime-dependence of venue leadership.** Pascual et al. (2025) find that futures-leads-spot findings for BTC become "mixed" during high-volatility periods — exactly when arbitrage opportunity and model risk are highest. A lead-lag model validated only in calm regimes should not be assumed to generalize to stress periods.
7. **Market concentration biases naive "representative agent" assumptions.** Both Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill (top 6 firms, >80% of equity race wins/losses) and Öz et al. (top 5 addresses, >50% of cross-chain arbitrage trades; one address ~40% post-Dencun) show extreme concentration among arbitrageurs. P6 should not model arbitrage capacity as if available to a generic competitive fringe of traders; capacity/latency/capital advantages are highly concentrated in both TradFi and crypto.

## 6) Gaps where P6 can credibly claim novelty (be critical — what already exists)

- **What already exists and P6 should NOT claim as novel:**
  - The *existence* and rough *magnitude* of cross-venue crypto arbitrage (Makarov-Schoar; multiple Gogol et al. papers) is well established.
  - The *methodological toolkit* for lead-lag/price discovery (Hasbrouck information share, Gonzalo-Granger P-T decomposition, Hayashi-Yoshida, Hoffmann-Rosenbaum-Yoshida) is classic and already applied to crypto CEX-vs-DEX and spot-vs-futures pairs as recently as June 2025 (Pascual et al.).
  - The *theory and empirical scale* of latency arbitrage in regulated equity markets (Budish-Cramton-Shim; Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill) is mature, with precise microsecond/basis-point/dollar figures already published using proprietary message-level data.
  - The on-chain, block-level decay/persistence of CEX-DEX and cross-rollup arbitrage (10–20 blocks; 9s vs 242s inventory-vs-bridge) has already been measured empirically for 2023–2024 data (Gogol et al.; Öz et al.).
- **Where a credible gap remains (based on what could NOT be found/opened in this session):**
  1. **No equity-style message-level latency-race study for CEX-CEX crypto markets.** Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill's method (using full exchange message data to see both winners and losers of microsecond races) has not been found applied to crypto CEX order books (e.g., Binance, Coinbase, OKX) in a primary paper opened here. If P6 has or can obtain message-level crypto exchange data, replicating this methodology for crypto would be a genuine, citable gap.
  2. **No unified cross-regime decay model.** No paper opened here fits a single OU/AR(1) (or survival-analysis) decay model spanning CEX-CEX, CEX-DEX, and cross-chain arbitrage with comparable units and controls for the confounds in Section 5. A rigorously unit-consistent, cross-regime half-life comparison (equity microseconds vs. crypto CEX-CEX seconds-minutes vs. on-chain blocks vs. cross-country days) is not something we found assembled anywhere — this is a plausible, defensible novelty claim for P6, provided the measurement pitfalls above are explicitly handled.
  3. **Crypto funding-rate/spot-perp basis persistence:** leads were found via search (e.g., arXiv:2506.08573 on funding-rate design, and SSRN papers on perpetual futures basis risk) but could **not be opened/verified** in this session (SSRN returned HTTP 403 on fetch attempts). This remains a real literature but is listed under Section 7 as unverified — P6 should not assume these numbers without independent verification, and a rigorous, verifiably-sourced study of funding-rate/basis arbitrage persistence specifically (with net-of-cost, regime-aware analysis) is a plausible gap.
  4. **ML/Hawkes-process/transfer-entropy lead-lag methods applied to crypto microstructure (2023–2026):** search results suggested such work exists (e.g., GitHub repos, blog posts from Arrakis Finance, and an SSRN paper "Binance Leads, but Some Wallets Anticipate") but **none of these could be verified as primary academic sources** in this session — the GitHub repo and finance-blog post are not peer-reviewed/preprint sources, and the SSRN page returned a 403 error on fetch. A properly peer-reviewed/arXiv-indexed Hawkes-process or transfer-entropy lead-lag study for crypto spot/perp/DEX triplets, with transparent net-of-cost backtests, appears to be a genuine open gap P6 could fill or cite into once such sources are verified.
