## ISSUES

1. **blocking - The headline H1 does not evaluate the registered "same first stage, choose whom to upgrade" policy.** `eval/preregistration/c6_v02.md:13-16`, `components/targeting/policy_v02.py:49-60,75-77`, `eval/targeting_v02_eval.py:82-87`. When the chosen first arm costs more than Rs 10 per eligible person, `rule_policy` lotteries recipients but `two_stage(..., arm_choice="rule")` sorts them by model uplift and returns before any upgrade. In registered seeds **12** (incentive) and **15** (BC visit), both policies make **zero upgrades**; the H1 differences of +0.0482 and +0.1116 come wholly from changing first-stage recipients. The code was frozen and the numeric t-test is correct, but the registered policy description and proposal/ADR interpretation are not. **Fix:** make first-stage assignments identical in both policies (and predefine the costly-first-arm case), then register a corrected comparison on new untouched seeds; append a dated clarification to the original registration/results rather than silently rewriting them.

2. **blocking - The DP release can be inverted to the exact confidential counts.** `components/dp_publisher/dp.py:19-27`. `default_rng(seed)` defaults to the publicly known **0**, and the release gives both `rate` and rounded `noisy_denominator`. Replaying its two known Laplace draws recovers a released synthetic `(numerator, denominator) = (27, 100)` **exactly** as `(27, 100)`. The published mechanism is deterministic to anyone with the code and seed, so calling it differential privacy is untenable, regardless of its reproduced MAE. **Fix:** draw secret, fresh, cryptographically secure noise per release; never expose/reuse a production seed. Inject a deterministic RNG only in private tests, and test resistance to reconstruction and repeated-query differencing.

3. **blocking - The women's-share floor fails in the policies that actually run.** `components/targeting/policy_v02.py:49-60,75-77`, `eval/targeting_v02_eval.py:49,87-90`. The default/fallback rule accepts no female vector or floor, and the challenger's `k1 < n` branch returns before its allocator enforces one. In registered seed **15**, eligible women are **57.4508%**, but the default reaches **55.72%** and the challenger **56.98%**; the gate passes in **0/10**, so that unfair default is what would deploy. A two-person, one-contact example yields **zero women treated** even when the requested floor is 50%. Spends remain within budget; fairness, not arithmetic budget, is broken. **Fix:** constrain or stratify the stage-one lottery and costly-first-arm selection, apply the floor to every deployable policy, explicitly reject infeasible requests, and assert the exact floor on final plans.

4. **major - The gate validates a different learned policy from the one it may deploy.** `components/targeting/policy_v02.py:105-119`, `eval/targeting_v02_eval.py:75,84-90`. The gate fits on 70% of the **eligible** pilot and judges its plan on the other 30%; the advertised `challenger_who` is separately fitted on the **whole** pilot, including already-meaningful people and the gate's validation people, with separately estimated arm effects. A positive IPW bound for the first model would not certify the second. The current 0/10 gate outcome masks the defect. **Fix:** validate and deploy the *same frozen model, pilot cohort, arm effects, and selection rule*; if refitting with validation data, require another independent evaluation before enabling it.

5. **major - The claimed 10% deployment holdout and per-BC task cap are only contract fields.** `architecture/contracts/contracts.py:180-186,213-222`, `components/targeting/policy_v02.py:49-93`, `components/simulator/sim.py:50-57`; compare `deliverables/round1-proposal.md:70`. Policies return arm arrays without an `in_holdout` flag, BC assignment, or a `max_tasks_per_bc` argument; the simulator's 20% training pilot is **not** a randomized holdout from each deployed plan. The proposal says "Every plan keeps a 10% randomised holdout." **Fix:** generate a person-level holdout before allocation, remove held-out persons from spend/tasks, enforce capacity with actual BC identifiers, and validate the returned typed plan; otherwise mark these as future safeguards, not implemented behavior.

6. **major - The targeting model sees generator-truth covariates where the documentation promises bank-observable proxies.** `eval/pipeline.py:3-4,31-33,45-48`, `components/simulator/sim.py:289-303`, `components/targeting/engine.py:18-26`. The pipeline maps the simulator's **true** `literate`, `smartphone`, `has_phone`, and DBT type onto policy features via evaluation-only `person_id`. The same true literacy/phone/smartphone fields directly define arm-specific treatment effects; no missingness or proxy error is simulated. This is not direct exposure of `p_arm` or test outcomes, but it is an optimistic, unrealistically clean covariate channel that can inflate H1. **Fix:** generate distinct observable bank proxies with documented error and missingness, train/predict only on those, and retest on fresh worlds.

7. **major - The PMJDY-specific calibration and tested population do not coincide.** `components/simulator/sim.py:132-141,174-190`, `eval/targeting_v02_eval.py:40-46`; compare `deliverables/round1-proposal.md:74`. Accounts are randomly designated PMJDY, but the 23% inoperative calibration covers **all** accounts and targeting includes all non-meaningful people. Direct reconstruction of seeds **12/15** found **16.62%/16.68%** of *unique eligible people* own **no PMJDY account**. The official PMJDY annexure is not a calibration for this mixed-bank population. **Fix:** choose and describe a PMJDY-relevant cohort, calibrate/score that same cohort (or explicitly label this as a generic-bank stress test), and independently re-evaluate any Jan Dhan-specific headline.

8. **major - "Inoperative" is not the RBI legal status for DBT/scholarship-purpose accounts.** `components/usage_state/classify.py:42-58`, `architecture/contracts/contracts.py:56-79`. R1 sets `state="inoperative"` at 24 months regardless of the input `official_inoperative` or account-opening purpose. A constructed row with `official_inoperative=False` is labelled `inoperative`. The [RBI revised instructions, Annex paragraph 3](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12589&Mode=0) require accounts **opened for DBT or scholarships** to be segregated so the inoperative stipulation does not apply. Receiving a DBT credit is not necessarily the same as having been opened for that purpose. **Fix:** distinguish behavioral 24-month non-use from CBS/legal inoperativity; carry the opening-purpose flag, honor the exemption for the official status, and test it separately from pass-through usage.

9. **major - The privacy-budget ledger neither composes releases nor rejects invalid epsilon.** `components/dp_publisher/dp.py:9-16,19-24`, `eval/scorecard_eval.py:78-85`. Evaluation creates separate `Ledger(1.0)` objects for unit and district releases over overlapping people, so even with proper randomness the cumulative bound is at least epsilon **2**, not 1. Restarting a process resets the in-memory ledger. `Ledger(1).charge(float("nan"), ...)` makes `spent=nan`, after which a charge of **100** succeeds against a limit of 1. **Fix:** validate finite positive total/charge epsilon, persist atomic per-population/per-purpose accounting across calls and restarts, and compose unit/district and repeated time-series releases before publishing. Wire C5's epsilon obligation into this enforcement rather than merely recording it.

10. **major - The "oracle" is an approximate allocator, not an upper bound.** `eval/frozen_allocate.py:27-35`, `eval/targeting_eval.py:103-106`; compare `deliverables/round1-proposal.md:85`. With two identical people each offering uplift 1 from a Rs 3 SMS, budget Rs 3, and all other arms negative, the frozen binary-search Lagrangian picks **neither** person (value 0), while picking one is feasible (value 1). Discrete ties defeat the dual threshold. Thus `(V - V_random)/(V_oracle - V_random)` is **not guaranteed** to be a "share of achievable gain" even though the ten published worlds produced stable denominators. **Fix:** use an exact or certified multiple-choice knapsack benchmark (including equity), or relabel the denominator as a heuristic reference and avoid upper-bound language; regression-test ties and tight budgets.

11. **major - The scorecard's four-quarter result is not a longitudinal, end-to-end gaming test.** `eval/scorecard_eval.py:49-58,68-85`, `components/scorecard/scorecard.py:14-21,36-40`. Setting `n = 3,000 * quarters` generates **12,000 independent records** per unit for "four quarters", with no quarter/person identifier, fixed-person holdout, repeat usage, attrition, or correlation. The outcome `meaningful` is drawn directly from the true effect instead of going through C2/C3; for non-gamers `acct_operative = meaningful` exactly, while gamers get a large extra token-credit signal. The rise in rho **0.708 -> 0.900**, 95% CI coverage, and perfect gaming flags therefore validate this favorable IID generator, not sustained real-person attribution or subtle gaming. **Fix:** test the complete transaction-to-person pipeline with stable people, assignments, correlated repeated outcomes and realistic manipulations; use person/cluster-aware uncertainty. Otherwise describe the four-quarter number as a larger independent sample, not follow-up.

12. **major - Incomplete linkage makes "one person, one outcome" an optimistic counterfactual.** `components/linker/link.py:6-9,22-35`, `eval/pipeline.py:24-33`, `eval/targeting_eval.py:36-44`. The intentionally unseeded accounts become singleton tokens; in seeds 12/15 there are **244/216** more eligible tokens than unique true people. The plan can pay for multiple different contacts to one person, while `person_value` picks the **maximum true arm uplift after the fact** for that person. This is neither a single-treatment potential outcome nor a modeled effect of multiple contacts; its effect on the +20.25-person average H1 gain is unmeasured. **Fix:** report linked versus unlinked cohort sensitivity, enforce one assignment/cost per known person, and specify/test the joint-outcome assumption for genuinely unlinked duplicate contacts.

13. **minor - Small units with no holdout silently produce NaN estimates and CIs.** `components/scorecard/scorecard.py:14-22`. A constructed unit with treated records but no holdout yields `incremental=nan` and `ci_low=nan`; the 3,000-person/unit benchmark never exercises a small BC. **Fix:** enforce minimum treated/control counts, emit an explicit insufficient-data status or pool units, and test small and empty cells before generating scorecards.

14. **minor - The proposal overstates the gaming-rank result, beyond what the numeric drift checker checks.** `deliverables/round1-proposal.md:72`, `eval/scorecard_eval.py:73-75`, `eval/check_docs.py:43-52`. It says four-quarter top-20 gamers are "no more common" than overall, but the reproduced mean is **23.0% vs 21.4%** overall; seed 0 is **40% vs 19%**. The difference may well be sampling noise, but the literal assertion is false and `check_docs.py` checks only the three rho substrings. **Fix:** write "roughly their base rate on average, with sizable seed variation" and show the denominators; do not imply a reliable anti-gaming ranking from this generator.

## Verdict

The registered output numbers, paired t-tests, public annexure totals, scorecard rho values and real-retail RCT estimates **reproduce**. Synthetic and retail-versus-banking labels are generally candid; the original test's push chronology and code freeze are supported, and no budget overspend appeared in the ten worlds. But **a reproducible p-value does not establish the registered upgrade-only comparison**, the actual fallback misses the women's floor, and the current "DP" release can disclose exact counts. Fix or explicitly withdraw those claims before presenting them as deployable, regulated-bank safeguards; the remaining major issues are substantive sensitivity tests for the lead to decide.
