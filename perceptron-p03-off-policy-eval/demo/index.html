A policy switch, re-enacted on real traffic: the uniform-random policy's logs for the first 42 hours, the Thompson-sampling policy's (BTS) for the next 42. Can't tell from these logs

Our evaluator refuses this one. To score "Random during the last 42 h" it must reweight the BTS logs, and BTS almost never shows what Random would. The 2,769,425 BTS impressions are worth 25,395 uniform-random ones for this question (ESS/n 0.92%, below the frozen 5% floor). A single click logged at propensity 0.000001 counts 12,500 times.

Post hoc The overlap gate (ESS/n ≥ 5%, mean-weight |z| ≤ 3) was calibrated on the simulator only and frozen before it touched OBD, but it was designed after we had seen these OBD failures, so every OBD verdict on this sheet is post hoc.

Was refusing right?

Here, no: the interval held the A/B check, so this refusal cost an answer. On the pre-registered whole-day pair 25 → 26 Nov, the same estimate failed: DR +1.48 (+1.20 … +1.76) against a check of +1.06, with the dashboard at +1.66. The gate refused that one too (ESS/n 0.80%).

When the logs can tell: 4.936 vs 4.890

Uniform-random logs cover every item at every position, so they can score the Thompson-sampling policy. The gate answers (ESS/n 11.4%, mean-weight z +1.4). Doubly robust lands 0.9% from what the BTS arm actually earned, 4.890 ±0.056.

An approximate target, said plainly

BTS kept learning: 20/20 of its busiest item-position cells moved their propensity at least 1.5× within the 84 h (median 16×). So we score an hourly reconstruction of it. The standard OBD protocol scores one static prior (the top block), which is a different policy.

The direct method looked overconfident

DM's interval (SE 0.0007 per 1,000) misses the check. Refitting its model inside the bootstrap gives SE 0.196 per 1,000, and DM becomes consistent (dashed red, post hoc).

Same evaluator, opposite logs

Sheet 1 asked BTS logs about Random: overlap 0.9%, refused. Here Random logs answer about BTS: overlap 11.4%, answered, within 0.9%. Which logs you kept decides which questions you can ever answer. Sheet 3 makes that a knob.

Turn the knob: how far can a new policy stray before the logs can't tell?

Candidate policy πα = (1 − α) × BTS + α × uniform random. Its true value is a straight line between the two A/B arms, a real check at every α. From BTS logs the importance weights are exact: (1 − α) + α / (80 × logged propensity). IPS always prints a number. Our evaluator prints it only when the frozen gate passes.
