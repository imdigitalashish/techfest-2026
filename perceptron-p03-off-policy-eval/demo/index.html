Can't tell from these logs

Why it refuses. To score "Random during the last 42 h" it must reweight the BTS logs, and BTS rarely shows what Random would. Weight ESS is about 25,395, or 0.92% of the 2,769,425 rows, below the frozen 5% floor: a measure of weight concentration, not guaranteed precision. A click at the smallest logged propensity (0.000001) would get weight 12,500; the largest weight on an actual click here is about 139 (propensity 9e-05).

Post hoc The gate (ESS/n ≥ 5%, mean-weight |z| ≤ 3) was selected using simulator runs after OBD failures had informed its design, then frozen. Every OBD verdict on this sheet is therefore post hoc.

When the logs can tell: 4.936 vs 4.890

Uniform-random logs cover every item at every position, so they can score the Thompson-sampling policy. The gate answers (weight ESS 11.4% of rows, mean-weight z +1.4). Doubly robust lands 0.9% from the BTS arm's observed click rate, 4.890 ±0.056: an approximate-target comparison, not verified accuracy.

An approximate target, said plainly

BTS kept learning: 20/20 of its busiest item-position cells moved their propensity at least 1.5× within the 84 h (median 16×). So we score an hourly reconstruction of it. The standard OBD protocol scores one static prior (the top block), which is a different policy.

The direct method looked overconfident

DM's interval (SE 0.0007 per 1,000) misses the BTS arm's rate. Refitting its model inside the bootstrap gives SE 0.196 per 1,000, and DM becomes consistent (dashed red, post hoc).

Same evaluator, opposite logs

Sheet 1 asked BTS logs about Random: weight ESS 0.9% of rows, refused. Here Random logs answer about BTS: weight ESS 11.4%, answered, within 0.9%. Which logs you keep decides which questions you can answer. Sheet 3 makes that a knob.

Turn the knob: how far can a new policy stray before the logs can't tell?

Candidate policy πα = (1 − α) × BTS + α × uniform random. We compare against a noisy concurrent-A/B reference: the two arms' observed click rates, mixed by α, not known truth. From BTS logs the weights come straight from the logged propensities, (1 − α) + α / (80 × propensity). Plain IPS always prints a number; our evaluator prints it only when the frozen gate passes.

Registered primary: IPS, row-level, BTS logs (606 grid points). The gate answered 87/606 and E1 held at 75/87 of those answers (12 misses: Nov 26, α 0.28–0.39, at most 0.036 per 1,000 beyond the interval). Of those answers, 6 are at α = 0, the logging end, where the estimate equals the reference by construction; at α > 0, E1 held at 69/81. It refused 519/606: 403 with the reference inside the own CI, 116 outside. Ungated IPS on the same points: E1 holds at 478/606. E2 on the same scope: answered 48 consistent, 39 rejecting; refused 458 consistent, 61 rejecting.

Secondary: Random logs (approximate target, 606 points): answered 606/606; IPS E1 held at 606/606, E2 at 606/606. Secondary: pooled (not the registered primary): 681 / 12 / 403 / 116 (answered inside / answered outside / refused inside / refused outside).

These are correlated grid locations from overlapping periods and deterministic α steps, not independent decisions. Under this point-containment diagnostic the gate trades fewer answers for a lower observed miss fraction (12/87 answered vs 128/606 ungated); deployment value remains untested.

Endpoint-derived checks: 31/33 matched

IPS(λ) = (1 − λ)·p + λ·u
SNIPS(λ) = [(1 − λ)·p + λ·u] / [(1 − λ) + λ·m]

λ = the far-end share (BTS logs: λ = α; Random logs: λ = 1 − α); p = the logging arm's click rate; u = IPS at the far end; m = the far end's mean weight. The curves are determined by these endpoint statistics, so the registered checks mainly test algebra and implementation, not independent forecasts. The gate's mean-weight z is the same at every α away from the logging end. Computed beyond the endpoints: hour-cluster uncertainty, the full-window BTS aggregation and previously unrecorded z statistics.
