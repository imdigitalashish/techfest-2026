When the logs can tell: {{S2_dr}} vs {{S2_truth}}

Uniform-random logs cover every item at every position, so they can score the Thompson-sampling policy. The gate answers (ESS/n {{S2_ess_pct}}, mean-weight z {{S2_z}}). Doubly robust lands {{S2_dr_relerr}} from what the BTS arm actually earned, {{S2_truth_plain}} {{S2_truth_hw}}.

An approximate target, said plainly

BTS kept learning: {{S2_a1_cells}} of its busiest item-position cells moved their propensity at least 1.5× within the 84 h (median {{S2_a1_med}}×). So we score an hourly reconstruction of it. The standard OBD protocol scores one static prior (the top block), which is a different policy.

The direct method looked overconfident

DM's interval (SE {{S2_dm_se_old}} per 1,000) misses the check. Refitting its model inside the bootstrap gives SE {{S2_dm_se}} per 1,000, and DM becomes consistent (dashed red, post hoc).

Turn the knob: how far can a new policy stray before the logs can't tell?

Candidate policy πα = (1 − α) × BTS + α × uniform random. Its true value is a straight line between the two A/B arms, a real check at every α. From BTS logs the importance weights are exact: (1 − α) + α / (80 × logged propensity). IPS always prints a number. Our evaluator prints it only when the frozen gate passes.
