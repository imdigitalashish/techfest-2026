When the logs can tell: {{S2_dr}} vs {{S2_truth}}

Uniform-random logs cover every item at every position, so they can score the Thompson-sampling policy. The gate answers (weight ESS {{S2_ess_pct}} of rows, mean-weight z {{S2_z}}). Doubly robust lands {{S2_dr_relerr}} from the BTS arm's observed click rate, {{S2_truth_plain}} {{S2_truth_hw}}: an approximate-target comparison, not verified accuracy.

An approximate target, said plainly

BTS kept learning: {{S2_a1_cells}} of its busiest item-position cells moved their propensity at least 1.5× within the 84 h (median {{S2_a1_med}}×). So we score an hourly reconstruction of it. The standard OBD protocol scores one static prior (the top block), which is a different policy.

The direct method looked overconfident

DM's interval (SE {{S2_dm_se_old}} per 1,000) misses the BTS arm's rate. Refitting its model inside the bootstrap gives SE {{S2_dm_se}} per 1,000, and DM becomes consistent (dashed red, post hoc).

Turn the knob: how far can a new policy stray before the logs can't tell?

Candidate policy πα = (1 − α) × BTS + α × uniform random. We compare against a noisy concurrent-A/B reference: the two arms' observed click rates, mixed by α, not known truth. From BTS logs the weights come straight from the logged propensities, (1 − α) + α / (80 × propensity). Plain IPS always prints a number; our evaluator prints it only when the frozen gate passes.

Endpoint-derived checks: {{K_CHECKS}} matched

IPS(λ) = (1 − λ)·p + λ·u
SNIPS(λ) = [(1 − λ)·p + λ·u] / [(1 − λ) + λ·m]

λ = the far-end share (BTS logs: λ = α; Random logs: λ = 1 − α); p = the logging arm's click rate; u = IPS at the far end; m = the far end's mean weight. The curves are determined by these endpoint statistics, so the registered checks mainly test algebra and implementation, not independent forecasts. The gate's mean-weight z is the same at every α away from the logging end. Computed beyond the endpoints: hour-cluster uncertainty, the full-window BTS aggregation and previously unrecorded z statistics.
