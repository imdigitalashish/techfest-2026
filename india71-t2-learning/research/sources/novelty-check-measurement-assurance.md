# Novelty check: "measurement assurance" spike-2 for FLN outcomes (India@71/100 Theme 2)

Access date for every source opened in this pass: **2026-10-04**. Claims without an opened primary/official source are marked **UNVERIFIED**. This file is additive to the existing research set (`methods-sae-audit.md`, `international-practice.md`, `evidence-base.md`, `deployed-states-ngos.md`, `official-udise-nipun.md`) — see those for Jacob & Levitt, Haryana Saksham, UP NIPUN RFP, Gujarat Gunotsav, SHANGRLA/ALPHA, IRS audits, and World Bank/`emdi` SAE+benchmarking, which are **not** repeated in full here.

Design under test (spike-2): (1) rotating risk-and-uncertainty-weighted random subsample of schools re-tested independently each term with known, non-zero inclusion probabilities (design-unbiased); (2) re-tests calibrate teacher-reported census results via a GREG/Horvitz-Thompson bias model using forensic auxiliaries (heaping, low variance, covariate gaps); (3) block-level Fay-Herriot SAE with UDISE+ covariates, benchmarked to a sparse national survey anchor (PARAKH/ASER); (4) no school-level punitive trust labels.

## 2. Verdict

**Established practice (each individual component, separately):**
- External/statistical moderation of teacher-graded marks against an independent anchor is established and mainstream in high-stakes school examinations (HKDSE, NSW HSC, QCAA) — but always as a **census** operation (every school, every year/cohort), not a random rotating subsample with known, uncertainty-weighted inclusion probabilities.
- Sampling only a minority of units for external re-check, rotating across years, is established in the England KS2 system (≥25%/year, every school ≥once per 4 years) — but the sampling rule is a flat statutory share, not a design that explicitly targets statistical uncertainty × decision value, and KS2 moderation does not feed a GREG/HT bias-corrected aggregate or a SAE model.
- Random/stratified "back-check" re-verification subsamples to catch and correct fabricated or biased field-collected data are established IPA/SurveyCTO field practice, and independent third-party verification gating RBF/DLI payouts is established World Bank practice for *learning outcomes specifically* — but neither is reported, in any source opened, as feeding a formal design-based bias-correction estimator (GREG/HT) or a benchmarked small-area model.
- Fay-Herriot SAE combining a sparse household survey with census auxiliaries has been applied to a literacy outcome as recently as 2026 (Kenya) — confirming the SAE half of the pipeline is mainstream and currently active research, but with zero re-test/bias-correction/benchmarking layer in the opened source.
- The statistical ancestor of "cheap census + small gold-standard validation subsample corrects bias" is Tenenbein's 1970 double-sampling scheme — a 50+-year-old, foundational technique, though its FLN/education application was not found anywhere, and the original text was not opened (paywalled) so details are **UNVERIFIED**.

**Likely-new combination:** No source opened in this pass or the prior research pass combines, for *government FLN census data specifically*: (i) a rotating, risk-and-uncertainty-weighted *random* re-test subsample with *known inclusion probabilities* (design-unbiased by construction, not a flat statutory share); (ii) a GREG/Horvitz-Thompson bias-correction model that recalibrates *every* school's teacher-reported score using re-test discrepancies plus forensic auxiliary features (heaping, variance, covariate gaps); (iii) a Fay-Herriot small-area model benchmarked to an independent national survey anchor (PARAKH/ASER) for block-level estimates; and (iv) an explicitly non-punitive, no-school-level-trust-label design intent. Each pairwise piece has partial precedent (moderation↔anchor-exam; back-check↔random-subsample re-verification; RBF/DLI↔independent verification of learning outcomes; Fay-Herriot↔literacy SAE), but the four-part pipeline applied to teacher-administered FLN census data was not found assembled anywhere in the sources opened.

**Recommended one-sentence defensible novelty claim:**
"While statistical moderation of teacher-graded scores against an external anchor (HKDSE/NSW/QCAA), rotating-sample external moderation (England KS2, ~25%/year), random back-check validation subsamples (IPA/SurveyCTO), independent verification of learning-outcome DLIs (World Bank RBF), and Fay-Herriot small-area estimation of a literacy indicator from survey+census data (Kenya, 2026) are each separately established practice, no source found in this review combines them into a single pipeline — a risk-and-uncertainty-weighted, known-probability random re-test subsample of schools that corrects every school's teacher-reported FLN census score via a design-based (GREG/Horvitz-Thompson) bias model and feeds a benchmarked Fay-Herriot small-area estimate — for government-administered foundational learning data, which is the specific combination this spike proposes as its novel contribution."
