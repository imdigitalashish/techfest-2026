## 1. ASER 2024: rural FLN and enrolment

ASER 2024 is the strongest “lived reality” source for household-level FLN because it tests children one-on-one at home, rather than relying on school-reported administrative data. The survey reached 649,491 children in 17,997 villages across 605 rural districts and used a district-representative sample of 30 villages and 20 households per village (600 households per district).

**Std III reading:** all-school rural India recovered from 20.5% in 2022 to 27.1% in 2024, close to 27.3% in 2018. Government-school Std III children improved from 16.3% in 2022 to 23.4% in 2024, surpassing the 2018 government-school level of 20.9% but still leaving roughly three in four Std III government-school children unable to read a Std II text.

**Std III arithmetic:** all-school subtraction improved from 25.9% in 2022 to 33.7% in 2024, above 28.2% in 2018. Government-school children improved from 20.2% in 2022 to 27.6% in 2024, still 6.1 percentage points below all-school performance.

**Enrolment:** ASER shows the access story is much stronger than the learning story. Enrolment of children aged 6–14 remained 98.1% in 2024. Government-school enrolment for 6–14-year-olds was 66.8% in 2024 after a Covid-era spike to 72.9% in 2022.

**No ASER 2025/2026 found:** I checked `https://asercentre.org/aser-2025/` and `https://asercentre.org/aser-2026/`; both returned ASER 404 pages. No ASER 2025 or ASER 2026 annual report was found in this check.

## 2. PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 and SEAS 2023

PARAKH PRS 2024 is the newest official large-scale assessment source opened. The national report says it assessed 21,15,022 children from Grades 3, 6 and 9 across 74,229 unique schools and 781 districts in all 36 states/UTs.

### Grade 3

Grade 3 had 27,741 participating schools and 5,99,026 participating students. Language outperformed mathematics across gender/location/management/social-category charts. Social category data show a measurable disadvantage: ST students scored 61% in Language and 57% in Mathematics, while “Others” scored 66% and 61% respectively. Grade 3 mathematics competency tables identify the weakest national competencies as C-8.8 geometry/spatial relations (50%) and C-8.11 money transactions up to INR 100 (50%).

### Grade 6 and Grade 9

The report’s OCR/text extraction gives charted subgroup scores but not a clean all-India subject-average table. Grade 6 charts show Mathematics as the weakest subject: state-government schools scored 52% Language, 43% Mathematics and 47% The World Around Us; ST students scored 51% Language, 41% Mathematics and 45% The World Around Us. Grade 9 charts again show Mathematics as weakest: ST students scored 47% Language, 32% Mathematics, 36% Science and 36% Social Science; “Others” scored 60%, 40%, 44% and 43%.

### District/state report cards

The national report includes a “50 High Performing Districts (Grade 3)” section. The PARAKH site also exposes PRS report pages (`https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs-reports-2024`) and dashboards, but I did not fully extract district PDF/API data within the time budget.

### SEAS 2023

PARAKH navigation includes a “SEAS Dashboard” link on the official site. I did not complete extraction of SEAS 2023 numeric tables; keep this as an open source for the next research pass.

## 3. NCERT Foundational Learning Study (FLS) 2022

The official PARAKH FLS page states that FLS 2022 was administered 23–26 March 2022 across India; assessed Grade 3 foundational learning in 20 languages; covered approximately 86,000 Grade 3 students from 10,000 schools; and included more than 18,000 teachers. The report guide states ORF is calculated as Correct Words Read Per Minute by a student at the end of Grade 3 and presents global proficiency levels (“below partially meets”, “partially meets”, “meets”, “exceeds”).

## 5. UDISE+ 2024-25 and 2023-24

UDISE+ 2024-25 reports 1,471,473 schools, 246,932,680 enrolments (pre-primary to higher secondary), and 10,122,420 teachers. Government schools account for 1,013,322 schools and 121,589,911 enrolments. It reports 104,125 single-teacher schools with 3,376,769 enrolments — a crucial underserved-group fact for intervention design.

Primary GER is 90.9 and primary PTR is 20. UDISE+ says individual student-wise collection began in 2022-23 and captures more than 60 fields per student. It also says unique student/teacher identity can support monitoring attendance, learning outcomes and health parameters. However, the public report does not present classroom-level FLN results comparable to ASER/PARAKH; therefore UDISE+ should be treated as the administrative spine, not as the learning-outcome source by itself.

For 2023-24, I found secondary leads but did not download an official UDISE+ 2023-24 PDF from a government URL in time. The 2024-25 report itself warns that 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 are comparable with each other but not directly with earlier reports for some indicators.

## Candidate measurable gaps

1. **Access vs learning gap for rural Std III government-school children.** ASER 2024: 98.1% of 6–14-year-olds are enrolled, but only 23.4% of Std III government-school children can read a Std II text and 27.6% can do subtraction. This is a direct scorecard-vs-lived-reality gap for rural government-school children.

2. **Government-school vs all-school Std III learning gap.** In ASER 2024, all-school Std III reading is 27.1%, while government-school reading is 23.4% (3.7 percentage-point gap). All-school subtraction is 33.7%, while government-school subtraction is 27.6% (6.1 percentage-point gap).

3. **ST foundational assessment gap in PARAKH Grade 3.** PARAKH PRS 2024 Grade 3: ST children score 61% Language and 57% Mathematics; “Others” score 66% and 61%. The ST gap is 5 percentage points in Language and 4 points in Mathematics.

4. **Single-teacher school exposure.** UDISE+ 2024-25: 104,125 single-teacher schools enrol 3,376,769 children. This is a system-delivery gap: millions of children are in schools structurally unlikely to provide grade-level, competency-specific remediation without external decision support.

5. **Competency-specific Grade 3 numeracy gap.** PARAKH Grade 3 maths: geometry/spatial relations and money transactions each score 50%, while pattern extension is 69%. A remediation tool should not say “math is weak”; it should assign targeted activities for C-8.8 and C-8.11.

## Implications for our design

- Build around **student/teacher/school IDs from UDISE+** as the administrative spine, but do not rely on UDISE+ alone for learning levels.
- Ingest **ASER/PARAKH/FLS-style competency data** into a separate learning-evidence layer mapped to NIPUN Lakshyas and state curricular competencies.
- Prioritise **Std I–III government schools**, especially single-teacher schools and ST-concentrated geographies, because the measurable gaps are sharp and policy-relevant.
- Teacher-facing tools must be **competency-specific**: e.g., Grade 3 C-8.8 geometry/spatial relations and C-8.11 money transactions require different remediation cards.
- Block/district dashboards should translate large-scale assessments into **resource decisions**: cluster-level teacher training modules, school visits, textbook/workbook allocation and SMC agenda items.
- Keep low bandwidth: ASER demonstrates one-on-one oral assessment can work without high infrastructure; the product should support offline-first entry, sync, printable activities and voice/local-language prompts.
