## 1. Small-area estimation (SAE)

- **Fay-Herriot and recent SAE.** Opened World Bank area-level SAE PDF, access date 2026-10-04. It defines SAE operationally: “Small Area Estimation is a branch of statistics focused on improving reliability of estimates and the associated measures of uncertainty for populations where samples cannot produce sufficiently reliable estimates.” It says FH is used where “access to microdata is not possible” and “combines survey estimates and area-level data (e.g. census aggregates, geospatial data, admin data…).” This matches our sparse trusted-survey + rich school-admin-data problem.
- **Fay-Herriot original.** The opened CRAN emdi DESCRIPTION cites: “Estimates of income for small places: An Application of James-Stein Procedures to Census Data” by Fay and Herriot (1979), DOI `10.1080/01621459.1979.10482505`. The original paywalled article text was **UNVERIFIED** in this session; only citation/DOI and secondary implementation descriptions were opened.
- **Benchmarking.** Opened emdi manual, access date 2026-10-04. The function `benchmark(object, benchmark, share, type = "raking")` “benchmarks the EBLUP estimates of an area-level model” and supports “Raking”, “Ratio adjustment”, and “MSE adjustment.” This is exactly the mechanism to make block estimates aggregate to PARAKH/ASER/NAS anchors.
- **Education/learning applications.** Opened PARAKH dashboard HTML, access date 2026-10-04: it displays “PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024,” “Participation (Sampled),” “View Districts,” and stratifications “By Gender,” “By Location,” “By Management,” and “By Social Group.” This confirms PARAKH is an aggregate/sample dashboard suitable as an anchor, but sample design details from the PARAKH FAQ PDF failed and are **UNVERIFIED**. ASER official pages opened: process says “20 households are surveyed in every village” and “ASER testing tools are used to assess foundational skills of children aged 5 to 16 years” (https://asercentre.org/process-documents/); ASER survey page says volunteers from partner organisations conduct the survey (https://asercentre.org/aser-survey/).
- **Python options.** No dedicated Python SAE package was confirmed. statsmodels MixedLM and PyMC hierarchical models are viable building blocks; full MRP/poststratification must be custom.

## 2. Detecting inflated/manipulated test results

- **Jacob & Levitt (Rotten Apples).** Opened NBER page and PDF, access date 2026-10-04. The abstract says they “develop an algorithm for detecting teacher cheating that combines information on unexpected test score fluctuations and suspicious patterns of answers for students in a classroom,” estimating serious cheating in “a minimum of 4-5 percent” of classrooms. The paper explicitly links older investigations to “erasure patterns and the controlled re-testing of students,” and says a cheating indicator is a classroom with “unexpectedly large gains in test scores.”
- **Score-distribution forensics.** We should implement features for heaping at cut-scores, implausible gains, low within-class variance, too-perfect completion, and anomalous item/skill response patterns. Direct primary sources for heaping/bunching in education assessment were **UNVERIFIED** in this session.
- **Recent ML anomaly detection.** Opened Frontiers 2021 review, access date 2026-10-04. It frames “Educational Anomaly Analytics” and notes that educational data systems create “an unprecedented opportunity to explore educational anomalies from a data-driven perspective.” However, this is broad educational analytics (dropout/failure/mental health etc.), not validated state FLN census inflation detection. Mark ML-for-FLN-inflation as **UNVERIFIED / gap** unless validated on re-test labels.

## 3. Audit sampling with statistical guarantees

- **Risk-limiting audits.** Opened Lindeman & Stark PDF, access date 2026-10-04. It states: “Risk-limiting audits provide statistical assurance that election outcomes are correct by manually examining portions of the audit trail,” and defines the risk limit as “the largest chance that the audit will stop short of a full hand tally when the original outcome is in fact wrong.” For FLN, map “full hand tally” to full independent re-test/re-scoring and the reported claim to “≥80% children meet goal.”
- **SHANGRLA / ALPHA.** Opened SHANGRLA repo and arXiv pages. SHANGRLA expresses outcomes as assertions: “the mean of a list of non-negative numbers is greater than 1/2,” then tests each assertion sequentially; if nulls are not rejected, “we proceed to a full manual recount.” For FLN, define an assorter from independent re-test outcomes and claimed threshold (e.g. 80%) and sample until the martingale p-value/e-value confirms or escalates.
- **Risk-weighted random sampling and bias correction.** Opened IRS audit-selection paper, access date 2026-10-04. It gives the exact design principle our engine needs: combine risk targeting with population estimation. It says a policy should “maximize reward and maintain an accurate (ideally unbiased) population estimate”; the IRS uses both “a stratified random sample to estimate the tax gap” and “a focused risk-selected sample.” We can oversample high-risk schools while retaining a randomized calibration arm and known inclusion probabilities for Horvitz-Thompson/GREG correction.
- **HT/GREG.** Opened World Bank SAE PDF. It names “Horvitz-Thompson (1952) – design unbiased but large variance.” A primary GREG/two-phase source could not be opened; details are **UNVERIFIED**, but the design direction is clear: estimate reported-vs-retested bias using known inclusion probabilities plus auxiliary covariates, then correct census reports.

## 4. Independent re-testing / verification in education systems

- **Haryana Saksham.** Opened Haryana government PR page, access date 2026-10-04. It states that under Saksham Ghoshna, “more than 80% students score over 50% marks” makes a block Saksham, “less than 60%” not Saksham, and “data analysis indicate mass cheating” can disqualify a block. It also says the exam was “a third-party assessment for all six grades… 3 to 8 in government schools in all 119 blocks.” This is highly relevant precedent: a threshold claim with independent verification, but not a formal sequential risk-limiting audit.
- **UP NIPUN / Mission Prerna.** Opened official GeM RFP PDF, access date 2026-10-04. It requests a “third-party assessment agency” for UP primary schools under NIPUN. It says UP wants “regular and independent data,” is recruiting an “independent, high-quality external agency,” covers “all 75 districts and 880 blocks,” recommends district samples with “95% Confidence interval and 5% Error of Margin,” and includes “Audits: A subsample of schools to be selected and the same tests… to recognise any discrepancies.” This is the closest direct precedent to our re-test audit component.
- **Gujarat Gunotsav.** Opened official Gunotsav pages, access date 2026-10-04. About page says Gunotsav is an “accountability framework for quality of primary education” including “learning outcomes,” and process is two-phase: “self evaluation… covering 100% schools” followed by evaluation by “3000 senior government officials… around 9000 schools,” with “25% of schools in each block” personally assessed. Gunotsav-4 page says “External & Self Evaluation” and 60% education/subject marks + 20% co-scholastic + 20% resources/community. This validates self-assessment + external-check architecture, but not random audit guarantees.

The closest precedents are: Jacob & Levitt for forensic scoring + re-test validation; UP NIPUN/Haryana/Gunotsav for independent education verification; IRS audits for risk-selection plus unbiased population estimation; and World Bank/emdi for SAE + benchmarking.
