## Patterns: why the gap persists despite these

1. **Scale is easier than verified learning impact.** VSK, SwiftChat, Vaachan Samiksha and UP NIPUN show production-scale data pipes, but opened sources rarely include independently estimated learning effects.
2. **Teacher-administered assessment remains vulnerable.** The MP Pratibha Parv audit is the sharpest warning: tests “proctored and graded by teachers” with little oversight misled learning-level inferences.
3. **Verification design matters.** Assam Gunotsav’s external-evaluator sampling and Vaachan Samiksha’s geotagged/tamper-proof design directly address manipulation risk, but neither source proves student-learning impact.
4. **Actionability is uneven.** TaRL links diagnosis to a clear instructional response (group by level + targeted instruction); many dashboards monitor gaps but do not prove teachers changed instruction.
5. **Language/localisation is a hard bottleneck.** Shiksha Copilot’s Kannada results show that teacher decision-support in Indian languages needs human-in-loop QA, not just model deployment.
6. **Evidence often sits outside government systems.** Strong impact evidence exists for TaRL and the MP audit; many state systems publish process dashboards/guidelines rather than causal evaluations.
