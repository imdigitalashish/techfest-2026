## Source entries

### [1] Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014) — "The Price Impact of Order Book Events"
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402 (accessed 2026-10-04). Published: J. Financial Econometrics, DOI 10.1093/jjfinec/nbt003 (verified via page `citation_doi`/`citation_author` meta tags: Cont, Rama; Kukanov, Arseniy; Stoikov, Sasha).
- Verified abstract content (from page metadata, exact text): "We study the price impact of order book events - limit orders, market orders and cancelations - using the NYSE TAQ data for 50 U.S. stocks. We show that, over short time intervals, price changes are mainly driven by the order flow imbalance, defined as the imbalance between supply and demand at the best bid and ask prices. Our study reveals a linear relation between order flow imbalance and price changes, with a slope inversely proportional to the market depth. These results are shown to be robust to seasonality effects, and stable across time scales and across stocks. We argue that this linear price impact model, together with a scaling argument, implies the empirically observed 'square-root' relation between price changes and trading volume. However, the relation between price changes and trade volume is found to be noisy and less robust than the one based on order flow imbalance."
- Significance for us: THE foundational OFI paper. Key verified claims: (a) OFI (not trade volume/sign) is the primary short-horizon driver of price changes at top of book; (b) relation is linear with slope ~ 1/market depth (price impact coefficient); (c) robust to intraday seasonality and stable across stocks/time-scales. The exact per-event formula (e_n contribution rules for bid/ask price+size changes) is in the paper body (PDF), not reproduced in the abstract metadata fetched here — exact formula notation is given in "Feature set" section below flagged as standard-literature restatement, cross-checked against later papers' framing (see entries below) rather than quoted verbatim from this page.

### [2] Ntakaris, Magris, Kanniainen, Gabbouj & Iosifidis (2018) — "Benchmark Dataset for Mid-Price Forecasting of Limit Order Book Data with Machine Learning Methods" (FI-2010)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03233 (accessed 2026-10-04). DOI 10.1002/for.2543 (J. Forecasting). Authors verified via meta tags.
- Verified abstract (exact): "Managing the prediction of metrics in high-frequency financial markets is a challenging task... This paper describes the first publicly available benchmark dataset of high-frequency limit order markets for mid-price prediction. We extracted normalized data representations of time series data for five stocks from the NASDAQ Nordic stock market for a time period of ten consecutive days, leading to a dataset of ~4,000,000 time series samples in total. A day-based anchored cross-validation experimental protocol is also provided..."
- Significance: defines FI-2010, the standard benchmark LOB dataset (5 Finnish stocks, NASDAQ Nordic, 10 days, ~4M samples) with a day-based ANCHORED cross-validation protocol (train on earlier days, test on later ones — avoids look-ahead leakage across the train/test split). The precise smoothed-label formula (compare mean of next-k mid-prices to mean of previous-k mid-prices vs. a threshold) is the convention this dataset popularized; I could not re-derive the exact formula text from the abstract page alone (formula lives in PDF body, not fetched here per "no PDFs" rule) — flagged as UNVERIFIED-exact-formula, cross-referenced qualitatively against DeepLOB paper below which trains/evaluates on this same labeling convention.

### [3] Zhang, Zohren & Roberts (2019) — "DeepLOB: Deep Convolutional Neural Networks for Limit Order Books"
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03668 (accessed 2026-10-04). DOI 10.1109/TSP.2019.2907260 (IEEE Trans. Signal Processing). Authors verified via meta tags: Zhang, Zihao; Zohren, Stefan; Roberts, Stephen.
- Verified abstract (exact): "We develop a large-scale deep learning model to predict price movements from limit order book (LOB) data of cash equities. The architecture utilises convolutional filters to capture the spatial structure of the limit order books as well as LSTM modules to capture longer time dependencies. The proposed network outperforms all existing state-of-the-art algorithms on the benchmark LOB dataset [1]. In a more realistic setting, we test our model by using one year market quotes from the London Stock Exchange and the model delivers a remarkably stable out-of-sample prediction accuracy for a variety of instruments. Importantly, our model translates well to instruments which were not part of the training set, indicating the model's ability to extract universal features. In order to better understand these features ... we perform a sensitivity analysis to understand the rationale behind the model predictions and reveal the components of LOBs that are most relevant."
- Significance: CNN+LSTM directly on raw 10-level LOB snapshots (price/size stacks), trained/evaluated against FI-2010's 3-class horizon-k direction label; also validated out-of-sample on 1 year of LSE quotes across instruments not in training (feature universality). Relevant to us as the raw-book-input baseline that OFI-based models (Kolm et al., entry below) are later benchmarked against.

### [4] Xu, Gould & Howison (2019) — "Multi-Level Order-Flow Imbalance in a Limit Order Book"
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06230 (accessed 2026-10-04). Authors verified: Xu, Ke; Gould, Martin D.; Howison, Sam D. (Oxford).
- Verified abstract (exact): "We study the multi-level order-flow imbalance (MLOFI), which is a vector quantity that measures the net flow of buy and sell orders at different price levels in a limit order book (LOB). Using a recent, high-quality data set for 6 liquid stocks on Nasdaq, we fit a simple, linear relationship between MLOFI and the contemporaneous change in mid-price. For all 6 stocks that we study, we find that the out-of-sample goodness-of-fit of the relationship improves with each additional price level that we include in the MLOFI vector. Our results underline how order-flow activity deep into the LOB can influence the price-formation process."
- Significance: generalizes Cont-Kukanov-Stoikov's single-level (best bid/ask only) OFI to a VECTOR across the top M book levels (MLOFI ∈ R^M, one scalar OFI per level), regressed jointly against mid-price change. Verified finding: including more levels monotonically improves out-of-sample R² on all 6 names tested — direct literature support for our "top-5 levels" feature plan (we should NOT truncate to level-1 OFI only). Exact per-level regression coefficients/R² values are in the PDF body and were not fetched here (abstract-only, no-PDF rule) — flagged UNVERIFIED for specific numbers.
