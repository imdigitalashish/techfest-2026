### [1] Adams & MacKay (2007) — "Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742 (accessed 2026-10-04)
- arXiv ID 0710.3742. Abstract (og:description, verbatim from page): "Changepoints are abrupt variations in the generative parameters of a data sequence. Online detection of changepoints is useful in modelling and prediction of time series in application areas such as finance, biometrics, and robotics. While frequentist methods have yielded online filtering and prediction techniques, most Bayesian papers have focused on the retrospective segmentation problem. Here we examine the case where the model parameters before and after the changepoint are independent and we derive an online algorithm for exact inference of the most recent changepoint. We compute the probability distribution of the length of the current ``run,'' or time since the las[t changepoint, truncated]."
- This is the canonical BOCPD paper: maintains a run-length posterior p(r_t | x_{1:t}) updated online via a hazard function + conditional predictive distributions (UPM, underlying predictive model); exact recursive (non-approximate) online inference.
- Relevance: foundational Bayesian alternative to rolling-KS/ADWIN — instead of a binary alarm, maintains a full posterior over "time since last changepoint," naturally giving a probabilistic regime-shift signal that can gate a LOB forecaster's retrain/adapt logic.
- Gap: page content was truncated at 3000 chars (hit tool limit before reaching algorithmic detail/cost); full method detail (O(t) naive state space growing per step, needs pruning/particle approximations for bounded compute) is widely cited secondary knowledge but NOT itself re-verified on this page — flag as UNVERIFIED cost detail beyond what's quoted.
### [2] Podkopaev & Ramdas (ICLR 2022) — "Tracking the risk of a deployed model and detecting harmful distribution shifts"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06177 (accessed 2026-10-04)
- arXiv ID 2110.06177 (v4). Abstract (verbatim, og:description): "When deployed in the real world, machine learning models inevitably encounter changes in the data distribution, and certain -- but not all -- distribution shifts could result in significant performance degradation. In practice, it may make sense to ignore benign shifts, under which the performance of a deployed model does not degrade substantially, making interventions by a human expert (or model retraining) unnecessary. While several works have developed tests for distribution shifts, these typically either use non-sequential methods, or detect arbitrary shifts (benign or harmful), or both. We argue that a sensible method for firing off a warning has to both (a) detect harmful shifts while ignoring benign ones, and (b) allow continuous monitoring of model performance without increasing the false alarm rate. In this work, we design simple sequential tools for testing if the difference between source (training) and target (test) distributions leads to a significant increase in a risk function of interest, like accuracy or calibration. Recent a[dvances in sequential testing / e-processes implied, truncated]."
- KEY distinction vs. the brief's "rolling KS test" baseline: this is explicitly sequential (anytime-valid, controls false-alarm rate under continuous monitoring, unlike fixed-sample KS) AND harm-aware — it tests for a rise in a *risk* functional (e.g., forecaster loss/accuracy/calibration), not just a generic divergence between feature distributions. Directly matches the "harmful vs benign shift" framing in the thread.
- Relevance to P04: instead of alarming on any LOB feature-distribution change (which is constant at HF and mostly benign/noise), gate retraining on a sequential test of whether the forecaster's *realized loss* has risen — avoids false alarms from benign microstructure noise.
- Gap: truncated before reaching the concrete construction (likely betting-score / e-process based on their other sequential-testing work) — mechanism beyond the abstract is UNVERIFIED here.

### [3] Rabanser, Günnemann & Lipton (NeurIPS 2019) — "Failing Loudly: An Empirical Study of Methods for Detecting Dataset Shift"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11953 (accessed 2026-10-04)
- arXiv ID 1810.11953 (v4). Abstract (verbatim, og:description): "We might hope that when faced with unexpected inputs, well-designed software systems would fire off warnings. Machine learning (ML) systems, however, which depend strongly on properties of their inputs (e.g. the i.i.d. assumption), tend to fail silently. This paper explores the problem of building ML systems that fail loudly, investigating methods for detecting dataset shift, identifying exemplars that most typify the shift, and quantifying shift malignancy. We focus on several datasets and various perturbations to both covariates and label distributions with varying magnitudes and fractions of data affected. Interestingly, we show that across the dataset shifts that we explore, a two-sample-testing-based approach, using pre-trained classifiers for dimensionality reduction, performs best. Moreover, we demonstrate that domain-discriminating approaches tend to be helpful for characterizing shifts qualitatively and determining if they are harmful."
- Best-performing method per abstract: two-sample testing on a *dimensionality-reduced* representation (pre-trained classifier / autoencoder bottleneck) rather than raw-feature KS — directly relevant to "what goes wrong with KS on huge-n data": running many univariate KS tests per raw feature is worse than one multivariate test on a learned low-dim projection.
- Relevance to P04: motivates projecting LOB state (40-100 raw features: price/size at each level) to a small forecaster-relevant embedding before testing, instead of per-feature KS with Bonferroni correction.
- Gap: page truncated before dataset/benchmark details (MNIST/CIFAR-style image shifts, not financial) — cannot confirm applicability specifics to LOB data beyond the general method; no ARL0/false-alarm numbers retrieved.
### [4] River docs — KSWIN (Kolmogorov-Smirnov Windowing)
URL: https://riverml.xyz/latest/api/drift/KSWIN/ (accessed 2026-10-04)
- Verbatim parameters: `alpha` (float, default 0.005, "very sensitive, therefore should be set below 0.01"), `window_size` (int, default 100), `stat_size` (int, default 30), `seed`.
- Mechanism (verbatim from page): maintains sliding window Ψ of size `window_size`; the last `stat_size` samples are the "last concept" R; `stat_size` samples are uniformly drawn from the first n-r samples as approximated-older-concept W. Runs two-sample KS test between R and W. Drift fires when `dist(R,W) > sqrt(-ln(alpha)/r)`.
- Input: explicitly **one-dimensional** per the page ("the detector accepts one dimensional input as array") — confirms that multivariate LOB state needs either per-feature KSWIN + correction, or a scalar projection (e.g., running it on the forecaster's residual/loss rather than raw features).
- Reference cited on page: Christoph Raab, Moritz Heusinger, Frank-Michael Schleif, "Reactive Soft Prototype Computing for Concept Drift Streams," Neurocomputing, 2020 (page truncated after this citation).
- Per-update cost: O(window_size) for the uniform re-sampling of W plus O(stat_size log stat_size) for the KS statistic each update (sort-based ECDF comparison) — this is read off the stated mechanism, not an explicit Big-O stated on the page; treat complexity figure as inferred/UNVERIFIED beyond the described re-sampling+KS steps.

### [6] Shin, Ramdas & Rinaldo (2022) — "E-detectors: a nonparametric framework for sequential change detection"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03532 (accessed 2026-10-04)
- arXiv ID 2203.03532 (v4). Abstract (verbatim, og:description): "Sequential change detection is a classical problem with a variety of applications. However, the majority of prior work has been parametric, for example, focusing on exponential families. We develop a fundamentally new and general framework for sequential change detection when the pre- and post-change distributions are nonparametrically specified (and thus composite). Our procedures come with clean, nonasymptotic bounds on the average run length (frequency of false alarms). In certain nonparametric cases (like sub-Gaussian or sub-exponential), we also provide near-optimal bounds on the detection delay following a changepoint. The primary technical tool that we introduce is called an *e-detector*, which is composed of sums of e-processes -- a fundamental generalization of nonnegative supermartingales -- that are started at consecutive times. We first introduce simple Shiryaev-Roberts and CUSUM-style e-detectors, and then show how to design their mixtures in order to achieve both statistical and computational efficiency. Our e-detector framework can be instantiated t[o recover classical likelihood-ratio CUSUM/Shiryaev-Roberts in the parametric case — truncated]."
- KEY guarantee type: **nonasymptotic bounds on ARL0** (average run length / false-alarm frequency) directly, not an asymptotic approximation — this is the rigorous analogue of "calibrating thresholds" that the thread flags as a fix for KS-test false alarms. Nonparametric (no exponential-family assumption needed), unlike classical CUSUM.
- Mechanism family: "Shiryaev-Roberts- and CUSUM-style e-detectors" + mixtures — i.e., e-detectors generalize CUSUM, replacing the parametric likelihood ratio with an e-process (nonneg. supermartingale under the null), summed over all possible start times.
- Relevance to P04: gives a principled way to set the alarm threshold so that false-alarms-per-day is controlled *by construction* (finite-sample), rather than tuned empirically — answers thread's "calibrating thresholds on a calm reference period" with a provable alternative.
- Per-update cost: maintaining a sum of e-processes "started at consecutive times" implies state growth unless mixtures/pruning are used — paper's own abstract flags this is addressed via "mixtures ... for both statistical and computational efficiency," but the exact per-step complexity number is not in the truncated abstract (UNVERIFIED beyond qualitative claim).

### [7] Vovk, Nouretdinov, Manokhin & Gammerman (2021, MLR v152 / arXiv 2021) — "Retrain or not retrain: Conformal test martingales for change-point detection"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10439 (accessed 2026-10-04); also published at COPA 2021, PMLR v152 (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v152/vovk21b.html, not separately fetched)
- arXiv ID 2102.10439 (v1). Abstract (verbatim): "We argue for supplementing the process of training a prediction algorithm by setting up a scheme for detecting the moment when the distribution of the data changes and the algorithm needs to be retrained. Our proposed schemes are based on exchangeability martingales, i.e., processes that are martingales under any exchangeable distribution for the data. Our method, based on conformal prediction, is general and can be applied on top of any modern prediction algorithm. Its validity is guaranteed, and in this paper we make first steps in exploring its efficiency."
- Mechanism: wrap ANY predictor in conformal prediction → stream of conformal p-values; combine into an "exchangeability martingale" (a betting-style accumulator that is a nonnegative martingale as long as data stays exchangeable/i.i.d.); large martingale value = evidence against exchangeability = regime shift / "retrain now" signal. Model-agnostic wrapper (works on top of the LOB forecaster itself, not just raw features).
- Directly literal match to thread's "conformal test martingales for change detection."
- Gap: abstract doesn't give the concrete betting/martingale construction (e.g., simple-jumper or plug-in martingale) or any numeric efficiency result — those are in the body, not retrieved (page truncated at 3500 chars).

### [8] Bhattacharyya & Ramdas (2026, arXiv preprint) — "Change detection with conformal martingales: new optimal constructions, and suboptimality of existing methods"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27179 (accessed 2026-10-04) — NOTE: arXiv ID prefix 2609 = Sept 2026, i.e. genuinely a ~1-month-old preprint relative to today (2026-10-04); directly answers the thread's ask for "2024-2026 successors."
- arXiv ID 2609.27179 (v1). Abstract (verbatim, truncated at source): "We study distribution-free sequential changepoint detection for independent observations with unknown and unrestricted pre- and post-change laws. We build on the conformal test martingales and associated e-detectors of Vovk (2021), which control the probability of false alarm (PFA) and the average run length (ARL) respectively. The majority of these works focus on validity, with statistical efficiency usually left for simulations. We develop a comprehensive theory of how conformal p-values behave under non-exchangeable data with a changepoint at an unknown time T. We use this to analyze the post-change growth and resulting detection delay of conformal martingale methods, and prove that the standard existing methods are suboptimal for PFA and ARL control, and can lead to delays that are Ω(T) and Ω(√ARL) respectively. We propose different conformal e-processes and e-detectors that are provably minimax optimal, with delays Θ(log T) and [likely Θ(log ARL) — truncated]."
- HEADLINE FINDING (verbatim, high-value for "innovation beyond rolling KS"): standard/existing conformal-martingale change detectors have detection delay that grows **Ω(T)** (linear in time since stream start!) or **Ω(√ARL)** — i.e. naive conformal martingales get *worse*, not just suboptimal, the longer the stream has run without a change. The paper's proposed alternative achieves **Θ(log T)** delay — exponentially better scaling. This is a precise, quantitative, very recent (2026) result directly usable as a scalar-metric innovation claim ("detection delay scaling" ) for the hackathon writeup.
- Directly supersedes citation [7] (Vovk 2021) — exactly the "2024-2026 successor" the thread asked for.

### [9] River docs — ADWIN (Adaptive Windowing)
URL: https://riverml.xyz/latest/api/drift/ADWIN/ (accessed 2026-10-04)
- Verbatim mechanism: "ADWIN efficiently keeps a variable-length window of recent items; such that it holds that there has [not] been change in the data distribution. This window is further divided into two sub-windows (W0, W1)... ADWIN compares the average of W0 and W1 to confirm that they correspond to the same distribution. Concept drift is detected if the distribution equality no longer holds. Upon detecting a drift, W0 is replaced by W1 and a new W1 is initialized." Uses significance value δ (default 0.002) with "mathematical guarantees."
- Verbatim parameters: `delta=0.002` (significance), `clock=32` ("How often ADWIN should check for changes... Higher values speed up processing, but may also lead to increased delay in change detection" — explicit per-update-cost/detection-delay tradeoff knob), `max_buckets=5` (compressed bucket representation from the "ADWIN2" variant — logarithmic-memory window via exponential histogram/bucketing, not a naive O(window) buffer), `min_window_length=5`, `grace_period=10`.
- Per-update cost: with default `clock=32`, full change-test runs only once per 32 updates (amortizes cost); bucket-merging (`max_buckets`) bounds memory/compute to O(log W) in window size W rather than O(W) — read directly off the documented mechanism, consistent with the ADWIN2 "compression algorithm" the page references (exact Big-O not restated numerically on this page — complexity class inferred from the described bucket-merge compression, flag as lightly-inferred not a literal quoted bound).
- Mean/variance tracked online as attributes (`estimation`, `variance`, `total`, `width`) — i.e. ADWIN as shipped in River is a mean-shift detector (tests equality of sub-window **averages**), not a full-distribution test like KSWIN — important nuance for "detector comparison table."

### [12] NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods — §6.3.2.3 CUSUM Control Charts (V-Mask design)
URL: https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc323.htm (accessed 2026-10-04)

## Pitfalls on HF financial data

- **Autocorrelation breaks the KS null.** The classical KS two-sample test assumes i.i.d. draws in each sample; this mechanism (autocorrelation → effective-sample-size collapse → inflated KS statistic → false "significant" shifts even when the underlying distribution hasn't moved) was explained by a web-search summary during this session but **could not be traced to a specific primary paper opened in this session** — reported here as UNVERIFIED-primary-source, flagged per the zero-hallucination rule, not as a confirmed citation.
- **"Big-n p-value collapse."** KS critical values shrink roughly with window size n; LOB streams can produce thousands of updates per second, so even a practically tiny, economically meaningless distributional wobble becomes "statistically significant" quickly. This is standard asymptotic behavior of the KS statistic, not confirmed against a specific opened source this session — flagged as background statistical knowledge, not a citation.
- **Suggested fixes raised in the thread (subsampling, block bootstrap, Bonferroni across features):** searched explicitly this session (`block bootstrap OR subsampling two-sample test dependent time series … arxiv`); the search tool's own summary stated **"there are not direct arXiv links in the top results"** — only secondary blog posts (e.g. numberanalytics.com) and a CRAN package (`blocklength`) surfaced, none of which were opened as primary sources per the "prefer arXiv/docs/READMEs, no PDFs, cite only what you opened" rule. **Conclusion: searched, no high-trust primary source found/opened for block-bootstrap-for-drift-detection specifically — treat as a known remedy from the general statistics literature, not as verified here.**
- **What *was* verified as a concrete calibration fix:** NIST's CUSUM V-Mask design [12] gives a closed-form way to set a detector's threshold from a target false-alarm probability α, miss probability β, and minimum shift size δ to detect — i.e., calibrate on a calm reference period by choosing α to match an acceptable false-alarms-per-day budget, rather than using a generic textbook constant. KSWIN's own docs independently flag the same sensitivity concern (α "very sensitive, should be set below 0.01") [4], consistent with the thread's "calibrate thresholds" fix, though KSWIN's docs do not provide a formula tying α to a false-alarm-per-day budget the way the NIST CUSUM page does.
- **Verified alternative to per-feature KS + Bonferroni:** Rabanser et al. [3] (abstract, verbatim) found that "a two-sample-testing-based approach, using pre-trained classifiers for dimensionality reduction, performs best" across the shifts they studied — i.e., testing one reduced representation beats running many per-feature tests (which is what would otherwise force a Bonferroni correction across, e.g., 40+ raw LOB price/size features).
- **Harmful vs. benign conflation:** per Podkopaev & Ramdas [2] (abstract, verbatim), most distribution-shift tests "either use non-sequential methods, or detect arbitrary shifts (benign or harmful), or both" — i.e. a generic KS alarm cannot by construction distinguish a regime shift that actually degrades the LOB forecaster from one that doesn't; this is the precise gap the "innovation beyond rolling-KS" judging criterion is pointing at.

## Innovation room (beyond rolling-KS-then-retrain)

1. **Conformal test martingale wrapped around the forecaster** (Vovk et al. [7]; optimal construction in Bhattacharyya & Ramdas [8]) instead of a KS test on raw features. *Scalar metric:* detection delay (in ticks since true changepoint) normalized by log(stream length so far) — per [8]'s proved result, naive conformal-martingale delay scales **Ω(T)** while their optimal e-detector achieves **Θ(log T)**; a hackathon demo can plot delay vs. T and show it stays flat instead of growing.

2. **Gate retraining on sequential *risk* tracking, not input drift** (Podkopaev & Ramdas [2]). Track an anytime-valid sequential test on the forecaster's realized loss/calibration rather than on raw order-book feature distributions. *Scalar metric:* harmful-shift recall at fixed false-alarms-per-day — i.e. of all injected regime changes that actually raise forecast loss by >X%, what fraction trigger an alarm, versus what fraction of loss-neutral ("benign") shifts are correctly ignored — directly operationalizes the "harmful vs benign" distinction the baseline KS test cannot make.

3. **Robust (β-divergence) BOCPD instead of vanilla BOCPD** (Knoblauch, Jewson & Damoulas [10]) for outlier-resilient regime posteriors at the same quoted "linear time, constant space" cost as plain BOCPD [1]. *Scalar metric:* false-discovery rate of changepoints under a single-outlier-tick stress test (inject synthetic fat-finger/latency-spike ticks into an otherwise stationary stream and count spurious changepoint flags) — target near-zero vs. a naive detector's inflated FDR.

4. **Provable ARL0 via e-detectors instead of empirically-tuned α** (Shin, Ramdas & Rinaldo [6]). Rather than hand-tuning KSWIN's `alpha` and hoping the realized false-alarm rate matches, construct an e-detector whose nonasymptotic bound *guarantees* a target ARL0 (false alarms per N trading days) by proof, not by backtest luck. *Scalar metric:* achieved-ARL0 ÷ target-ARL0 ratio, measured on held-out calm data — should be ≥ 1 by construction, a claim KSWIN/ADWIN cannot make from their docs alone.

5. **Test one learned scalar/low-dim projection instead of O(features) raw-feature tests** (Rabanser et al. [3]). Project the full LOB state (price+size across levels, both book sides) through a small bottleneck (autoencoder or the forecaster's own hidden state) and run a single sequential test on that, avoiding both the Bonferroni penalty and the "huge-n KS" false-alarm blowup. *Scalar metric:* number of statistical tests executed per update (1 vs. O(number of raw features)), reported alongside TCPDBench-style tolerance-window F1 [5] on an injected-changepoint benchmark, to show detection quality isn't traded away for the efficiency gain.

6. **Evaluate with TCPDBench-style tolerance-window F1 + ARL0 + delay, not a single accuracy number** (van den Burg & Williams [5]). Since even the 5 human annotators in TCPDBench disagree on exact changepoint location, report detection quality as F1 with an explicit ±K-tick tolerance window rather than exact-timestamp accuracy. *Scalar metric:* report a triple (detection delay in ticks, ARL0 in false-alarms/day, F1@±K-ticks) for every candidate detector on the same injected-regime-shift LOB benchmark — a strictly more judge-convincing evaluation protocol than a single bare "accuracy" or "AUC" number.
