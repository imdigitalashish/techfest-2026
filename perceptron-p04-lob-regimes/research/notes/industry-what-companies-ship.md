### 3. Optiver TATC — 1st place solution write-up (hyd / hydantess), confirms ONLINE RETRAINING helped the winner
- **Org**: Kaggle competitor "hyd" (community write-up, hosted on Optiver's official competition site as the designated 1st-place writeup) — **third-party reporting**, not an Optiver statement, but it is the canonical winning solution recognized by the host.
- **URL**: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-trading-at-the-close/writeups/hyd-1st-place-solution — accessed 2026-10-04 (via r.jina.ai). Dated on-page "Oct 14, 2024."
- **What it says (verbatim/exact numbers seen)**: Final model = CatBoost + GRU + Transformer ensemble (weights 0.5/0.3/0.2), 300 shared features. Explicit quote: "Besides, online learning(OL) and post-processing(PP) also play an important role in my final submission." Reported table shows test-set MAE-type metric improving monotonically with more online-learning passes: CatBoost+GRU+Transformer ensemble = 5.4438 (test, w/o OL, w/ PP) → 5.4157 (w/ OL one time) → **5.4030 (w/ OL five times, "*(overtime)")**. Validation strategy: "train on first 400 days and choose last 81 days as my holdout validation set" (temporal holdout, not random split). Retraining cadence stated explicitly: "I retrain my model every 12 days, 5 times in total," with a described engineering trick (save/load training data one file per day) to manage memory during incremental retraining with GBDT.
- **Relevance**: **Direct, numeric evidence that periodic online/incremental retraining (not a static frozen model) measurably improved held-out performance in Optiver's own competition** — this is the single best citation for justifying an adaptive/regime-aware retraining component in our solution, with exact before/after numbers from the actual #1 solution.
- **Where we borrow**: (a) temporal train/holdout split design (first N days train, last M days holdout — matches "no look-ahead" judge concern); (b) periodic retrain cadence as a concrete, citable precedent for "detects shift and adapts" instead of a single static model; (c) ensemble diversity (tree + sequence + cross-sectional transformer) as a feature-engineering reference point, not to copy directly but to benchmark complexity.
- **Where we can beat it**: This solution retrains on a fixed calendar cadence (every 12 days) rather than retraining conditional on a detected distribution-shift signal — our pitch (explicit shift-detection trigger rather than blind periodic retrain) is a defensible differentiation judges can be shown directly against this public precedent.

### 4. Optiver RVP — 2nd/1st-ranked public solution write-up (nyanp), time-series CV + covariate-shift detection methodology
- **Org**: Kaggle competitor "nyanp" (community write-up on Optiver's official competition site) — **third-party reporting**.
- **URL**: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-realized-volatility-prediction/writeups/nyanp-1st-place-solution-nearest-neighbors — accessed 2026-10-04 (via r.jina.ai). Note page title says "1st place" but body text says "I don't know where I rank on the final leaderboard... my public 2nd place solution" — **flagging this discrepancy explicitly**: UNVERIFIED whether this was final 1st or 2nd place; treat as a top public solution regardless.
- **What it says (verbatim)**: Reverse-engineered the anonymized `time_id` order using tick-size price reconstruction + t-SNE compression, then validated against real yfinance prices to confirm "the training data is for the period between 2020/1/1~2021/3/31." Used this recovered order for: "**Time series cross-validation**. Now that we know the correct order of the timestamps, we can construct the validation sets as if they were normal time-series data" (explicit 4-fold time-series CV, 10% held out per fold); "**Detection of covariate shifts**. Using methods such as adversarial validation enables us to find features that change over time"; "**Data exclusion** ... we can exclude periods of specific market events from our data, for example, the stock market crash in early 2020" (not used by author, but named as a live technique others considered).
- **Relevance**: Independent, primary-source-adjacent confirmation that a top Optiver-competition solution (a) treated the look-ahead problem as serious enough to reverse-engineer true chronological order even though Optiver had anonymized it, and (b) explicitly names **adversarial validation as a covariate-/distribution-shift detector** and **event-based data exclusion** as live mitigation strategies for regime shift — both directly reusable in our shift-detection module design.
- **Where we borrow**: Adversarial-validation-style shift detection (train a classifier to separate "early" vs "late" windows; high separability flags regime drift) as a concrete, named technique a top Optiver-competition solver actually used.

### 7. Two Sigma Street View — "A Machine Learning Approach to Regime Modeling"
- **Org**: Two Sigma (official company blog / "Street View" research publication) — **primary source**.
- **URL**: https://www.twosigma.com/articles/a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling/ — accessed 2026-10-04 (direct fetch, two pages: start_index=0 and start_index=6000)
- **What it says (verbatim)**: Uses a **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)** — "a type of unsupervised learning method" — fit on the joint distribution of 17 factors from the "Two Sigma Factor Lens" (factor history "dating back to the early 1970s") to produce **four data-driven market conditions**, each "characterized by a 17-dimensional Gaussian distribution" with its own means, volatilities, and correlation structure. Named/labeled post-hoc by the authors as: **"Market Condition 1: Crisis"** (negative Equity & Credit, worst Emerging Markets, positive Interest Rates/"flight to quality", highest average absolute factor correlation); **"Market Condition 2: Steady State"** (normal/healthy, no large drawdowns); **"Market Condition 3: Inflation"** (double-digit mean return for the Local Inflation factor, weak Equity/Rates, strongest Foreign Currency factor move); **"Market Condition 4: Walking on Ice (WOI)"** ("tends to occur around Crisis (and Steady State) periods, potentially indicating market fragility" — equity still positive but "factor volatilities were on average 1.6 percentage points higher... than their respective long-term averages," e.g., Value 18.2% vol in WOI vs 8.9% long-term, Momentum 19.1% vs 10.5%, Low Risk 20% vs 10.4%). States explicitly: "For any given historical period, the GMM will estimate probabilities that the market was in the four market conditions" (soft/probabilistic regime membership, not hard labels).
- **Relevance**: This is the clearest **primary-source production example of unsupervised regime detection via GMM with soft (probabilistic) regime membership** — directly maps to a "regime shift detector" component design: fit a mixture model on rolling factor/feature statistics, output a probability vector over K regimes per period, and flag a "shift" when the arg-max regime changes or entropy spikes.
- **Where we borrow**: (a) GMM-over-engineered-features (not raw prices) as the regime-detection backbone; (b) soft/probabilistic regime assignment instead of hard clustering, explicitly stated as `each market condition will have a probability... and the four probabilities will sum to 100%`; (c) the idea of *labeling* unsupervised clusters post-hoc using domain-informed factor behavior (Crisis/Steady-State/Inflation/WOI) as a template for presenting our own LOB-feature-space regimes to judges in interpretable terms.
