## TL;DR (what practitioners believe works / doesn't)

**What practitioners believe works:**
- Fusing LLM/sentiment signals **with** historical price/fundamentals data, not relying on text alone (HN lead #6: "vibe investing" critique explicitly calls for mixing LLM output with "another program that does some fundamentals analysis based on historical data" — this is exactly the hackathon brief's fusion approach).
- Ad-hoc bootstrap/ensemble confidence intervals (bag trees, spread of predictions) are a common practitioner fallback when full/transductive conformal prediction is too computationally expensive (lead #1).
- Dictionary-based sentiment (Loughran-McDonald) over transformer models (FinBERT) when corpus size makes transformer inference too slow for commodity hardware — a real throughput/cost tradeoff a hackathon team should budget for (lead #8).
- Conformal prediction is the practitioner- and academic-endorsed route to distribution-free, finite-sample, model-agnostic uncertainty intervals in finance (GitHub lead #9, ml4trading.io lead #15) — but **every credible source caveats this on the exchangeability assumption holding**.

**What practitioners believe doesn't work / is unresolved:**
- News-sentiment trading signals are suspected of simply lagging price (reverse causality) rather than leading it — raised and left unrebutted by the bot's own author (lead #3).
- Publicly shared/open-sourced trading signals are believed to decay in value ("crowding") once exposed (lead #4) — relevant if the hackathon pitch includes "share this signal" framing.
- LLM-only stock prediction without grounding in real analysis is viewed skeptically as unverifiable and adversarially gameable — HN commenters explicitly flagged that an LLM signal generator could be "weaponised" by someone gaming it adversarially, and the builder himself conceded he could "lie about answers" with no way to make it reproducible (lead #5) — this is the single most on-point practitioner skepticism for a "manipulated text" robustness pitch.
- Conformal prediction's core finite-sample coverage guarantee is explicitly conditional on **exchangeability** — multiple independent sources (2017 HN thread lead #2, and the 2025/2026-era ml4trading.io primer lead #15) converge on this exact point: the guarantee is "only as good as the exchangeability you are willing to believe." Manipulated/adversarial text is precisely the kind of distribution shift that can break this assumption, so a pitch claiming conformal bounds "robust to manipulated text" must explicitly address how it detects/handles non-exchangeable (manipulated) inputs rather than assuming vanilla conformal calibration suffices.

## Skeptical takes our pitch must answer

1. **"Isn't this just reverse causality / following price with a lag?"** (HN, beforeolives, 2021-04-18, lead #3) — must show the text signal has information not already in price (e.g., via lead-lag / Granger-style check or an ablation showing price-only baseline underperforms fusion).
2. **"Couldn't someone game this adversarially?"** (HN, ggm, 2025-04-13, lead #5) — this is literally the hackathon's "manipulated text" premise; the pitch must name concrete manipulation vectors (fake headlines, coordinated bot sentiment, prompt-injected text) and show the conformal bound widens / signal is down-weighted under detected manipulation, not just under ordinary noise.
3. **"LLM output alone is 'vibe investing' — where's the grounding?"** (HN, ungreased0675, 2025-04-13, lead #6) — answered by the fusion-with-price-timeseries design itself; make this explicit in the pitch rather than assumed.
4. **"Was this backtested at all?"** (HN, ben_w, 2025-04-13, lead #7) — a demo without an honest historical backtest (with realistic train/calibration/test splits respecting time order) will draw the same immediate question that sank the Ftocks Show HN.
5. **"Does your conformal interval actually hold when the calibration assumption (exchangeability) is violated?"** (synthesized from HN lead #2 + ml4trading.io lead #15) — the pitch needs a specific answer: e.g., detect distribution shift/manipulation and either (a) explicitly flag coverage as untrustworthy, or (b) use a conformal variant designed for non-exchangeable/drifting data (adaptive conformal inference), rather than silently presenting a nominal-coverage interval that no longer holds.
6. **"Does FinBERT/LLM sentiment extraction even scale to your data volume?"** (HN, jgfriedman1999, 2025-01-07, lead #8) — be ready to state compute budget/latency, since a practitioner independently found full-corpus FinBERT inference impractical on a laptop.
